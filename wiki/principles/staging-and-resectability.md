# Staging & resectability — how to think about it

> **Principle node.** A *reasoning lens*: "before any systemic plan, what is the
> stage, and is this disease potentially curable with local therapy?"

## How to think about staging

The first fork in most solid-tumor care is **stage**, because stage determines
**intent**: curative (localized/locally advanced) vs palliative/disease-control
(metastatic). Choosing systemic therapy before resolving stage and resectability
risks treating a curable patient as incurable, or vice versa.

Work through:

1. **Stage accurately and completely.**
   - Use the appropriate **TNM / disease-specific staging** with adequate imaging
     and, where it changes management, pathologic nodal staging (e.g. invasive
     mediastinal staging in lung; for many tumors, biopsy of equivocal sites).
   - A staging shortcut that misclassifies nodal or metastatic status changes the
     entire plan.

2. **Ask the resectability / curative-intent question explicitly.**
   - Is the disease **resectable** (or amenable to definitive local therapy —
     surgery, definitive radiation, ablation)?
   - Resectability is both **anatomic** (can it be removed with negative margins)
     and **physiologic** (can the patient tolerate the operation — see
     [[tolerability-and-comorbidity]]).
   - "Potentially resectable / borderline" disease is a distinct category where
     neoadjuvant therapy may convert to resectable — flag it rather than defaulting
     to palliative systemic therapy.

3. **Sequence local and systemic therapy deliberately.**
   - Neoadjuvant vs adjuvant vs definitive-systemic decisions follow from stage and
     resectability, and increasingly from [[biomarker-testing|biomarkers]].

## When an entity links here, ask

- What is the stage, and is the staging workup adequate to trust it?
- Is this curative-intent or palliative-intent disease?
- Is it resectable — anatomically *and* physiologically?
- If borderline, is conversion (neoadjuvant) therapy on the table?
