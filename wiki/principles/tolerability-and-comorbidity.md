# Tolerability & comorbidity — how to think about it

> **Principle node.** A *reasoning lens*: "can *this* patient tolerate the
> treatment, and how do their comorbidities reshape the menu?"

## How to think about tolerability

Identifying comorbidities is essential to determine **what type of treatment the
patient can tolerate**. The trial told you what works in a fit, selected
population; the clinic asks whether *your* patient can receive it safely. The same
diagnosis yields very different plans for a fit 60-year-old and a frail
80-year-old with organ dysfunction.

Work through:

1. **Establish fitness, not just age.**
   - **Performance status** (ECOG/KPS) is the single most useful summary of whether
     a patient can tolerate intensive therapy. Chronologic age alone should not
     decide.
   - Consider geriatric assessment domains (function, cognition, nutrition,
     polypharmacy, social support) for older or borderline patients.

2. **Organ reserve sets hard constraints.**
   - Renal, hepatic, cardiac, pulmonary, and marrow function gate eligibility and
     dosing. Map each candidate regimen's [[adverse-events|toxicities]] onto the
     organ systems already compromised.

3. **Goals of care and burden.**
   - Tolerability includes the *burden* of treatment (visits, infusion time,
     monitoring, financial toxicity) measured against the patient's goals and the
     [[efficacy|magnitude of expected benefit]].
   - A marginal survival gain may not be worth a toxic, demanding regimen in a
     frail patient; a durable benefit may justify dose-attenuation rather than
     omission.

4. **De-escalate intelligently.**
   - When full-intensity therapy is not tolerable, the question is rarely
     treat-vs-nothing — it is which **attenuated or alternative** regimen preserves
     benefit at acceptable cost.

## When an entity links here, ask

- What is the patient's performance status and organ reserve?
- Which comorbidities collide with this regimen's toxicities?
- Does the expected benefit justify the burden for *this* patient's goals?
- Is there a tolerable de-escalated alternative if full intensity is unsafe?
