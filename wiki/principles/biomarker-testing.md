# Biomarker testing — how to think about it

> **Principle node.** A *reasoning lens*: for a cancer, drug, or trial linking
> here, "what must I know about this tumor's molecular/IHC profile before I can
> choose therapy?"

## How to think about biomarker testing

Modern systemic therapy is **biomarker-gated**: the right drug often cannot be
chosen until the tumor is characterized. Testing is not an academic exercise — it
**identifies the subset of patients for whom a given therapy is appropriate** (see
[[efficacy]], where the benefit is frequently concentrated in a biomarker-defined
subgroup).

Work through:

1. **What testing does this disease require, and when?**
   - Broad **NGS** (DNA ± RNA fusion) to find actionable driver alterations
     (e.g. EGFR, ALK, ROS1, KRAS, BRAF in lung; MSI/MMR, HER2 across tumors).
   - **IHC** for protein expression (e.g. PD-L1, HER2, hormone receptors).
   - Consider **timing**: test at diagnosis of advanced disease so results are
     ready before therapy starts; avoid starting a default regimen that a pending
     result would have changed.

2. **Does the result actually gate a decision?**
   - A biomarker matters when it *opens or closes* a therapy: a targetable driver
     that mandates a TKI over chemo-immunotherapy; a PD-L1 level that shifts the
     IO regimen; MSI-high that unlocks immunotherapy.
   - Distinguish **predictive** markers (predict benefit from a specific drug)
     from **prognostic** markers (predict outcome regardless of therapy).

3. **Adequacy and method.**
   - Is there enough tissue? Is liquid biopsy (ctDNA) an acceptable or
     complementary route? A negative result on an inadequate sample is not a true
     negative.

## When an entity links here, ask

- Which biomarkers must be resolved before choosing therapy for this disease?
- Is the marker **predictive** (drug selection) or merely prognostic?
- What is the right assay and timing, and is the sample adequate?
- If the marker is positive/negative, which therapy does it open or close?
