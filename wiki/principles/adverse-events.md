# Adverse events — how to think about toxicity

> **Principle node.** A *reasoning lens* for any drug or trial that links here:
> "what harms does this treatment cause, and how does that harm profile change who
> I would give it to?"

## How to think about adverse events

Treatment is almost always associated with adverse events (AEs) documented in the
**original trial** and codified in the **FDA label**. The side-effect profile is
not just a safety footnote — it is a **treatment-selection input**. The same
efficacy can be the right or wrong choice for two patients depending on how their
comorbidities intersect with the drug's toxicities.

Work through:

1. **What are the characteristic and the serious AEs?**
   - Distinguish *common but manageable* (fatigue, rash, low-grade GI) from
     *uncommon but dangerous* (pneumonitis, myocarditis, severe cytopenias,
     hepatotoxicity).
   - Note grade (CTCAE): a grade 1–2 event is a nuisance; grade 3–4 changes care.
   - Note frequency and reversibility, and whether AEs are class effects
     (e.g. immune-related AEs for checkpoint inhibitors, ILD for some ADCs/TKIs).

2. **Map the toxicity onto the patient's comorbidities.**
   - The decisive question is *interaction*: a cardiotoxic regimen in a patient
     with reduced ejection fraction; a pneumonitis-prone drug in fibrotic lung;
     a nephrotoxic agent in CKD; an immunotherapy in active autoimmune disease.
   - A comorbidity can move a drug from first choice to contraindicated even when
     its [[efficacy]] is best-in-class.

3. **Dose, monitoring, and management.**
   - Many AEs are anticipatable and mitigable (premedication, monitoring schedule,
     dose modification, supportive care). Ask whether the patient can adhere to
     the monitoring the drug requires.

## Good sources for side-effect data

- The **registrational trial** publication (AE tables, grade distribution,
  discontinuation rates) — the primary signal.
- The **FDA label / package insert** (Boxed Warnings, Warnings & Precautions,
  Adverse Reactions) and the EMA SmPC.
- NCCN/ASCO/ESMO **toxicity-management guidelines** (e.g. immune-related AE
  management) for how to act on events.
- Pharmacovigilance / post-marketing signals for rare events not seen in trials.

## When a drug or trial links here, ask

- What are the dose-limiting and serious AEs, and at what grade/frequency?
- Which of this patient's comorbidities does the toxicity profile collide with?
- Is the regimen's monitoring/management feasible for this patient?
- Does the toxicity change my preference relative to alternatives of similar
  [[efficacy]]?
