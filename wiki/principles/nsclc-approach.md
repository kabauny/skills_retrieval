# Approach to NSCLC — how to think about it

> **Principle node (disease framework).** This is the worked example of a
> per-disease reasoning scaffold: "when I encounter non-small cell lung cancer,
> what is the order of operations before I choose therapy?" It does not list
> regimens — it encodes *how to approach* the disease and points to the lenses.

## How to approach NSCLC

For NSCLC the most important early moves are **staging to identify resectability**
and **NGS (broad molecular profiling) to identify whether targeted or
immune-based systemic therapy is appropriate**. Identifying **comorbidities** then
determines what the patient can actually tolerate. In order:

1. **Stage and ask the resectability question first.**
   - Establish TNM stage with adequate imaging and **invasive mediastinal nodal
     staging** where it changes management. See [[staging-and-resectability]].
   - Localized/locally advanced disease is potentially curable — do not default to
     palliative systemic therapy before resolving resectability and curative-intent
     options (surgery, definitive chemoradiation, neoadjuvant strategies).

2. **Get molecular profiling before committing to systemic therapy.**
   - Broad **NGS** for actionable drivers (EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, KRAS G12C, MET,
     RET, NTRK, HER2) plus **PD-L1 IHC**. See [[biomarker-testing]].
   - In advanced non-squamous disease especially, a targetable driver mandates a
     TKI over chemo-immunotherapy — starting the wrong default regimen while NGS is
     pending can compromise care. Wait for results when feasible.

3. **Assess fitness and comorbidities to set the tolerable menu.**
   - Performance status, pulmonary reserve, cardiac and other comorbidities
     determine eligibility for surgery, the intensity of chemoradiation, and the
     choice among systemic regimens. See [[tolerability-and-comorbidity]].

4. **Then choose therapy, weighing benefit against harm.**
   - Compare candidate regimens on [[efficacy]] (which subset benefits, by how
     much, on which endpoint) against [[adverse-events]] (which toxicities collide
     with this patient's comorbidities).

## When NSCLC comes up, ask in order

1. What is the **stage**, and is it **resectable** (anatomically and physiologically)?
2. What does **NGS + PD-L1** show — is there a driver that changes first-line choice?
3. What are the **comorbidities / performance status**, and what do they rule out?
4. Among tolerable options, which has the best **efficacy-for-this-subset vs
   toxicity** balance?
