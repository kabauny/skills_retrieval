# Follicular lymphoma (FL)

## Overview
Follicular lymphoma is the most common indolent B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma, accounting for roughly 20-30% of all NHL, with a median survival now approaching two decades. It typically follows a chronic, relapsing-remitting course, and asymptomatic low-burden disease is often managed with watchful waiting. The defining molecular lesion is the t(14;18) translocation driving BCL2 overexpression; prognosis is dominated by histologic grade, progression within 24 months (POD24), and risk of histologic transformation to aggressive lymphoma.

## Key facts
- Epidemiology and subtypes: FL is the most common indolent NHL (~20-30% of lymphomas); WHO grading by centroblasts per high-power field (grade 1: 0-5, grade 2: 6-15, grade 3: >15), with grade 3B being biologically distinct (frequent loss of t(14;18)/CD10) and treated like aggressive lymphoma [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK538206/).
- Hallmark biomarker: ~85-90% of FL carries the t(14;18)(q32;q21) translocation placing BCL2 under the IGH promoter, causing BCL2 overexpression; BCL2 positivity is highest in grade 1-2 disease [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK538206/).
- Risk stratification and intent: the FLIPI index (age >60, Hb <12 g/dL, elevated LDH, Ann Arbor stage III/IV, >4 nodal areas) stratifies prognosis; FL is generally incurable but indolent, so therapy intent is disease control rather than cure, ranging from observation in low-burden disease to immunochemotherapy for symptomatic/high-burden disease [Diagnosis and Management Update](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ajh.26737).
- POD24 prognostic gate: progression of disease within 24 months of first-line therapy occurs in ~15-20% of patients and identifies a high-risk group with ~50% 5-year OS versus >90% for non-POD24 patients, marking candidates for intensified or novel salvage approaches [LEO Consortium analysis](https://ashpublications.org/bloodadvances/article/9/5/1013/526312/Treatment-patterns-and-outcomes-in-follicular).
- First-line landscape: standard frontline options for symptomatic/high-burden disease are anti-CD20 (rituximab/obinutuzumab) combined with chemotherapy (bendamustine or CHOP), followed by maintenance anti-CD20; localized stage I-II disease may be treated with involved-site radiotherapy with curative intent [NCCN Guideline (FL)](https://www2.tri-kobe.org/nccn/guideline/hematologic/nhl/english/foll.pdf).
- Relapsed/refractory bispecific therapy: mosunetuzumab (Lunsumio), a CD20xCD3 bispecific, is FDA-approved after ≥2 prior therapies based on the GO29781 phase 2 study, with ORR ~80% and CR ~60% by independent review and durable responses (3-year follow-up median DOR ~35.9 months) [GO29781 3-year follow-up](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/145/7/708/525779/Long-term-3-year-follow-up-of-mosunetuzumab-in).
- Biomarker-gated targeted therapy: the EZH2 inhibitor tazemetostat received FDA accelerated approval (2020) for R/R FL, with EZH2-mutant tumors showing ORR 69% versus 34% in EZH2 wild-type, illustrating how an FDA-approved companion test gates therapy selection [FDA tazemetostat approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/fda-granted-accelerated-approval-tazemetostat-follicular-lymphoma).
- Histologic transformation: a minority of patients progress to aggressive (often diffuse large B-cell) lymphoma, which carries a worse prognosis and shifts management toward aggressive-lymphoma regimens and consolidation [Diagnosis and Management Update](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ajh.26737).
