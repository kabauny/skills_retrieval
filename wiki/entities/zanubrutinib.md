# Zanubrutinib

## Overview

Zanubrutinib (Brukinsa) is a next-generation, covalent Bruton's tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor designed for greater selectivity and BTK occupancy than first-generation ibrutinib. It is approved across multiple B-cell malignancies, including chronic lymphocytic leukemia/small lymphocytic lymphoma (CLL/SLL), mantle cell lymphoma, Waldenström macroglobulinemia, marginal zone lymphoma, and follicular lymphoma. In head-to-head trials it has demonstrated superior progression-free survival and a more favorable cardiac safety profile than ibrutinib.

## Key facts

- Mechanism: forms a covalent bond with a cysteine residue in the BTK active site, irreversibly inhibiting BTK signaling downstream of the B-cell receptor that drives malignant B-cell proliferation, trafficking, and adhesion [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/213217s015lbl.pdf).
- FDA indications span five B-cell malignancies: CLL/SLL, relapsed/refractory mantle cell lymphoma, Waldenström macroglobulinemia, relapsed/refractory marginal zone lymphoma, and (with obinutuzumab) relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma — the first BTK inhibitor approved in FL [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/beigene-secures-fifth-approval-for-brukinsa-expands-into-follicular-lymphoma).
- Pivotal ALPINE trial (R/R CLL/SLL): zanubrutinib was superior to ibrutinib for PFS, median 50.2 vs 28.0 months (HR 0.56; 95% CI 0.36–0.88; P=0.0110) [JCO interim analysis](https://ascopubs.org/doi/abs/10.1200/JCO.22.00510).
- Pivotal SEQUOIA trial (treatment-naive CLL/SLL): vs bendamustine-rituximab, zanubrutinib reduced progression/death by 72% (HR 0.28; 95% CI 0.20–0.38; P<.0001), with 72-month PFS 74% vs 32% [SEQUOIA 5-year JCO](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO-24-02265).
- In high-risk del(17p) CLL, zanubrutinib achieved a 5-year PFS of ~72% and OS of ~85% [SEQUOIA 5-year data](https://www.ajmc.com/view/zanubrutinib-shows-durable-benefit-for-high-risk-cll-sll-at-5-years-in-sequoia-trial).
- Notable adverse events: hemorrhage (≥30%; Grade ≥3 in 3.8%), serious/opportunistic infections, cytopenias (decreased neutrophils 51%, platelets 41%), and atrial fibrillation/flutter (4.4%, lower than ibrutinib), plus second primary malignancies [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/213217s015lbl.pdf).
- The lower incidence of cardiac events, especially atrial fibrillation, versus ibrutinib was a key differentiator in the ALPINE comparison [NEJM ALPINE](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2211582).
