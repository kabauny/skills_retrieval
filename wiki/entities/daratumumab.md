# Daratumumab

## Overview
Daratumumab (Darzalex) is a human IgG1κ monoclonal antibody targeting CD38, a glycoprotein highly expressed on multiple myeloma cells. It was the first anti-CD38 antibody approved for multiple myeloma and is now a backbone agent across both newly diagnosed and relapsed/refractory settings, used in combination with immunomodulatory drugs and proteasome inhibitors as well as in monotherapy.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: binds CD38 on myeloma cells and triggers tumor cell death via complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC), antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP), apoptosis, and immunomodulation by depleting CD38-positive regulatory cells [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/761036s054lbl.pdf).
- Principal FDA-approved indications: multiple myeloma, including newly diagnosed disease (transplant-eligible and -ineligible) in combination regimens and relapsed/refractory disease, as well as monotherapy in heavily pretreated patients [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/761036s054lbl.pdf).
- MAIA (newly diagnosed, transplant-ineligible): daratumumab + lenalidomide/dexamethasone (D-Rd) vs Rd improved OS (HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.53–0.86, p=0.0013) and PFS (median NR vs 34.4 mo, HR 0.53) [MAIA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34655533/).
- POLLUX (relapsed/refractory): D-Rd vs Rd gave final median OS 67.6 vs 51.8 months (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.58–0.91, p=0.0044) with early PFS HR 0.37 [POLLUX](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.22.00940).
- CASTOR (relapsed/refractory): daratumumab + bortezomib/dexamethasone (D-Vd) vs Vd improved OS at 72.6-month follow-up (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.59–0.92, p=0.0075) [CASTOR](https://ashpublications.org/bloodadvances/article/8/2/388/506492/Daratumumab-for-patients-with-myeloma-with-early).
- Notable/serious adverse events: infusion-related reactions (bronchospasm, hypoxia, hypertension), neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anemia, lymphopenia (increased infection risk), and upper respiratory infections [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/761036s054lbl.pdf).
- Lab interference: binds CD38 on red blood cells, causing a positive indirect antiglobulin (Coombs) test that can persist up to ~6 months and interferes with blood cross-matching [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/761036s054lbl.pdf).
