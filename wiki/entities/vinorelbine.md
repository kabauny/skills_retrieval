# Vinorelbine

## Overview
Vinorelbine (Navelbine) is a semisynthetic vinca alkaloid antimitotic agent given intravenously. It is FDA-approved for advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), either as a single agent or in combination with cisplatin, and is also widely used off-label in breast cancer. Its dose-limiting toxicity is granulocytopenia, and severe myelosuppression can lead to fatal infection.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: vinorelbine binds tubulin and inhibits microtubule assembly, blocking mitotic spindle formation and arresting dividing cells in metaphase, with relatively greater selectivity for mitotic over axonal microtubules than older vinca alkaloids [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2002/20388s014lbl.pdf).
- FDA-approved indications: single-agent treatment of metastatic NSCLC, and combination with cisplatin for first-line locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2002/20388s014lbl.pdf).
- Pivotal trial: the European multicenter randomized trial (Le Chevalier, JCO 1994; n=612) showed vinorelbine+cisplatin superior to vindesine+cisplatin and to vinorelbine alone, with response rate 30% vs 19% vs 14% [JCO 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8113844/).
- In that trial, median overall survival was longest with vinorelbine+cisplatin at 40 weeks vs 32 weeks (vindesine+cisplatin) and 31 weeks (vinorelbine alone); superiority confirmed after 6 years follow-up (p < 0.02) [Oncologist long-term analysis](https://academic.oup.com/oncolo/article/6/S1/8/6387673).
- Adjuvant role: in JBR.10, vinorelbine+cisplatin after complete resection of stage IB/II NSCLC improved overall survival vs observation (HR 0.78, 95% CI 0.61-0.99; P=.04), with 5-year survival 67% vs 56%, benefit concentrated in stage II/node-positive disease [JBR.10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2799232/).
- Dose-limiting toxicity is granulocytopenia; granulocyte nadir occurs 7-10 days after dosing with recovery in 7-14 days, and severe myelosuppression can cause serious infection, septic shock, and death [FDA safety communication](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-updates-vinca-alkaloid-labeling-preparation-intravenous-infusion-bags-only).
- Other notable adverse events include injection-site reactions and severe vesicant extravasation injury, peripheral neuropathy, constipation/ileus, and fatigue; vinorelbine must be prepared in intravenous infusion bags (never syringes) to prevent fatal accidental intrathecal administration [FDA safety communication](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-updates-vinca-alkaloid-labeling-preparation-intravenous-infusion-bags-only).
