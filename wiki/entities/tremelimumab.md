# Tremelimumab

## Overview
Tremelimumab (Imjudo) is a fully human IgG2 monoclonal antibody that blocks cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated antigen 4 (CTLA-4), releasing inhibition of T-cell priming and activation. It is approved in combination with the anti-PD-L1 antibody durvalumab for unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (the "STRIDE" regimen) and, with durvalumab plus platinum chemotherapy, for metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC).

## Key facts
- Mechanism: anti-CTLA-4 monoclonal antibody that blocks CTLA-4 binding to CD80/CD86, enhancing T-cell activation and proliferation; used to augment anti-PD-L1 (durvalumab) immunotherapy [FDA approval summary](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37676259/).
- FDA approved Oct 21, 2022 with durvalumab for unresectable HCC, delivered as the STRIDE regimen: a single 300 mg priming dose of tremelimumab plus durvalumab 1500 mg, then durvalumab every 4 weeks [Pharmacy Times](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-approves-tremelimumab-durvalumab-combination-for-unresectable-liver-cancer).
- Pivotal HCC trial HIMALAYA (phase 3): STRIDE vs sorafenib gave median OS 16.4 vs 13.8 mo, OS HR 0.78 (95% CI 0.67-0.92; P=0.0035) [HIMALAYA, NEJM Evidence](https://evidence.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/EVIDoa2100070).
- HIMALAYA 5-year update confirmed a durable benefit, with roughly 1 in 5 STRIDE patients alive at 5 years versus far fewer on sorafenib [ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/issues/october-10-2024/himalaya-5-year-analysis-confirms-survival-benefit-of-stride-regimen-in-hepatocellular-carcinoma/).
- FDA approved Nov 10, 2022 with durvalumab + platinum chemotherapy for metastatic NSCLC based on POSEIDON [FDA D.I.S.C.O.](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-disco-burst-edition-fda-approval-imjudo-tremelimumab-combination-imfinzi-durvalumab-and-platinum).
- Pivotal NSCLC trial POSEIDON (phase 3): tremelimumab + durvalumab + chemo vs chemo gave OS HR 0.77 (95% CI 0.65-0.92; P=0.00304); 5-year OS 15.7% vs 6.8% [POSEIDON, J Thorac Oncol](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(24)02264-0/fulltext).
- Common adverse reactions (≥20%) include rash, fatigue, diarrhea, pruritus, musculoskeletal pain and abdominal pain; serious immune-mediated toxicities (colitis, hepatitis, pneumonitis, endocrinopathies) are class effects of CTLA-4 blockade [FDA approval summary](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37676259/).
