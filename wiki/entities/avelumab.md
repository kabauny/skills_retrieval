# Avelumab

## Overview
Avelumab (Bavencio) is a fully human IgG1 monoclonal antibody against PD-L1 that blocks PD-L1/PD-1 (and PD-L1/B7.1) interaction to restore antitumor T-cell immunity; its intact Fc region also retains potential for antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC). It is FDA-approved across metastatic Merkel cell carcinoma, urothelial carcinoma (first-line maintenance and post-platinum), and—with axitinib—advanced renal cell carcinoma. It was the first agent FDA-approved for metastatic Merkel cell carcinoma.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: human IgG1 anti–PD-L1 monoclonal antibody that competitively blocks PD-L1 binding to PD-1 and B7.1, releasing inhibition of cytotoxic T cells; the preserved IgG1 Fc may also engage NK-cell ADCC [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2020/761049s009lbl.pdf).
- Indications: metastatic Merkel cell carcinoma (age ≥12), first-line maintenance of locally advanced/metastatic urothelial carcinoma not progressing on platinum chemotherapy, post-platinum metastatic urothelial carcinoma, and (with axitinib) first-line advanced renal cell carcinoma [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2020/761049s009lbl.pdf).
- JAVELIN Bladder 100 (urothelial maintenance, phase 3): avelumab + best supportive care vs BSC alone after first-line platinum gave median OS 21.4 vs 14.3 mo (HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.56–0.86; P=0.001), establishing maintenance as a standard of care [JAVELIN Bladder 100](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2002788).
- JAVELIN Merkel 200 (phase 2, previously treated metastatic MCC): objective response rate 33% (95% CI 23.3–43.8) with 11.4% complete responses; durable responses supported the first FDA approval in this disease [JAVELIN Merkel 200](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7239697/).
- JAVELIN Renal 101 (phase 3, first-line advanced RCC): avelumab + axitinib vs sunitinib gave median PFS 13.8 vs 8.4 mo in the overall population (HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.56–0.84; P<0.001) [JAVELIN Renal 101](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1816047).
- Adverse events: infusion-related reactions are common (premedication recommended); immune-mediated toxicities include pneumonitis, hepatitis, colitis, endocrinopathies (thyroid, adrenal, type 1 diabetes), nephritis, and dermatologic reactions [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2020/761049s009lbl.pdf).
- In combination with axitinib, common reactions (≥20%) include fatigue, infusion-related reaction, musculoskeletal pain, nausea, decreased appetite, and urinary tract infection, with grade ≥3 events in roughly 80% of patients [JAVELIN Renal 101 final analysis](https://www.annalsofoncology.org/article/S0923-7534(24)04987-1/fulltext).
