# Cervical cancer

## Overview
Cervical cancer is the fourth most common cancer in women worldwide and is overwhelmingly driven by persistent high-risk HPV infection (especially types 16 and 18), making it largely preventable through vaccination and screening. Squamous cell carcinoma dominates histology; adenocarcinoma is the main non-squamous subtype and carries worse outcomes. FIGO stage sets treatment intent — early-stage disease is managed with surgery or chemoradiation, while locally advanced and metastatic disease increasingly incorporate PD-(L)1 immunotherapy guided by PD-L1 CPS.

## Key facts
- HPV (mainly types 16/18) drives the great majority of cases; cervical cancer is the fourth most common malignancy in women globally and is highly preventable via HPV vaccination and screening [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK431093/).
- Squamous cell carcinoma is the dominant histology (~75%), followed by adenocarcinoma (~10–25%), with adenocarcinoma associated with worse survival; other rare subtypes include adenosquamous, clear cell, and small cell carcinoma [Global Cervical Cancer Incidence by Histological Subtype](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11043316/).
- FIGO 2018 staging and imaging/surgical staging set intent: radical hysterectomy with pelvic node dissection for stage IB1/IIA1, and concurrent platinum-based chemoradiation for bulkier or locally advanced disease (e.g., IB2/IIA2 and beyond) [NCCN Guidelines summary](https://emedicine.medscape.com/article/253513-guidelines).
- For high-risk FIGO 2014 stage III–IVA locally advanced disease, adding pembrolizumab to chemoradiotherapy improved progression-free survival (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.55–0.89) in KEYNOTE-A18, establishing a new standard and supporting FDA approval [KEYNOTE-A18 / FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-pembrolizumab-chemoradiotherapy-figo-2014-stage-iii-iva-cervical-cancer).
- In persistent/recurrent/metastatic disease, first-line pembrolizumab added to chemotherapy ± bevacizumab improved median OS to 26.4 vs 16.8 mo (HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.52–0.77) in KEYNOTE-826; benefit concentrated in PD-L1 CPS ≥1 (OS HR 0.60) [KEYNOTE-826](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2112435).
- PD-L1 (CPS) gates first-line immunotherapy use, while HPV status underpins prevention/screening; CPS ≥1 selects patients who derive the clearest pembrolizumab OS benefit [KEYNOTE-826 final OS](https://ascopubs.org/doi/abs/10.1200/JCO.23.00914).
- Second-line cemiplimab monotherapy improved OS vs chemotherapy (median 11.7 vs 8.5 mo, HR 0.67) regardless of PD-L1 in EMPOWER-Cervical 1 [EMPOWER-Cervical 1](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2112187).
- The tissue-factor–directed antibody-drug conjugate tisotumab vedotin improved OS vs chemotherapy (median 11.5 vs 9.5 mo, HR 0.70) in innovaTV 301, gaining full FDA approval for recurrent/metastatic disease after prior therapy [innovaTV 301](https://ascopost.com/issues/november-25-2023/in-recurrent-cervical-cancer-tisotumab-vedotin-tftv-improves-overall-survival-in-phase-iii-trial/).
