# Small cell lung cancer (SCLC)

## Overview
SCLC is a poorly differentiated, high-grade neuroendocrine carcinoma of the lung, accounting for roughly 13–17% of lung cancers and strongly linked to tobacco exposure. It is characterized by rapid doubling time, early widespread metastasis, and a brisk but short-lived response to chemotherapy. Disease is staged using the two-tier limited- vs extensive-stage (VALG) system, which sets curative versus palliative intent; about 60–70% of patients present with extensive-stage disease.

## Key facts
- SCLC is a high-grade neuroendocrine carcinoma making up ~13–17% of lung cancers (~30,000 US cases/year), tightly associated with smoking; only ~2–3% of cases occur in never-smokers [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482458/).
- Staging follows the two-tier limited-stage (confinable to one radiation port, curative intent with chemoradiation ± surgery) vs extensive-stage (palliative systemic therapy) framework; ~60–70% present extensive-stage [NCCN Guidelines SCLC v2.2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10203822/).
- Unlike NSCLC, SCLC lacks routine actionable oncogenic drivers; management is staging-driven rather than biomarker-gated, with DLL3 emerging as the first therapeutically exploited surface target [NCCN Guidelines SCLC v2.2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10203822/).
- Extensive-stage first line is platinum/etoposide plus a PD-L1 inhibitor: IMpower133 (atezolizumab) showed median OS 12.3 vs 10.3 mo (HR 0.70, p=0.0069) [IMpower133 JCO](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.20.01055).
- CASPIAN (durvalumab + platinum/etoposide) showed median OS 13.0 vs 10.3 mo (HR 0.73, p=0.0047), establishing the second chemoimmunotherapy standard [CASPIAN ESMO Open](https://www.esmoopen.com/article/S2059-7029(22)00029-1/fulltext).
- Limited-stage care is concurrent chemoradiation; ADRIATIC showed consolidation durvalumab improved median OS to 55.9 vs 33.4 mo (HR 0.73) and PFS 16.6 vs 9.2 mo [ADRIATIC NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2404873).
- For relapsed/progressive disease, tarlatamab (DLL3-targeted bispecific T-cell engager) gained FDA approval based on DeLLphi-301: ORR 40%, median DoR 9.7 mo, median OS 14.3 mo, with cytokine release syndrome as a key toxicity [Tarlatamab FDA Approval Summary, Clin Cancer Res](https://aacrjournals.org/clincancerres/article/32/7/1191/775509/FDA-Approval-Summary-Tarlatamab-for-the-Treatment).
