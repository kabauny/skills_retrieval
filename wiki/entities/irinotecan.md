# Irinotecan

## Overview

Irinotecan is a camptothecin-derivative topoisomerase I inhibitor used as a cytotoxic backbone in gastrointestinal cancers. It is a water-soluble prodrug that is converted by carboxylesterase to its active metabolite SN-38. Its principal roles are first- and later-line metastatic colorectal cancer (as FOLFIRI/IFL) and metastatic and adjuvant pancreatic cancer (as part of FOLFIRINOX). Dose-limiting toxicities are severe diarrhea and myelosuppression, both flagged in a boxed warning.

## Key facts

- Mechanism: irinotecan and its ~1000-fold more potent metabolite SN-38 bind the topoisomerase I–DNA complex, preventing religation of single-strand breaks and causing lethal double-strand DNA damage during replication [FDA/prescribing info](https://www.drugs.com/pro/irinotecan.html).
- FDA-approved indications: first-line metastatic colorectal cancer in combination with 5-fluorouracil and leucovorin, and metastatic colorectal cancer that has recurred or progressed after prior fluorouracil-based therapy [FDA/prescribing info](https://www.drugs.com/pro/irinotecan.html).
- PRODIGE 4/ACCORD 11 (Conroy, NEJM 2011): in metastatic pancreatic cancer, FOLFIRINOX vs gemcitabine improved median OS 11.1 vs 6.8 months (HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.45–0.73, P<0.001) [NEJM 2011](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1011923).
- PRODIGE 24/CCTG PA.6 (adjuvant resected pancreatic cancer): modified FOLFIRINOX vs gemcitabine improved median DFS 21.6 vs 12.8 months (HR 0.58, 95% CI 0.46–0.73, P<0.001); 5-year update showed median OS 53.5 vs 35.5 months (HR 0.68) [NEJM 2018](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1809775).
- Boxed warning: severe, potentially fatal late-onset diarrhea and severe myelosuppression, with deaths from sepsis following neutropenia; diarrhea and neutropenia are the frequent dose-limiting toxicities [FDA/prescribing info](https://www.drugs.com/pro/irinotecan.html).
- Pharmacogenomics: patients homozygous for UGT1A1*28 (7/7 genotype, ~10% of North Americans) have reduced SN-38 clearance and significantly increased risk of severe neutropenia and diarrhea, supporting reduced starting doses [UGT1A1 meta-analysis (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11492647/).
- Additional common adverse events: leukopenia, anemia, nausea/vomiting, alopecia, and acute cholinergic syndrome (early diarrhea, diaphoresis, abdominal cramping) manageable with atropine [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK554441/).
