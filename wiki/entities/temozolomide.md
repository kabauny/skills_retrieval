# Temozolomide

## Overview

Temozolomide (Temodar, TMZ) is an oral imidazotetrazine alkylating agent that crosses the blood-brain barrier and is used principally in malignant glioma. It is the chemotherapy backbone of the standard "Stupp protocol" for newly diagnosed glioblastoma, given concurrently with radiotherapy and then as adjuvant/maintenance cycles. It is valued for oral dosing and a manageable, predominantly hematologic toxicity profile.

## Key facts

- Mechanism: a prodrug spontaneously hydrolyzed at physiologic pH to the active metabolite MTIC, which methylates DNA at the O6 and N7 positions of guanine; the cytotoxic O6-methylguanine lesion drives mismatch-repair-mediated double-strand breaks and apoptosis, and is normally reversed by the repair enzyme MGMT [[FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/021029s036_037_038lbl.pdf)].
- FDA-approved indications: newly diagnosed glioblastoma (concomitant with radiotherapy, then maintenance) and, under Project Renewal, adjuvant treatment of newly diagnosed anaplastic astrocytoma and treatment of refractory anaplastic astrocytoma [[FDA drug approval summary](https://aacrjournals.org/clincancerres/article/11/19/6767/190430/Food-and-Drug-Administration-Drug-Approval-Summary)].
- Pivotal trial — EORTC-NCIC 26981/22981 (Stupp et al., NEJM 2005): 573 patients randomized to radiotherapy plus concomitant/adjuvant temozolomide vs radiotherapy alone; median OS 14.6 vs 12.1 months, death HR 0.63 (95% CI 0.52–0.75; P<0.001) [[Stupp NEJM 2005](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa043330)].
- Durable survival benefit: 2-year survival 26.5% vs 10.4% with radiotherapy alone, and the 5-year EORTC-NCIC analysis confirmed sustained benefit (~10% alive at 5 years) [[5-year EORTC-NCIC analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19269895/)].
- MGMT promoter methylation predicts benefit; in CeTeG/NOA-09, adding lomustine to temozolomide improved survival in MGMT-methylated newly diagnosed glioblastoma [[CeTeG/NOA-09](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7005933/)].
- Principal toxicity is myelosuppression (thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, anemia; nadirs ~26–28 days), which is dose-limiting and greater in elderly patients and women [[FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/021029s036_037_038lbl.pdf)].
- Other serious adverse reactions: myelodysplastic syndrome and secondary myeloid malignancies, Pneumocystis pneumonia (PCP prophylaxis required during concomitant therapy due to lymphopenia), and fatal/severe hepatotoxicity [[FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/021029s036_037_038lbl.pdf)].
