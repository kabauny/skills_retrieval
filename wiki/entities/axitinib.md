# Axitinib

## Overview
Axitinib (Inlyta) is an oral second-generation tyrosine kinase inhibitor that potently and selectively blocks VEGF receptors 1, 2, and 3. It is FDA-approved for advanced renal cell carcinoma (RCC), both as first-line therapy in combination with an immune checkpoint inhibitor (pembrolizumab or avelumab) and as single-agent second-line therapy after failure of one prior systemic regimen.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: selective inhibitor of VEGFR-1/2/3 at subnanomolar potency, with additional activity against PDGFR-α and c-KIT; this antiangiogenic action drives both efficacy and class toxicities [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=84137882-e000-47da-bd5b-fa76ab3c76f9&type=display).
- FDA indications: first-line advanced RCC in combination with pembrolizumab or with avelumab, and as a single agent for advanced RCC after failure of one prior systemic therapy [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=84137882-e000-47da-bd5b-fa76ab3c76f9&type=display).
- KEYNOTE-426 (phase 3, n=861, first-line ccRCC): pembrolizumab + axitinib vs sunitinib improved PFS (median 15.1 vs 11.1 mo, HR 0.69) and ORR (59.3% vs 35.7%); first interim OS HR 0.53 [KEYNOTE-426](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1816714).
- KEYNOTE-426 with ≥5-year follow-up showed sustained OS benefit (HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.71–0.99) supporting this combination as standard of care [KEYNOTE-426 5-year](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-03867-5).
- JAVELIN Renal 101 (phase 3, first-line): avelumab + axitinib vs sunitinib improved PFS in the overall population (median 13.8 vs 8.4 mo, HR 0.69; PD-L1+ HR 0.61) [JAVELIN Renal 101](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1816047).
- AXIS (phase 3, second-line): axitinib vs sorafenib improved median PFS (6.7 vs 4.7 mo, P<0.0001), establishing the original monotherapy approval [AXIS review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3873008/).
- Most common adverse reactions (≥20%): diarrhea, hypertension, fatigue, decreased appetite, nausea, dysphonia, palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia; serious risks include hypertension (Grade 3/4 ~16%), hemorrhage, arterial/venous thromboembolism, GI perforation, and hepatotoxicity [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=84137882-e000-47da-bd5b-fa76ab3c76f9&type=display).
