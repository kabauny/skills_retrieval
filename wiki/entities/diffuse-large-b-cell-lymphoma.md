# Diffuse Large B-cell Lymphoma

## Overview

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most common aggressive non-Hodgkin lymphoma and is potentially curable with chemoimmunotherapy. It is biologically heterogeneous, classified by cell of origin into germinal-center B-cell (GCB) and activated B-cell (ABC) subtypes, which carry distinct prognoses. Treatment is anchored on the anti-CD20 antibody rituximab combined with anthracycline-based chemotherapy, with antibody-drug conjugates and CAR T-cell therapy reshaping the modern landscape.

## Key facts

- DLBCL is the most common non-Hodgkin lymphoma, accounting for ~31% of NHL cases, with a US incidence of about 5.6 per 100,000/year and a median age at diagnosis of ~66 years [[Medscape DLBCL Guidelines](https://emedicine.medscape.com/article/202969-guidelines)].
- It can arise de novo or by transformation of an indolent lymphoma (e.g., follicular lymphoma); the major biologic split by gene-expression/IHC is GCB vs ABC cell of origin [[Medscape DLBCL Guidelines](https://emedicine.medscape.com/article/202969-guidelines)].
- Staging (PET/CT, IPI score) frames curative intent: the International Prognostic Index guides risk stratification, and POLARIX enrolled intermediate/high-risk patients (IPI 2-5) for whom outcomes are worse [[POLARIX (NEJM)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2115304)].
- Cell of origin matters prognostically: GCB subtype has superior outcomes versus ABC after R-CHOP (reported ~84% vs ~56% 3-year survival in cited series) [[CancerNetwork frontline DLBCL](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/journal-current-frontline-treatment-of-diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma)].
- "Double-hit" lymphoma with concurrent MYC and BCL2 rearrangements (MYC rearrangement seen in ~9-17% of cases) confers high R-CHOP failure risk and poor outcomes, often prompting more intensive regimens such as DA-EPOCH-R [[CancerNetwork frontline DLBCL](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/journal-current-frontline-treatment-of-diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma)].
- CD20 expression on B cells is the therapeutic target of rituximab, the backbone antibody added to chemotherapy; CD79b is the target of polatuzumab vedotin in newer regimens [[POLARIX (NEJM)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2115304)].
- First line: R-CHOP (rituximab + cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, prednisone) cures ~50-60% of patients; Pola-R-CHP (replacing vincristine with polatuzumab vedotin) improved PFS in POLARIX and is a new frontline standard for higher-risk disease [[POLARIX (NEJM)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2115304)] [[NCCN Patient Guidelines DLBCL 2025](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/nhl-diffuse-patient.pdf)].
- Relapsed/refractory disease is treated with salvage chemo plus autologous transplant or, increasingly, anti-CD19 CAR T-cell therapy and CD20-targeted bispecific antibodies [[NCCN Patient Guidelines DLBCL 2025](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/nhl-diffuse-patient.pdf)].
