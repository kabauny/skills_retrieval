# gemcitabine

## Overview

Gemcitabine (Gemzar) is a fluorinated nucleoside analogue antimetabolite that interferes with DNA synthesis and is used across multiple solid tumors. It is a chemotherapy backbone in pancreatic, biliary tract, urothelial, ovarian, and breast cancers, often paired with platinum agents or taxanes. Its 1997 approval in advanced pancreatic cancer was a landmark, establishing both a new standard of care and the "clinical benefit response" endpoint.

## Key facts

- Mechanism: gemcitabine is intracellularly phosphorylated by deoxycytidine kinase to active diphosphate and triphosphate metabolites that inhibit ribonucleotide reductase and incorporate into DNA, causing masked chain termination and blocking the G1/S transition [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2018/209604s003lbl.pdf).
- Principal FDA-approved indications: first-line locally advanced/metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma; with carboplatin in platinum-sensitive relapsed ovarian cancer (≥6 months); with paclitaxel in metastatic breast cancer after anthracycline failure; and (as established combinations) biliary tract and urothelial carcinoma [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2018/209604s003lbl.pdf).
- Pivotal pancreatic trial (Burris 1997, JCO): gemcitabine vs 5-FU improved median OS 5.65 vs 4.41 months (P=0.0025), 1-year survival 18% vs 2%, and clinical benefit response 24% vs 5% (P=0.0022) [Burris 1997 JCO](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.1997.15.6.2403).
- MPACT phase 3: adding nab-paclitaxel to gemcitabine in metastatic pancreatic cancer improved median OS 8.5 vs 6.7 months, HR 0.72 (P<0.001), with long-term (>3-year) survivors only in the combination arm [MPACT (Von Hoff)](https://academic.oup.com/jnci/article/107/2/dju413/902453).
- Most common toxicity is myelosuppression (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anemia), dose-limiting and amplified in combination regimens [Pfizer prescribing info](https://www.pfizermedical.com/gemcitabine/warnings).
- Serious/notable adverse events: pulmonary toxicity (interstitial pneumonitis, ARDS, fatal respiratory failure), hemolytic uremic syndrome with renal failure (~0.25%), capillary leak syndrome, hepatotoxicity, and posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome [Pfizer prescribing info](https://www.pfizermedical.com/gemcitabine/warnings).
