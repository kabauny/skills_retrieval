# Lenvatinib

## Overview
Lenvatinib (LENVIMA) is an oral multitargeted receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor used across several solid tumors. It is approved as monotherapy for radioactive-iodine-refractory differentiated thyroid cancer and unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma, and in combination regimens (with everolimus in renal cell carcinoma and with pembrolizumab in endometrial carcinoma and RCC). Its broad antiangiogenic and antitumor activity comes at the cost of substantial vascular and organ toxicity requiring active dose management.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: inhibits VEGFR1-3, FGFR1-4, PDGFRα, KIT, and RET kinases, blocking pathogenic angiogenesis, tumor growth, and progression [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2021/206947s021lbl.pdf).
- Principal indications: radioactive-iodine-refractory differentiated thyroid cancer (monotherapy), unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (first-line monotherapy), advanced RCC (with everolimus or pembrolizumab), and advanced endometrial carcinoma not MSI-H/dMMR (with pembrolizumab) [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2021/206947s021lbl.pdf).
- SELECT (phase 3, RAI-refractory DTC): lenvatinib vs placebo improved median PFS to 18.3 vs 3.6 months with objective response rate 64.8% vs 1.5% [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK567768/).
- REFLECT (phase 3, first-line HCC): lenvatinib was non-inferior to sorafenib for OS (median 13.6 vs 12.3 mo; HR 0.92, 95% CI 0.79-1.06) and superior for PFS (HR 0.66) and ORR (OR 3.13) [REFLECT (Lancet)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29433850/).
- KEYNOTE-775/Study 309 (phase 3, previously treated advanced endometrial cancer): lenvatinib + pembrolizumab vs chemotherapy improved OS in the overall population (median 18.3 vs 11.4 mo; HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.51-0.75) and pMMR population (17.4 vs 12.0 mo; HR 0.68) [KEYNOTE-775 (NEJM)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJMoa2108330).
- Serious/notable adverse events: hypertension (grade 3 in ~13%), proteinuria (~25%, grade 3 ~6% in REFLECT), hepatotoxicity, cardiac dysfunction, arterial thromboembolic events, renal failure, GI perforation/fistula, hemorrhage, and QT prolongation; blood pressure must be controlled before and monitored during treatment [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2021/206947s021lbl.pdf).
