# I-131 iobenguane

## Overview

I-131 iobenguane (Azedra) is a high-specific-activity radiopharmaceutical: a structural analog of norepinephrine radiolabeled with the beta-emitting isotope iodine-131. It is taken up into neural-crest-derived tumor cells via the norepinephrine transporter (NET/SLC6A2), where I-131 delivers targeted cytotoxic radiation. In July 2018 it became the first FDA-approved systemic therapy for iobenguane-scan-positive, unresectable, locally advanced or metastatic pheochromocytoma or paraganglioma (PPGL) in patients aged 12 and older.

## Key facts

- Mechanism: norepinephrine analog actively transported into chromaffin/neuroendocrine tumor cells via the norepinephrine transporter (NET), where iodine-131 emits cytotoxic beta particles — requiring tumors to be iobenguane-scan positive for uptake [Iobenguane, DrugBank](https://go.drugbank.com/drugs/DB06704)
- FDA approved July 2018 as the first treatment for unresectable, locally advanced or metastatic PPGL requiring systemic anticancer therapy, in patients ≥12 years old [FDA approval, AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/fda-approves-the-first-treatment-for-unresectable-pheochromocytoma-or-paraganglioma)
- Approval based on a multicenter, open-label, pivotal phase 2b trial (Pryma et al.) of 74 enrolled patients, 68 evaluable, given up to two therapeutic doses 3 months apart [ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/issues/november-25-2018/iobenguane-i-131-for-advanced-pheochromocytoma-or-paraganglioma/)
- Primary endpoint met: 25% of patients (17/68; 95% CI 16–37%) achieved ≥50% reduction in all antihypertensive medication for ≥6 months; 32% among those receiving 2 doses [JCO 2018, abstract 4005](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2018.36.15_suppl.4005)
- Tumor response: ~22–23% partial response (overall response 15/68), with the large majority achieving partial response or stable disease; median overall survival 36.7 months (95% CI 29.9–49.1) [OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/fda-approves-iobenguane-i131-for-rare-neuroendocrine-tumors)
- Serious adverse events dominated by myelosuppression: among 88 treated patients, Grade 4 thrombocytopenia 33%, Grade 4 neutropenia 16%, Grade 4 anemia 7%; common Grade 3–4 events included lymphopenia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, fatigue, and anemia [Full Prescribing Information](https://www.azedra.com/content/pdf/full-prescribing-information.pdf)
- Other notable adverse reactions: nausea, dizziness, hypertension, vomiting, and increased INR; thyroid blockade and prior hydration/dosimetry steps are part of administration [RxList](https://www.rxlist.com/azedra-drug.htm)
