# Urothelial carcinoma

## Overview
Urothelial carcinoma arises from the transitional epithelium lining the bladder, ureters, and renal pelvis, with the bladder the dominant site. It is among the most common urologic cancers, with disease behavior and treatment intent governed by depth of muscle invasion and metastatic status. Management spans intravesical therapy for non-muscle-invasive disease, cisplatin-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy plus radical cystectomy for muscle-invasive disease, and an antibody-drug-conjugate/immunotherapy combination for advanced/metastatic disease.

## Key facts
- Bladder cancer is the fourth most commonly diagnosed cancer in men and tenth most common in women in the US; roughly 75% present as non-muscle-invasive disease (NMIBC), with muscle-invasive (MIBC) and metastatic disease comprising the remainder [AUA/ASCO/ASTRO/SUO guideline](https://www.auanet.org/guidelines-and-quality/guidelines/bladder-cancer-non-metastatic-muscle-invasive-guideline).
- The dominant histology is urothelial (transitional cell) carcinoma; variant/divergent histologies (squamous, glandular/adenocarcinoma, plasmacytoid, micropapillary, sarcomatoid) carry distinct biology and worse prognosis [NCCN Bladder Cancer Guidelines Insights v3.2024](https://jnccn.org/view/journals/jnccn/22/4/article-p216.xml).
- Muscle invasion sets intent: NMIBC is managed with TURBT plus intravesical therapy (e.g., BCG), whereas MIBC is treated with curative-intent cisplatin-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by radical cystectomy [AUA/ASCO/ASTRO/SUO guideline](https://www.auanet.org/guidelines-and-quality/guidelines/bladder-cancer-non-metastatic-muscle-invasive-guideline).
- Key biomarkers gating therapy include FGFR2/3 alterations (target for erdafitinib), cisplatin eligibility (renal function/ECOG/hearing), Nectin-4 expression (target of enfortumab vedotin), HER2, and PD-L1 (which does not reliably predict checkpoint-inhibitor benefit in the frontline setting) [NEJM EV-302](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2312117).
- First-line advanced/metastatic standard of care is enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab: in the phase 3 EV-302/KEYNOTE-A39 trial, median OS was 33.8 vs 15.9 months versus platinum chemotherapy (HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.43–0.61) with confirmed ORR 67.7% vs 44.4% [NEJM EV-302](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2312117).
- For FGFR2/3-altered disease after prior anti–PD-(L)1 therapy, erdafitinib improved median OS to 12.1 vs 7.8 months versus chemotherapy (HR 0.64, 95% CI 0.47–0.88, P=.005) in the phase 3 THOR trial and holds full FDA approval [FDA erdafitinib approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-erdafitinib-locally-advanced-or-metastatic-urothelial-carcinoma).
- The treatment landscape by line: NMIBC (intravesical BCG) → MIBC (neoadjuvant cisplatin + cystectomy, with adjuvant nivolumab an option) → first-line metastatic (enfortumab vedotin + pembrolizumab) → later lines (erdafitinib for FGFR-altered disease, single-agent enfortumab vedotin, checkpoint inhibitors, chemotherapy) [NCCN Bladder Cancer Guidelines Insights v3.2024](https://jnccn.org/view/journals/jnccn/22/4/article-p216.xml).
