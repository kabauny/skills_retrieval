# Endometrial cancer

## Overview
Endometrial (uterine) cancer is the most common gynecologic malignancy and the fourth-most common cancer in U.S. women, with the large majority being endometrioid histology and the remainder higher-risk types such as serous, clear cell, and carcinosarcoma. Most cases are diagnosed at a localized stage and are curable with surgery (total hysterectomy/bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy with staging), with adjuvant radiation and/or chemotherapy added by risk. The TCGA-derived molecular classification (POLE-ultramutated, MMR-deficient/MSI-H, p53-abnormal/copy-number high, and no-specific-molecular-profile/copy-number low) now refines prognosis and adjuvant decisions. In advanced/recurrent disease, immune checkpoint inhibitors added to carboplatin–paclitaxel have become frontline standard of care.

## Key facts
- It is the most common gynecologic cancer and 4th-most common cancer in U.S. women; ~80% are endometrioid histology, with serous and other type II histologies being less common but more aggressive and rising in incidence [SEER trends analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9904185/).
- Surgical FIGO staging and risk stratification set intent: most patients present with localized disease (~70%) curable by hysterectomy ± adjuvant therapy, whereas stage III–IV or recurrent disease is treated with palliative-intent systemic therapy [SEER trends analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9904185/).
- Four molecular subtypes guide prognosis and adjuvant therapy — POLE-ultramutated (best prognosis), MMR-deficient/MSI-H (intermediate), p53-abnormal/copy-number high (worst), and no-specific-molecular-profile — assessed via IHC for p53 and MMR proteins plus POLE mutation testing [NCCN endometrial management update](https://jnccn.org/view/journals/jnccn/22/Supplement/article-e245014.xml?print=).
- MMR/MSI status gates immunotherapy: dMMR/MSI-H tumors derive the largest benefit from PD-1 blockade, while p53-abnormal tumors require multimodality chemotherapy-based regimens [NCCN endometrial management update](https://jnccn.org/view/journals/jnccn/22/Supplement/article-e245014.xml?print=).
- In NRG-GY018/KEYNOTE-868 (810 pts), adding pembrolizumab to carboplatin–paclitaxel improved PFS, with a 70% reduction in progression/death in the dMMR cohort (HR 0.30) and 46% reduction in the pMMR cohort (HR 0.54) [NRG-GY018, NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2302312).
- In the RUBY trial (ENGOT-EN6/GOG-3031, 494 pts), dostarlimab plus chemotherapy improved median OS in the overall population to 44.6 vs 28.2 months (31% reduction in risk of death, +16.4 mo), with the largest PFS benefit in dMMR/MSI-H disease [RUBY OS, Annals of Oncology](https://www.annalsofoncology.org/article/S0923-7534(24)00721-X/fulltext).
- HER2 overexpression (notably in serous carcinoma) and hormone receptor (ER/PR) status further direct therapy — HER2-targeted and endocrine approaches respectively — making biomarker testing a core part of treatment planning [Molecular classification and therapy selection](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11172295/).
