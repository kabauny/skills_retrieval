# bevacizumab

## Overview

Bevacizumab (Avastin) is a recombinant humanized monoclonal IgG1 antibody that targets vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A), blocking tumor angiogenesis. First approved in the U.S. in 2004 for metastatic colorectal cancer, it is now approved across multiple solid tumors, generally given in combination with chemotherapy or, more recently, immunotherapy. It carries class-specific antiangiogenic toxicities including hemorrhage, GI perforation, and impaired wound healing.

## Key facts

- Mechanism: humanized anti-VEGF-A monoclonal IgG1 antibody that prevents VEGF from binding VEGFR-1/2 on endothelial cells, reducing tumor neovascularization and growth [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2020/125085s332lbl.pdf).
- Approved across seven principal tumor types: metastatic colorectal cancer, non-squamous NSCLC, metastatic renal cell carcinoma, recurrent ovarian/fallopian tube/peritoneal cancer, recurrent glioblastoma, persistent/recurrent/metastatic cervical cancer, and unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma [StatPearls/NIH](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482126/).
- Pivotal trial AVF2107g (Hurwitz, NEJM 2004): adding bevacizumab to IFL chemotherapy in first-line metastatic colorectal cancer raised median OS from 15.6 to 20.3 months (HR 0.66, p<0.001) [Hurwitz NEJM 2004](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa032691).
- In the same trial, objective response rate improved from 34.8% to 44.8% (p=0.004), establishing the angiogenesis-inhibition strategy in CRC [Hurwitz NEJM 2004](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa032691).
- IMbrave150 (NEJM 2020): atezolizumab plus bevacizumab vs sorafenib in unresectable HCC improved median OS to 19.2 vs 13.4 months (HR 0.66) and PFS (HR 0.65), making it a first-line standard [IMbrave150 update, J Hepatol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34902530/).
- Serious adverse events: GI perforation (sometimes fatal), serious or fatal hemorrhage (notably in squamous NSCLC), and surgery/wound-healing complications—hold ≥28 days around elective surgery [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2020/125085s332lbl.pdf).
- Other common toxicities: hypertension (19-42%), proteinuria/nephrotic syndrome, and arterial thromboembolic events including stroke and MI [StatPearls/NIH](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK482126/).
