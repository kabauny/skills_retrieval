# Colorectal cancer (CRC)

## Overview
Colorectal cancer is the third most common cancer and the second leading cause of cancer death in the United States, with adenocarcinoma accounting for roughly 90% of cases. Management diverges sharply by stage, primary tumor sidedness, and molecular profile, with treatment intent set by resectability. Universal biomarker testing (MMR/MSI, RAS, BRAF V600E, HER2) now gates the choice of immunotherapy, anti-EGFR antibodies, and targeted regimens in the metastatic setting.

## Key facts
- Epidemiology and histology: the American Cancer Society estimated 152,810 new U.S. cases and 53,010 deaths in 2024, making CRC the second leading cause of cancer death; adenocarcinoma of the colorectal mucosa comprises ~90% of cases, and incidence is rising 1–2% per year in people under 55 [ACS Cancer Facts & Figures 2024](https://www.cancer.org/content/dam/cancer-org/research/cancer-facts-and-statistics/annual-cancer-facts-and-figures/2024/2024-cancer-facts-and-figures-acs.pdf).
- Staging and intent: localized/regional disease may be resected for cure (often with adjuvant chemotherapy), while 50–60% of patients eventually develop metastatic disease, where systemic therapy is generally palliative unless oligometastatic liver disease is rendered resectable; NCCN guidelines structure therapy around resectability and stage [NCCN Colon Cancer v3.2024](https://jnccn.org/abstract/journals/jnccn/22/2D/article-e240029.xml).
- RAS biomarker and sidedness: KRAS/NRAS mutations occur in ~50% of CRC and predict lack of benefit from anti-EGFR antibodies; NCCN restricts front-line anti-EGFR therapy (cetuximab/panitumumab) to RAS wild-type, left-sided tumors [AJMC biomarker review](https://www.ajmc.com/view/evolution-biomarkers-guide-treatment-metastatic-colorectal-cancer).
- MMR/MSI biomarker: universal MMR/MSI testing is recommended; in MSI-H/dMMR metastatic CRC, first-line pembrolizumab beat chemotherapy in KEYNOTE-177 (median PFS 16.5 vs 8.2 mo, HR 0.60) with durable benefit and improved OS at 5 years (77.5 vs 36.7 mo, HR 0.73) despite 62% crossover [KEYNOTE-177 (NEJM)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2017699).
- BRAF V600E biomarker: present in ~5–9% of CRC and prognostically poor; in previously treated BRAF V600E mCRC the BEACON CRC trial showed encorafenib + cetuximab (doublet) improved median OS to 9.3 mo vs 5.9 mo with chemotherapy (HR ~0.61) [BEACON CRC (NEJM)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1908075).
- HER2 biomarker: HER2 amplification defines a small actionable subset; the MOUNTAINEER trial of tucatinib + trastuzumab in chemotherapy-refractory HER2+, RAS wild-type mCRC achieved a 38.1% objective response rate (median DoR 12.4 mo), supporting FDA accelerated approval on January 19, 2023 [FDA tucatinib approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-tucatinib-trastuzumab-colorectal-cancer).
- Treatment landscape: chemotherapy backbones (FOLFOX, FOLFIRI, CAPEOX) plus bevacizumab anchor most metastatic regimens, with anti-EGFR added for RAS wild-type left-sided tumors, immunotherapy first-line for MSI-H/dMMR, and biomarker-matched targeted therapy for BRAF V600E and HER2-positive disease [NCCN Colon Cancer v3.2024](https://jnccn.org/abstract/journals/jnccn/22/2D/article-e240029.xml).
