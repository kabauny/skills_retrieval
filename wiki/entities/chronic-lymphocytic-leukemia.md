# Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

## Overview

Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) is a mature B-cell neoplasm and the most common adult leukemia in the Western world, with an age-adjusted incidence around 4.6 per 100,000/year and a median age at diagnosis near 70 years. Small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL) is the same disease biologically, distinguished only by predominantly nodal rather than peripheral-blood disease. The clinical course is highly variable, and management is increasingly defined by molecular biomarkers (IGHV mutation status, TP53/del(17p)) rather than chemotherapy. Targeted agents — BTK inhibitors and the BCL2 inhibitor venetoclax — have largely replaced chemoimmunotherapy in the front line.

## Key facts

- CLL is the most common leukemia in adults, accounting for roughly 25–35% of US leukemias, with a median diagnosis age near 70; SLL is considered the same underlying disorder as CLL per WHO classification [CLL 2025 Update](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11803567/).
- Treatment is not "staged" by anatomy but triggered by active/symptomatic disease (per iwCLL criteria); asymptomatic early-stage disease is observed ("watch and wait") rather than treated, and intent is disease control rather than cure with standard targeted therapy [CLL 2025 Update](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11803567/).
- TP53 disruption — del(17p) (found in ~5–8% of treatment-naive patients) and/or TP53 mutation — is the strongest predictor of chemo-refractoriness and the first decision node; it mandates BTK- or BCL2-targeted therapy and excludes chemoimmunotherapy [CLL Prognostic Biomarkers Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7226446/).
- Unmutated IGHV status, TP53 status, and β2-microglobulin are core CLL-IPI prognostic factors; unmutated IGHV predicts inferior response to chemoimmunotherapy and favors continuous BTK-inhibitor or fixed-duration venetoclax-based regimens [CLL Prognostic Biomarkers Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7226446/).
- Front-line options are continuous BTK inhibitors (acalabrutinib, zanubrutinib, ibrutinib) or fixed-duration venetoclax-based regimens with a CD20 antibody; chemoimmunotherapy is no longer recommended for high-risk (TP53-aberrant) disease [First-line CLL in targeted era](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41408-025-01434-2).
- CLL14 (venetoclax + obinutuzumab vs chlorambucil + obinutuzumab, fixed 12-month therapy) showed durable benefit: at 6 years median PFS 76.2 vs 36.4 months (HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.31–0.52, P<.0001), supporting FDA approval (May 2019) [CLL14 NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1815281).
- ELEVATE-TN (acalabrutinib ± obinutuzumab vs obinutuzumab + chlorambucil) showed median PFS not reached for both acalabrutinib arms vs 27.8 months for chemoimmunotherapy at 74.5-month follow-up (both P<.0001; 72-month PFS 78.0% with obinutuzumab vs 17.2% chemoimmunotherapy) [ELEVATE-TN Lancet](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34974526/).
- Major toxicities reflect drug class: venetoclax carries tumor lysis syndrome risk requiring ramp-up dosing; BTK inhibitors cause atrial fibrillation, hypertension, and bleeding; obinutuzumab causes infusion reactions and neutropenia/infection [First-line CLL in targeted era](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41408-025-01434-2).
