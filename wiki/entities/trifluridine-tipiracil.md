# Trifluridine/tipiracil

## Overview

Trifluridine/tipiracil (TAS-102, Lonsurf) is an oral combination cytotoxic agent: trifluridine is a thymidine-based nucleoside analog that is incorporated into DNA after phosphorylation, while tipiracil is a thymidine phosphorylase inhibitor that blocks trifluridine degradation, raising its bioavailability. It is FDA-approved for previously treated metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) and for previously treated metastatic gastric/gastroesophageal junction (GEJ) adenocarcinoma. In mCRC it is now most often combined with bevacizumab, and it remains under investigation for earlier minimal-residual-disease settings.

## Key facts

- Trifluridine is a thymidine analog incorporated into DNA to disrupt function; tipiracil inhibits thymidine phosphorylase to prevent trifluridine breakdown and sustain plasma levels [[FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/207981s012lbl.pdf)].
- First FDA approval Sept 2015 for mCRC after fluoropyrimidine, oxaliplatin, irinotecan, anti-VEGF, and (if RAS wild-type) anti-EGFR therapy [[Lonsurf review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5013844/)].
- Pivotal RECOURSE phase 3 trial (N=800, randomized 2:1 vs placebo + BSC): median OS 7.1 vs 5.3 months, HR 0.68 (95% CI 0.58–0.81; P<0.001) [[Lonsurf review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5013844/)].
- SUNLIGHT phase 3 (N=492) added bevacizumab to trifluridine/tipiracil: median OS 10.8 vs 7.5 months, HR 0.61 (95% CI 0.49–0.77); median PFS 5.6 vs 2.4 months [[SUNLIGHT, NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2214963)].
- FDA approved the trifluridine/tipiracil + bevacizumab combination for previously treated mCRC in August 2023 [[FDA approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-approvals-and-databases/fda-approves-trifluridine-and-tipiracil-bevacizumab-previously-treated-metastatic-colorectal-cancer)].
- Gastric/GEJ approval (2019) based on TAGS phase 3 (N=507): median OS 5.7 vs 3.6 months, HR 0.69 (95% CI 0.56–0.85) [[TAGS, Lancet Oncol/PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30355453/)].
- Toxicity is predominantly hematologic: neutropenia (~38%, grade ≥3 ~34%), leukopenia, anemia, plus GI effects (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea) and fatigue; febrile neutropenia occurs in ~4% [[TAGS, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30355453/)].
- Investigational use in MRD-positive resected colorectal cancer (e.g., ALTAIR program) reflects efforts to move the agent earlier in the disease course [[Lonsurf review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5013844/)].
