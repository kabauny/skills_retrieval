# Multiple Myeloma

## Overview

Multiple myeloma is a malignant plasma-cell neoplasm of the bone marrow, the second most common hematologic cancer, characterized by clonal plasma-cell proliferation, monoclonal (M) protein production, and end-organ damage. It is generally incurable but highly treatable, with diagnosis resting on the IMWG criteria (clonal marrow plasma cells plus CRAB end-organ damage or SLiM biomarkers). Therapy intent is uniformly disease-controlling rather than curative, and is stratified primarily by autologous stem-cell transplant (ASCT) eligibility and cytogenetic risk.

## Key facts

- Epidemiology: ~35,000 new U.S. cases yearly; median age at diagnosis ~69, with higher incidence in men and people of African descent. The malignant cell is a terminally differentiated plasma cell, and disease evolves from the precursor states MGUS and smoldering myeloma [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK534764/).
- Diagnosis requires ≥10% clonal marrow plasma cells (or biopsy-proven plasmacytoma) plus a myeloma-defining event: CRAB features (hyperCalcemia, Renal insufficiency, Anemia, lytic Bone lesions) or SLiM biomarkers (≥60% marrow plasma cells, serum free light-chain ratio ≥100, or >1 focal MRI lesion) [IMWG criteria](https://www.myeloma.org/international-myeloma-working-group-imwg-criteria-diagnosis-multiple-myeloma).
- Staging by Revised ISS (R-ISS) combines serum β2-microglobulin and albumin with LDH and high-risk cytogenetics; this defines prognosis rather than resectability, since myeloma is systemic and treatment intent is always palliative/disease-controlling, not curative [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK534764/).
- Biomarkers gating therapy: high-risk cytogenetic abnormalities by FISH — del(17p), t(4;14), t(14;16), and gain/amp(1q21) — occur in ~20-25% of new cases, confer inferior PFS/OS, and prompt more intensive or risk-adapted regimens [ASH 2025 high-risk criteria](https://ashpublications.org/thehematologist/article/doi/10.1182/hem.V22.6.202564/547773/Risk-Rewritten-The-Biology-Driven-2025).
- Transplant-eligible first line is now a quadruplet: in the phase 3 PERSEUS trial, subcutaneous daratumumab + bortezomib/lenalidomide/dexamethasone (D-VRd) around ASCT cut progression/death vs VRd (48-mo PFS 84.3% vs 67.7%; HR 0.42, 95% CI 0.30-0.59, P<0.001) [PERSEUS NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/pdf/10.1056/NEJMoa2312054).
- D-VRd produced deeper responses than VRd, with higher complete-response-or-better and MRD-negativity rates, supporting quadruplet therapy as standard of care for transplant-eligible newly diagnosed disease [ASCO Post PERSEUS](https://ascopost.com/issues/january-25-2024/perseus-daratumumab-regimen-significantly-improves-progression-free-survival-in-newly-diagnosed-multiple-myeloma/).
- Treatment landscape by line: induction (triplet/quadruplet) ± ASCT and maintenance for newly diagnosed disease; at relapse, regimens rotate proteasome inhibitors, immunomodulators, and anti-CD38 antibodies, escalating to BCMA-directed CAR-T and bispecific antibodies (e.g., teclistamab) in relapsed/refractory disease [NCI daratumumab + VRd](https://www.cancer.gov/news-events/cancer-currents-blog/2024/multiple-myeloma-darzalex-plus-vrd).
