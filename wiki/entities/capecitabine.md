# Capecitabine

## Overview
Capecitabine (Xeloda) is an oral fluoropyrimidine carbamate, a prodrug enzymatically converted to 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) preferentially in tumor tissue. It is widely used across colorectal, breast, gastroesophageal, and pancreatic cancers as a single agent or in combination regimens. In December 2022 it became the first drug to receive an updated label under the FDA's Project Renewal initiative, which modernized its indications, dosing, and safety information.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: capecitabine is a prodrug converted through a three-step enzymatic cascade (final step via tumor-enriched thymidine phosphorylase) to 5-FU; its metabolites FdUMP inhibit thymidylate synthase (blocking DNA synthesis) and FUTP is misincorporated into RNA, disrupting RNA processing and protein synthesis [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2001/20896S6lbl.pdf).
- Principal FDA-approved indications include unresectable/metastatic colorectal cancer (single agent or combination), adjuvant stage III colon cancer, advanced/metastatic breast cancer (alone or with docetaxel after anthracycline failure), unresectable/metastatic gastric/esophageal/GEJ cancer, and adjuvant pancreatic adenocarcinoma as part of combination therapy [FDA Project Renewal Approval Summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11649461/).
- The pivotal X-ACT trial (n=1987) compared adjuvant capecitabine vs bolus 5-FU/leucovorin in resected stage III colon cancer; capecitabine was at least equivalent for disease-free survival (HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.75-1.00) and trended toward improved relapse-free survival (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.74-0.99; P=0.04) [X-ACT, NEJM 2005](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa043116).
- At final analysis (median follow-up 6.9 years), X-ACT confirmed at-least equivalence for disease-free survival (HR 0.88, 95% CI 0.77-1.01) and overall survival (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.74-1.01), with significantly fewer adverse events than 5-FU/leucovorin [X-ACT final results, Ann Oncol 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21896539/).
- Boxed warning: co-administration with warfarin or other coumarin anticoagulants can markedly increase INR and cause severe, potentially fatal bleeding, requiring close monitoring [FDA Project Renewal Approval Summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11649461/).
- DPD (DPYD) deficiency confers risk of severe, life-threatening, or fatal toxicity; no dose is proven safe in complete deficiency, and the FDA advises DPYD testing before treatment unless therapy is urgent [FDA DPD safety announcement](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/safety-announcement-fda-highlights-importance-dpd-deficiency-discussions-patients-prior-capecitabine).
- Most common dose-limiting toxicities are palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia (hand-foot syndrome) and diarrhea; other notable adverse events include nausea, vomiting, stomatitis, fatigue, hyperbilirubinemia, and cardiotoxicity [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2001/20896S6lbl.pdf).
