# Ifosfamide

## Overview
Ifosfamide (IFEX) is an oxazaphosphorine alkylating agent and a structural analog of cyclophosphamide. It is a prodrug that requires hepatic cytochrome P450 activation to its cytotoxic alkylating species. Its sole FDA-approved indication is third-line combination chemotherapy for germ cell testicular cancer, but it is widely used off-label for sarcomas and lymphomas. It must be given with the uroprotectant mesna and vigorous hydration to limit bladder toxicity.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: ifosfamide is a prodrug activated by hepatic CYP450 (notably CYP3A4/2B6) by ring hydroxylation to 4-hydroxyifosfamide, which decomposes to the cytotoxic isophosphoramide mustard that crosslinks DNA at guanine N-7 positions, plus the urotoxic byproduct acrolein [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2018/019763s020lbl.pdf).
- FDA-approved indication: in combination with other antineoplastic agents for third-line chemotherapy of germ cell testicular cancer, dosed 1.2 g/m²/day IV for 5 consecutive days per 21-day cycle [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2018/019763s020lbl.pdf).
- Common off-label use: soft-tissue and Ewing sarcoma (objective response ~40%), osteosarcoma, and salvage therapy for Hodgkin/non-Hodgkin lymphoma [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542169/).
- Hemorrhagic cystitis: acrolein accumulates in the bladder causing ulceration and hemorrhage; mandatory co-administration of mesna (which binds urinary acrolein) plus ≥2 L/day hydration markedly reduces this urotoxicity [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2018/019763s020lbl.pdf).
- CNS/encephalopathy: ifosfamide-induced encephalopathy (confusion, hallucinations, seizures, rarely coma/death) occurs in roughly 10–30% of patients, usually within hours to days and typically reversible; methylene blue is used for treatment/prevention [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542169/), [IIE review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10332193/).
- Nephrotoxicity: dose-dependent renal tubular injury including Fanconi syndrome with metabolic acidosis and phosphate wasting (~5%, higher in children and with concurrent cisplatin); renal toxicity occurred in ~6% as a single agent [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542169/), [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2018/019763s020lbl.pdf).
- Other serious adverse events: severe myelosuppression with risk of fatal infection/sepsis, alopecia (~90%), nausea/vomiting (>50%), and risk of secondary malignancy; the label carries warnings for CNS, renal, and hematologic toxicity [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2018/019763s020lbl.pdf).
