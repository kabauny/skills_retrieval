# Cytarabine

## Overview
Cytarabine (cytosine arabinoside, Ara-C) is a pyrimidine nucleoside antimetabolite and a backbone agent in the treatment of acute myeloid leukemia (AML). It is used at standard doses in "7+3" induction (with an anthracycline) and at high doses for post-remission consolidation, and in liposomal/intrathecal forms for leptomeningeal disease. It is one of the oldest and most effective cytotoxics in hematologic malignancy.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: cytarabine is a deoxycytidine analog phosphorylated intracellularly (via deoxycytidine kinase) to ara-CTP, which is incorporated into DNA and inhibits DNA polymerase, halting DNA synthesis and arresting cells at the G1/S boundary; it is cell-cycle (S-phase) specific [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557680/).
- Principal indications: remission induction and consolidation in AML (and ALL, CML blast phase) at conventional/high doses; intrathecal liposomal cytarabine (DepoCyt) is FDA-approved for lymphomatous meningitis [DepoCyt FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/1999/21041lbl.pdf).
- "7+3" induction: cytarabine 100-200 mg/m2/day continuous infusion on days 1-7 plus daunorubicin or idarubicin on days 1-3 remains the standard induction regimen; SWOG S1203 confirmed 7+3 as standard of care for younger AML patients [ASH Clinical News / SWOG S1203](https://ashpublications.org/ashclinicalnews/news/1929/Results-from-Phase-III-SWOG-Trial-7-3-Still-the).
- Pivotal consolidation trial (CALGB 8525, Mayer NEJM 1994): high-dose cytarabine (3 g/m2 twice daily) vs lower doses gave superior 5-year continuous complete remission in patients <=60 (42% vs 29% for 400 mg/m2 vs 19% for 100 mg/m2), establishing high-dose Ara-C consolidation [CALGB 8525](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2653132/).
- Liposomal daunorubicin-cytarabine (CPX-351) outperformed conventional 7+3 in older patients with high-risk/secondary AML: median OS 9.56 vs 5.95 months (HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.52-0.90) [JCO CPX-351](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2017.77.6112).
- Notable/serious adverse events: profound myelosuppression (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia); high-dose Ara-C causes cerebellar toxicity (ataxia, dysarthria) in ~10-15%, plus seizures and encephalopathy; also mucositis, conjunctivitis, and the "cytarabine syndrome" (fever, myalgia, rash); renal impairment increases neurotoxicity risk [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557680/).
