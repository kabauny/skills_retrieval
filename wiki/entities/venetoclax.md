# Venetoclax

## Overview

Venetoclax (brand name Venclexta) is an oral, selective small-molecule inhibitor of the anti-apoptotic protein BCL-2. By restoring programmed cell death in malignant cells that overexpress BCL-2, it has become a backbone of fixed-duration chemotherapy-free regimens in chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) and a standard combination partner for older or unfit patients with acute myeloid leukemia (AML). First approved in 2016, it is now a cornerstone therapy across both diseases.

## Key facts

- Mechanism: venetoclax binds BCL-2 with >500-fold higher affinity than to BCL-XL/BCL-w, displacing pro-apoptotic proteins such as BIM, triggering mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization and caspase-driven apoptosis [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2016/208573s000lbl.pdf).
- Principal FDA-approved indications: adults with CLL or small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL), and in combination with azacitidine, decitabine, or low-dose cytarabine for newly diagnosed AML in adults ≥75 years or with comorbidities precluding intensive induction chemotherapy [FDA approval history](https://www.drugs.com/history/venclexta.html).
- MURANO (relapsed/refractory CLL): venetoclax + rituximab vs bendamustine-rituximab improved overall survival, OS HR 0.50 (95% CI 0.30–0.85) at 36-month median follow-up [MURANO](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.18.01580).
- CLL14 (previously untreated CLL with comorbidities): fixed-duration venetoclax + obinutuzumab vs chlorambucil-obinutuzumab reduced deaths (22% vs 32%; HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.48–1.01) with deep MRD eradication [CLL14](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/144/18/1924/516941/Venetoclax-obinutuzumab-for-previously-untreated).
- VIALE-A (untreated AML, intensive-chemo-ineligible): venetoclax + azacitidine vs placebo-azacitidine improved median OS to 14.7 vs 9.6 months, OS HR 0.66 (95% CI 0.52–0.85; p<0.001), with CR+CRi 66.4% vs 28.3% [VIALE-A (NEJM)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2012971).
- Boxed/serious risk — tumor lysis syndrome (TLS), including fatal cases and renal failure, can occur as early as 6–8 hours after the first dose; mitigated by weekly dose ramp-up, hydration, and anti-hyperuricemics [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2016/208573s000lbl.pdf).
- Other notable adverse events: Grade 3/4 neutropenia in 63–64% of CLL patients, plus infections, anemia, thrombocytopenia, and diarrhea [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2016/208573s000lbl.pdf).
