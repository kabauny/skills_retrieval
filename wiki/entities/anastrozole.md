# Anastrozole

## Overview
Anastrozole (Arimidex) is an oral, nonsteroidal aromatase inhibitor used in postmenopausal women with hormone receptor-positive breast cancer. By blocking the aromatase enzyme it suppresses peripheral conversion of adrenal androgens to estrogen, lowering serum estradiol by roughly 70% within 24 hours at the standard 1 mg daily dose. It is given in the adjuvant, first-line locally advanced/metastatic, and second-line advanced settings.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: selective nonsteroidal aromatase inhibitor that blocks conversion of adrenal androgens to estrone/estradiol in postmenopausal women, with no effect on adrenal corticosteroid or aldosterone synthesis [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=188c6fd5-6695-4d8b-af43-61d9c26abfe9).
- FDA-approved indications (postmenopausal women): adjuvant treatment of HR-positive early breast cancer; first-line treatment of HR-positive or unknown locally advanced/metastatic disease; second-line treatment of advanced disease progressing on tamoxifen [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=188c6fd5-6695-4d8b-af43-61d9c26abfe9).
- Pivotal ATAC trial (5-year analysis): anastrozole significantly prolonged disease-free survival vs tamoxifen as adjuvant therapy (HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.78-0.97, p=0.01) [ATAC 5-year](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15639680/).
- ATAC 10-year analysis: DFS benefit maintained overall (HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.83-0.99; p=0.04) and stronger in hormone-receptor-positive patients (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.78-0.95; p=0.003) [ATAC 10-year](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21087898/).
- Bone toxicity: decreased lumbar spine and total hip bone mineral density with increased fracture incidence (~10% vs ~7% with tamoxifen), warranting BMD monitoring [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=188c6fd5-6695-4d8b-af43-61d9c26abfe9).
- Cardiovascular/metabolic risks: increased ischemic cardiovascular events in women with pre-existing ischemic heart disease (17% vs 10% with tamoxifen) and elevated serum cholesterol (9% vs 3.5%) [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=188c6fd5-6695-4d8b-af43-61d9c26abfe9).
- Common adverse reactions (incidence ≥10%): hot flashes, arthralgia/arthritis, asthenia, hypertension, depression, nausea, rash, and back pain [FDA label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=188c6fd5-6695-4d8b-af43-61d9c26abfe9).
