# Cholangiocarcinoma

## Overview

Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) is an adenocarcinoma arising from the biliary epithelium, the most common biliary tract cancer, with a US incidence of roughly 1–2 per 100,000 per year. It is anatomically classified as intrahepatic (iCCA) or extrahepatic (perihilar and distal) disease, which differ in molecular profile and surgical approach. Resectability separates curative-intent surgery from palliative systemic therapy, and molecular profiling now gates a growing set of targeted options, making early biomarker testing essential in advanced disease.

## Key facts

- CCA is divided into intrahepatic (iCCA, ~10–20% of primary hepatic malignancies) and extrahepatic (perihilar and distal) subtypes; risk factors include primary sclerosing cholangitis, hepatolithiasis, choledochal cysts, and liver fluke infection [molecular profiling review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6642361/).
- Staging and resectability set treatment intent: complete surgical resection is the only curative option, while most patients present with unresectable or metastatic disease managed with palliative systemic therapy [molecular profiling review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6642361/).
- Actionable biomarkers cluster in iCCA — IDH1 mutations (~30%), FGFR2 fusions/rearrangements (~14%), plus BAP1 and ARID1A — whereas KRAS and SMAD4 alterations are more common in extrahepatic disease, justifying upfront molecular profiling [molecular profiling review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6642361/).
- First-line advanced disease: in TOPAZ-1, adding durvalumab to gemcitabine–cisplatin improved overall survival (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.66–0.97; updated median OS 12.9 vs 11.3 mo, HR 0.76) and is now a standard of care [TOPAZ-1 NEJM Evidence](https://evidence.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/EVIDoa2200015).
- IDH1-mutant disease after prior therapy: in the phase 3 ClarIDHy trial, ivosidenib improved PFS versus placebo (median 2.7 vs 1.4 mo, HR 0.37, p<0.0001), supporting its 2021 FDA approval [ClarIDHy FDA approval summary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9250596/).
- FGFR2 fusion/rearrangement disease after prior therapy: in FIGHT-202, pemigatinib produced an objective response rate of ~37% with median duration of response 7.5 mo, leading to FDA accelerated approval in April 2020 [FIGHT-202 final results](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11190465/).
- Treatment landscape by line: gemcitabine–cisplatin plus immunotherapy (durvalumab) front-line; second-line FOLFOX and biomarker-directed agents (ivosidenib for IDH1, FGFR inhibitors for FGFR2 fusions, plus HER2- and MSI-H/dMMR-directed therapy) define subsequent lines [molecular subtypes review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8269161/).
