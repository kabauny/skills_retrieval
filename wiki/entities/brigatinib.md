# Brigatinib

## Overview
Brigatinib (Alunbrig) is a next-generation oral tyrosine kinase inhibitor of anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) with activity against multiple resistance mutations and good CNS penetration. It is FDA-approved for ALK-positive metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), where the pivotal ALTA-1L trial established it as a frontline option superior to crizotinib. It is dosed at 180 mg once daily after a 7-day 90 mg lead-in intended to reduce early pulmonary events.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: tyrosine kinase inhibitor that blocks ALK, inhibiting ALK autophosphorylation and downstream signaling, with activity against several crizotinib-resistance mutations [FDA label](https://dailymed.awsprod.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=0fe9ff20-d402-41f3-bc1e-7002ea7007db&type=display).
- Indication: adult patients with ALK-positive metastatic NSCLC as detected by an FDA-approved test; the frontline indication was granted May 2020 [OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/fda-approves-brigatinib-for-frontline-alk-nsclc).
- Pivotal trial ALTA-1L (phase 3, n=275, brigatinib vs crizotinib in ALK-TKI-naive advanced NSCLC): median PFS 24.0 vs 11.1 months, HR 0.48 in the final analysis [ALTA-1L final](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)02398-4/fulltext).
- ALTA-1L durability: 3-year PFS 43% (brigatinib) vs 19% (crizotinib) at median 40.4-month follow-up [ALTA-1L final](https://www.jto.org/article/S1556-0864(21)02398-4/fulltext).
- Serious adverse event: interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis, which can be severe or fatal and may occur within the first week (5.1% in ALTA-1L), prompting the 90 mg lead-in dosing [FDA label](https://dailymed.awsprod.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=0fe9ff20-d402-41f3-bc1e-7002ea7007db&type=display).
- Other notable toxicities: hypertension (up to ~32%), bradycardia, and embryo-fetal toxicity; most common reactions (>=25%) include diarrhea, fatigue, nausea, rash, cough, and myalgia [FDA label](https://dailymed.awsprod.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=0fe9ff20-d402-41f3-bc1e-7002ea7007db&type=display).
- ALTA-1L second interim analysis (NEJM/JCO reporting) confirmed >50% reduction in risk of progression or death versus crizotinib, supporting frontline use [NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1810171).
