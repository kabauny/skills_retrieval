# Rituximab

## Overview

Rituximab is a chimeric murine/human IgG1 monoclonal antibody targeting CD20 on the surface of B lymphocytes. By depleting CD20-positive B cells, it became the first monoclonal antibody approved for cancer and transformed the treatment of B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma and chronic lymphocytic leukemia. It is given alone or, more commonly, combined with chemotherapy backbones such as CHOP.

## Key facts

- Mechanism: binds CD20 on B cells and depletes them via antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), phagocytosis (ADCP), and direct apoptosis induction [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2010/103705s5311lbl.pdf).
- Principal oncologic indications: CD20-positive non-Hodgkin lymphoma (including diffuse large B-cell and follicular lymphoma) and chronic lymphocytic leukemia, alone or with chemotherapy; also approved in granulomatosis with polyangiitis and microscopic polyangiitis [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2009/103705s5299lbl.pdf).
- Pivotal trial (GELA LNH-98.5, Coiffier et al., NEJM): in 399 elderly DLBCL patients, R-CHOP raised complete response from 63% to 76% (P=0.005) versus CHOP alone [GELA LNH-98.5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11807147/).
- In the same trial, R-CHOP significantly improved overall survival with a risk ratio for death of 0.64 (95% CI 0.45–0.89), an approximately 36% reduction, without significantly greater clinically relevant toxicity [GELA LNH-98.5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11807147/).
- The benefit was durable: with long-term follow-up R-CHOP improved overall survival by roughly 47% relative to CHOP alone, establishing R-CHOP as the DLBCL standard of care [CHOP plus rituximab approval](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/rituxan-chop-approved-diffuse-large-b-cell-nhl).
- Boxed warnings: fatal infusion reactions (about 80% occurring with the first infusion), tumor lysis syndrome, severe mucocutaneous reactions, and progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) from JC virus [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2009/103705s5299lbl.pdf).
- Hepatitis B virus reactivation, sometimes fulminant and fatal, can occur; screen all patients for HBV before initiating therapy and monitor during and after treatment [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2009/103705s5299lbl.pdf).
