# nivolumab

## Overview

Nivolumab (Opdivo) is a fully human IgG4 anti–PD-1 monoclonal antibody that restores antitumor T-cell activity by blocking the PD-1 immune checkpoint. First approved by the FDA in 2014, it is now indicated across many solid tumors—including melanoma, non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), renal cell carcinoma, urothelial carcinoma, and gastric/esophageal cancers—as monotherapy or in combination with ipilimumab or chemotherapy. A subcutaneous co-formulation with hyaluronidase (Opdivo Qvantig) has since been approved across solid tumor indications.

## Key facts

- Mechanism: binds the PD-1 receptor on T cells and blocks its interaction with ligands PD-L1 and PD-L2, releasing checkpoint inhibition and restoring antitumor immune response [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2022/125554s112lbl.pdf).
- Principal FDA indications include unresectable/metastatic melanoma (mono and with ipilimumab), metastatic NSCLC, advanced renal cell carcinoma, urothelial carcinoma, head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, and gastric/gastroesophageal/esophageal cancers [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2022/125554s112lbl.pdf).
- CheckMate 067 (advanced melanoma): at 5-year follow-up median OS was 36.9 mo with nivolumab vs 19.9 mo with ipilimumab (HR for death 0.63), and >60 mo with nivolumab+ipilimumab (HR 0.52) [CheckMate 067 NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1910836).
- CheckMate 057 (previously treated nonsquamous NSCLC): median OS 12.2 mo with nivolumab vs 9.4 mo with docetaxel (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.59–0.89) [CheckMate 057 ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/issues/november-25-2015/checkmate-057-nivolumab-improves-survival-vs-docetaxel-in-advanced-nonsquamous-non-small-cell-lung-cancer/).
- Pooled CheckMate 017/057 showed OS HR 0.70 (95% CI 0.61–0.81) for nivolumab vs docetaxel in previously treated NSCLC, with durable 5-year benefit [CheckMate 017/057 5-year ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/news/february-2021/five-year-outcomes-from-the-checkmate-017-and-057-trials-of-nivolumab-vs-docetaxel-in-previously-treated-patients-with-nsclc/).
- Notable toxicities are immune-related adverse events—rash, pruritus, hypothyroidism, and pneumonitis—plus serious immune-mediated colitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies, and nephritis; grade 3–5 events were far lower with nivolumab (10%) than docetaxel (54%) in NSCLC [CheckMate 017/057 PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29408986/).
- Combination with ipilimumab increases efficacy but also raises high-grade immune-related toxicity versus monotherapy [CheckMate 067 NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1910836).
