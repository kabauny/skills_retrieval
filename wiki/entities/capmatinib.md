# Capmatinib

## Overview
Capmatinib (Tabrecta) is an oral, selective MET tyrosine kinase inhibitor developed by Novartis. It is approved for metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) harboring a mutation that leads to MET exon 14 skipping. It blocks both wild-type and the dysregulated MET protein produced by exon 14 skipping, suppressing downstream oncogenic signaling.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: kinase inhibitor that targets MET, including the variant produced by MET exon 14 skipping, which lacks a regulatory domain and drives increased downstream MET signaling [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2020/213591s000lbl.pdf).
- Indication: adults with metastatic NSCLC whose tumors have a mutation leading to MET exon 14 skipping, detected by an FDA-approved test; accelerated approval May 2020, converted to regular approval August 2022 [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-capmatinib-metastatic-non-small-cell-lung-cancer).
- Recommended dose is 400 mg orally twice daily; FoundationOne CDx is the approved companion diagnostic [FDA approval summary](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34344795/).
- Pivotal trial GEOMETRY mono-1 (NCT02414139), phase 2: ORR 68% (95% CI 55.0–79.7) in treatment-naive patients with median DoR ~12.6 months [GEOMETRY mono-1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39362249/).
- In previously treated patients, ORR was 44% (95% CI 34.1–54.3) with median DoR ~9.7 months [GEOMETRY mono-1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39362249/).
- Most common adverse reactions: peripheral edema (~47%), nausea (~35%), increased creatinine (~21%), and vomiting (~20%) [GEOMETRY mono-1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39362249/).
- Serious warnings: interstitial lung disease/pneumonitis, hepatotoxicity (requiring serial liver-function monitoring), and photosensitivity; grade 3–4 events occurred in ~44%, with dyspnea the most frequent serious reaction [GEOMETRY mono-1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39362249/).
