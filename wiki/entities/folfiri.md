# FOLFIRI

## Overview

FOLFIRI is a combination chemotherapy regimen of folinic acid (leucovorin), 5-fluorouracil (5-FU), and irinotecan, used primarily as first- or second-line therapy for advanced or metastatic colorectal cancer. It is delivered intravenously on a biweekly (14-day) cycle and is commonly paired with a biologic (bevacizumab, cetuximab, or panitumumab) selected by RAS/BRAF status. Mechanistically, leucovorin stabilizes the 5-FU–thymidylate synthase complex to block DNA synthesis, while irinotecan's active metabolite SN-38 inhibits topoisomerase I to cause lethal DNA damage in dividing cells.

## Key facts

- Components: irinotecan 180 mg/m² + leucovorin 400 mg/m² + 5-FU 400 mg/m² bolus then 2400 mg/m² over 46h, every 2 weeks [FOLFIRI overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FOLFIRI).
- Irinotecan's SN-38 metabolite inhibits topoisomerase I; 5-FU/leucovorin inhibits thymidylate synthase to block DNA synthesis [Irinotecan PI, Drugs.com](https://www.drugs.com/pro/irinotecan.html).
- In FIRE-3 (phase 3, RAS wild-type subgroup, n=400), FOLFIRI + cetuximab gave median OS 33.1 vs 25.0 months for FOLFIRI + bevacizumab (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.54–0.90) [FIRE-3, Lancet Oncol/PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27575024/).
- In TRIBE (phase 3), FOLFIRI + bevacizumab gave median OS 25.8 months vs 29.8 months for the intensified FOLFOXIRI + bevacizumab triplet [TRIBE, Lancet Oncol](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanonc/article/PIIS1470-2045(15)00122-9/abstract).
- FOLFIRI + bevacizumab is an established first-line mCRC standard, with reported overall response rate ~53% and median PFS ~11 months [TRIBE, Lancet Oncol](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanonc/article/PIIS1470-2045(15)00122-9/abstract).
- Irinotecan carries a boxed warning for severe early (cholinergic, atropine-responsive) and life-threatening late diarrhea, plus severe myelosuppression [Irinotecan FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2022/020571Orig1s053lbl.pdf).
- UGT1A1*28/*6 poor metabolizers have elevated SN-38 exposure and increased risk of severe neutropenia; FDA advises dose reduction for UGT1A1*28 homozygotes [UGT1A1 testing guide, JCO Oncology Practice](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/OP.21.00624).
