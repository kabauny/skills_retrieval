# Vemurafenib

## Overview
Vemurafenib (Zelboraf) is an orally administered, ATP-competitive small-molecule inhibitor of the mutated BRAF V600 kinase. It was the first-in-class BRAF inhibitor, FDA-approved in 2011 for unresectable or metastatic melanoma harboring a BRAF V600E mutation. It is often combined with the MEK inhibitor cobimetinib to delay MAPK-pathway resistance, and is also approved for Erdheim-Chester disease.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: selectively inhibits the constitutively active mutant BRAF V600E kinase, blocking downstream MEK/ERK (MAPK) signaling and tumor proliferation; in BRAF wild-type cells it can paradoxically activate MAPK signaling [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2017/202429s012lbl.pdf).
- Principal FDA indication: unresectable or metastatic melanoma with a BRAF V600E mutation as detected by an FDA-approved test; also approved for Erdheim-Chester disease [FDA approval summary](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25096067/).
- Pivotal trial BRIM-3 (phase 3 vs dacarbazine): interim analysis showed OS HR 0.37 and PFS HR 0.26 favoring vemurafenib [BRIM-3 NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1103782).
- BRIM-3 final analysis: median OS 13.6 vs 9.7 months (HR 0.81, 95% CI 0.67-0.98, P=0.03), with curves converging after ~3 years due to dacarbazine-to-vemurafenib crossover [BRIM-3 final OS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5834156/).
- Common adverse events (>=30%): arthralgia (68%), fatigue (57%), rash (54%), photosensitivity (52%), nausea (42%), alopecia (38%), and skin papilloma (31%) [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK535429/).
- Serious toxicities: cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma / keratoacanthoma (up to ~25%), QTc prolongation, severe hypersensitivity and skin reactions (including Stevens-Johnson syndrome), and ocular toxicity [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2017/202429s012lbl.pdf).
- Combination with cobimetinib (MEK inhibitor) targets the MAPK pathway at two nodes to delay resistance; the doublet adds diarrhea, liver-enzyme abnormalities, and increased photosensitivity [coBRIM phase 1b](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25037139/).
