# Eribulin

## Overview
Eribulin (eribulin mesylate, Halaven) is a synthetic analogue of the marine natural product halichondrin B and a non-taxane microtubule dynamics inhibitor. It is given intravenously for heavily pretreated metastatic breast cancer and for advanced/unresectable liposarcoma after prior anthracycline therapy. In both settings it demonstrated a significant overall survival benefit in randomized phase 3 trials, with myelosuppression and peripheral neuropathy as its principal toxicities.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: binds the plus (growth) ends of microtubules and inhibits microtubule growth without affecting shortening, sequestering tubulin into nonproductive aggregates and producing irreversible G2/M mitotic arrest and apoptosis — distinct from taxanes and vinca alkaloids [Targeted Oncology](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/new-mechanism-of-action-prompts-renewed-interest-in-eribulin).
- Indication 1: metastatic breast cancer in patients who previously received at least two chemotherapy regimens for metastatic disease, including an anthracycline and a taxane [NCI](https://www.cancer.gov/types/breast/research/eribulin-improves-survival).
- Indication 2: advanced or unresectable liposarcoma after a prior anthracycline-based regimen — the first agent FDA-approved for liposarcoma on the basis of a survival benefit [ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/issues/may-25-2016/eribulin-in-liposarcoma-a-closer-look-at-the-comparative-data/).
- Pivotal trial (breast): the phase 3 EMBRACE study showed median OS 13.1 vs 10.6 months versus treatment of physician's choice (HR 0.81, 95% CI 0.66–0.99; p=0.041) [EMBRACE (Lancet 2011)](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(11)60070-6/abstract).
- Pivotal trial (sarcoma): the phase 3 Study 309 of eribulin vs dacarbazine improved OS in the overall population; in the preplanned liposarcoma subgroup median OS was 15.6 vs 8.4 months (HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.35–0.75) [Study 309 (Lancet 2016)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26874885/).
- Notable adverse events: neutropenia (most common grade 3/4 toxicity), fatigue, alopecia, and nausea; peripheral neuropathy is dose-limiting (grade 3 in ~8%, grade 4 ~0.4%) and the leading reason for discontinuation [JCO liposarcoma subanalysis](https://ascopubs.org/doi/full/10.1200/JCO.2016.71.6605).
