# Enasidenib

## Overview
Enasidenib (brand name IDHIFA) is an oral, small-molecule inhibitor of mutant isocitrate dehydrogenase-2 (IDH2). Mutant IDH2 produces the oncometabolite 2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG), which causes DNA/histone hypermethylation and a block in myeloid differentiation; enasidenib inhibits the mutant enzyme, lowers 2-HG, and promotes differentiation of leukemic blasts. It was granted FDA approval in August 2017 for relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukemia (AML) bearing an IDH2 mutation, the first targeted agent approved for this population.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: targeted inhibitor of mutant IDH2 enzymes (R140 and R172 variants), reducing oncometabolite 2-hydroxyglutarate and relieving the differentiation block in myeloid precursors; IDH2 mutations occur in roughly 8-19% of AML [Blood phase 1/2](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/130/6/722/36814/Enasidenib-in-mutant-IDH2-relapsed-or-refractory).
- FDA-approved indication: adults with relapsed or refractory IDH2-mutated AML (detected by an FDA-approved test), at a recommended dose of 100 mg orally once daily until progression or unacceptable toxicity [FDA approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-granted-regular-approval-enasidenib-treatment-relapsed-or-refractory-aml).
- Pivotal trial: Study AG221-C-001 (NCT01915498), an open-label single-arm phase 1/2 study in 199 relapsed/refractory IDH2-mutant AML patients, showing a complete remission (CR) rate of about 19% with a median CR duration of ~8.2 months [FDA approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-granted-regular-approval-enasidenib-treatment-relapsed-or-refractory-aml).
- Phase 3 confirmatory IDHENTIFY trial (NCT02577406) in older late-stage R/R patients missed its primary endpoint: median OS 6.5 vs 6.2 months vs conventional care (HR 0.86, P=.23), though ORR (40.5% vs 9.9%) and event-free survival (4.9 vs 2.6 mo) favored enasidenib [Blood IDHENTIFY](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/141/2/156/485601/Enasidenib-vs-conventional-care-in-older-patients).
- Boxed warning: differentiation syndrome, which occurred in ~14% of treated patients and can be life-threatening or fatal if untreated; managed with corticosteroids and hemodynamic monitoring [FDA safety communication](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-warns-symptoms-serious-condition-affecting-blood-cells-are-not-being-recognized-leukemia).
- Other notable adverse events: leukocytosis, tumor lysis syndrome, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, decreased appetite, and elevated bilirubin/hyperbilirubinemia [FDA approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-granted-regular-approval-enasidenib-treatment-relapsed-or-refractory-aml).
