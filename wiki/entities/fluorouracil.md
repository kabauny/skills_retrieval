# Fluorouracil

## Overview

Fluorouracil (5-FU) is a fluoropyrimidine antimetabolite that has served as a backbone of cytotoxic chemotherapy for gastrointestinal and other solid tumors for decades. Administered systemically (almost always with leucovorin to potentiate activity), it is a core component of regimens such as FOLFOX, FOLFIRI, and FOLFIRINOX. A topical 5% formulation is used for actinic keratoses and superficial basal cell carcinoma.

## Key facts

- Mechanism: its active metabolite FdUMP forms a stable ternary complex with thymidylate synthase (potentiated by the folate cofactor from leucovorin), depleting dTMP and blocking DNA synthesis; additional metabolites misincorporate into RNA and DNA, disrupting their processing and function [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK549808/).
- Systemic FDA-approved indications include colorectal, gastric, pancreatic, and breast adenocarcinoma; topical 5-FU is approved for multiple actinic/solar keratoses and superficial basal cell carcinoma [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK549808/).
- In the pivotal MOSAIC trial (2,246 stage II/III colon cancer patients), adding oxaliplatin to infusional 5-FU/leucovorin (FOLFOX4 vs LV5FU2) improved 5-year disease-free survival in stage III disease to 73.3% vs 67.4% (HR 0.80, p=.003) [MOSAIC](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19451431/).
- MOSAIC also showed an overall survival benefit in stage III disease: 6-year OS 72.9% vs 68.7% (HR 0.80, p=.023), establishing 5-FU/oxaliplatin as standard adjuvant therapy [MOSAIC](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19451431/).
- At 10-year follow-up the OS benefit persisted across all patients: 71.7% (FOLFOX4) vs 67.1% (5-FU/leucovorin), HR 0.85, p=.043 [ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/News/34054).
- Common toxicities: mucositis/stomatitis, diarrhea, nausea, myelosuppression (leukopenia), and hand-foot syndrome (notably with continuous infusion) [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK549808/).
- Serious/life-threatening toxicities: cardiotoxicity (angina, MI, heart failure — greater with infusional dosing), neurotoxicity (cerebellar syndrome, encephalopathy), and severe toxicity in patients with dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) deficiency, who clear the drug poorly [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK549808/).
