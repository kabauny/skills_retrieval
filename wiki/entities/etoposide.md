# Etoposide

## Overview
Etoposide is a topoisomerase II inhibitor (epipodophyllotoxin) used as a cytotoxic backbone in several cancers. It is FDA-approved for first-line small cell lung cancer (SCLC) in combination with cisplatin and for refractory testicular tumors in combination with other agents. A platinum-etoposide doublet remains the chemotherapy foundation of SCLC, now combined with PD-L1 blockade in the extensive-stage setting.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: stabilizes (poisons) the topoisomerase II–DNA cleavage complex, preventing DNA re-ligation and producing double-strand breaks that trigger cell death, with arrest primarily at the late S/G2 phase [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2019/020457s019lbl.pdf).
- Principal FDA-approved indications: refractory testicular tumors (in combination with other chemotherapy) and small cell lung cancer as first-line therapy in combination with cisplatin [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2019/020457s019lbl.pdf).
- In testicular cancer, platinum-etoposide–based combination regimens achieve roughly 80% response rates, making etoposide a core component of curative-intent therapy [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557864/).
- Pivotal trial — IMpower133: adding atezolizumab to carboplatin plus etoposide in first-line extensive-stage SCLC improved overall survival (median OS 12.3 vs 10.3 months; HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.54–0.91; P = 0.007) [IMpower133/NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1809064).
- Updated IMpower133 analysis confirmed durable benefit of the etoposide-containing regimen with atezolizumab over chemotherapy alone in extensive-stage SCLC [IMpower133 update/JCO](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.20.01055).
- Dose-limiting toxicity is myelosuppression (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia), and the drug carries risk of secondary acute myeloid leukemia, frequently with 11q23 (MLL) translocations [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557864/).
- Other notable adverse events include infusion-related hypersensitivity/anaphylactic reactions (flushing, bronchospasm, hypotension), alopecia, and gastrointestinal toxicity (nausea, vomiting, stomatitis) [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557864/).
