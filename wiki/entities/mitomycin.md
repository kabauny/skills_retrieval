# Mitomycin C

## Overview
Mitomycin C is an alkylating antitumor antibiotic that, after intracellular reductive activation, cross-links complementary DNA strands to inhibit DNA synthesis. It has long-standing systemic indications in metastatic gastric and pancreatic adenocarcinoma (in combination regimens) and is widely used as intravesical therapy for non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer. A reverse-thermal hydrogel formulation (UGN-101/Jelmyto) was FDA-approved in 2020 for low-grade upper tract urothelial carcinoma.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: a cell-cycle non-specific alkylating agent that, after reductive activation, cross-links guanine/cytosine on complementary DNA strands to inhibit DNA synthesis and at high doses also suppresses RNA and protein synthesis [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK562249/).
- FDA-approved for systemic treatment of metastatic adenocarcinoma of the stomach or pancreas in combination with other chemotherapy agents [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK562249/).
- Used as intravesical (bladder instillation) therapy for non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer, where maintenance dosing reduces recurrence of low- and intermediate-risk tumors [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK562249/).
- Jelmyto (UGN-101), a mitomycin reverse-thermal gel that becomes semisolid at body temperature to prolong urinary dwell time, was FDA-approved in 2020 for low-grade upper tract urothelial carcinoma [Jelmyto FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/211728s010lbl.pdf).
- Pivotal OLYMPUS trial (phase 3, single-arm, primary chemoablation of low-grade UTUC): initial complete response rate 59% (42/71; 95% CI 47-71), with Kaplan-Meier estimated 12-month durability of 82% among complete responders [OLYMPUS final report](https://www.auajournals.org/doi/10.1097/JU.0000000000002350).
- Most common serious systemic toxicity is cumulative, dose-related myelosuppression (thrombocytopenia and leukopenia), which is permanent in roughly 25% of episodes [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK562249/).
- Hemolytic uremic syndrome (microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, irreversible renal failure) occurs in under 15% of systemically treated patients and carries high mortality; pulmonary toxicity is also notable [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK562249/).
- With Jelmyto intracavitary use, ureteric stenosis is the most frequent adverse event (about 44% in OLYMPUS) [Jelmyto FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/211728s010lbl.pdf).
