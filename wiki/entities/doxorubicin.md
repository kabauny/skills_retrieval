# doxorubicin

## Overview

Doxorubicin (Adriamycin) is an anthracycline cytotoxic antibiotic used as a backbone agent across many hematologic and solid tumors. It is a cornerstone of curative-intent combination regimens such as ABVD (Hodgkin lymphoma) and R-CHOP (diffuse large B-cell lymphoma) and is also used in breast cancer, sarcoma, and other malignancies. Its clinical use is limited primarily by cumulative dose-dependent cardiotoxicity and myelosuppression.

## Key facts

- Mechanism: intercalates into DNA base pairs and inhibits topoisomerase II, causing DNA strand breaks and blocking DNA/RNA synthesis; iron-dependent free-radical generation contributes to both cytotoxicity and cardiotoxicity [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459232/).
- FDA-approved indications span breast cancer (adjuvant, node-positive), metastatic soft tissue and bone sarcoma, ovarian and bladder carcinoma, acute lymphoblastic and myeloblastic leukemia, Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma, small cell/bronchogenic lung carcinoma, and gastric carcinoma [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2010/050467s070lbl.pdf).
- In advanced classical Hodgkin lymphoma, doxorubicin is the "A" of the standard ABVD regimen, delivering roughly 70% long-term tumor control and validated against escalated BEACOPP in the RATHL trial [RATHL analysis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7960595/).
- As the anthracycline in R-CHOP (50 mg/m2 dose), it underpins the standard first-line DLBCL regimen; the pivotal GELA LNH-98.5 trial showed R-CHOP improved 10-year overall survival to 43.5% vs 27.6% for CHOP alone [GELA LNH-98.5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2951853/).
- Dose-limiting toxicities are myelosuppression and cumulative dose-dependent cardiomyopathy (boxed warning); congestive heart failure carries ~50% 1-year mortality, prompting LVEF monitoring before and during therapy [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459232/).
- Maximum cumulative dose is generally capped near 550 mg/m2, though cardiotoxicity can occur at lower doses; other serious risks include severe extravasation tissue necrosis and secondary AML/MDS [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459232/).
