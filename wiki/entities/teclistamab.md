# Teclistamab

## Overview
Teclistamab (Tecvayli) is a humanized IgG4 bispecific T-cell engager that simultaneously binds B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA) on malignant plasma cells and CD3 on T cells, redirecting T-cell cytotoxicity against myeloma cells. It was the first BCMA-directed bispecific antibody approved for multiple myeloma, receiving FDA accelerated approval in October 2022. It is given as weekly subcutaneous dosing after a step-up regimen designed to mitigate cytokine release syndrome.

## Key facts
- Mechanism: BCMA × CD3 bispecific antibody that physically bridges BCMA-expressing myeloma cells and T cells, driving T-cell-mediated lysis of plasma cells [[FDA approval](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39412823/)].
- FDA-granted accelerated approval on October 25, 2022 for adults with relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma after ≥4 prior lines including a proteasome inhibitor, an immunomodulatory agent, and an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody [FDA approval summary](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39412823/).
- Pivotal trial MajesTEC-1 (phase 1/2, single-arm, 165 triple-class-exposed patients) showed an overall response rate of 63.0% with 39.4% achieving complete response or better [MajesTEC-1 (NEJM)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2203478).
- In MajesTEC-1, median duration of response was 18.4 months and median progression-free survival was 11.3 months [MajesTEC-1 (NEJM)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2203478).
- Cytokine release syndrome occurred in 72.1% of patients (grade 3, 0.6%; no grade 4); all CRS events resolved with no discontinuations for CRS [MajesTEC-1 (NEJM)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2203478).
- Neurotoxic events occurred in 14.5%, including ICANS in 3.0% (all grade 1-2); the drug carries boxed warnings for CRS and neurologic toxicity and is available only through a REMS program [MajesTEC-1 (NEJM)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2203478).
- Hematologic and infectious toxicity is prominent: neutropenia (70.9%; grade 3/4 64.2%), anemia (52.1%), and infections (76.4%; grade 3/4 44.8%) [MajesTEC-1 (NEJM)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2203478).
- More recently the MajesTEC-3 trial supported teclistamab plus daratumumab in earlier relapse (after ≥1 prior line), expanding its role in the treatment landscape [MajesTEC-3](https://oncodaily.com/techology/majestec-3468305).
