# Non-small cell lung cancer

## Overview

Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for roughly 85% of all lung cancers and is the leading cause of cancer death worldwide. Management is driven by histology, stage (AJCC 8th/9th edition), and an expanding panel of molecular biomarkers that determine eligibility for targeted therapy and immunotherapy. Comprehensive biomarker testing and PD-L1 immunohistochemistry at diagnosis are now standard of care for advanced disease and increasingly inform early-stage treatment.

## Key facts

- NSCLC comprises ~85% of lung cancers; adenocarcinoma is the most common subtype (~40% of all lung cancers), followed by squamous cell carcinoma and large cell carcinoma — adenocarcinoma is typically peripheral while squamous cell carcinoma is typically central [Lung Adenocarcinoma StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519578/).
- Staging (AJCC v9, effective 2024) frames intent: early-stage (I–II and selected IIIA) is treated with curative-intent resection ± perioperative systemic therapy; unresectable stage III is treated with definitive chemoradiation; stage IV is treated with systemic therapy [ACS NSCLC staging](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/lung-cancer/detection-diagnosis-staging/staging-nsclc.html).
- NCCN recommends broad molecular profiling at diagnosis covering EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, KRAS G12C, MET exon 14 skipping, RET, NTRK1/2/3, and HER2 (ERBB2), plus PD-L1 IHC — each gating a distinct therapy [NCCN NSCLC v4.2024](https://jnccn.org/view/journals/jnccn/22/4/article-p249.xml?print=).
- EGFR (exon 19 del / L858R) gates first-line osimertinib, which extended PFS to 18.9 vs 10.2 months over earlier TKIs and is also adjuvant standard (ADAURA: ~83% reduction in recurrence/death risk in resected stage IB–IIIA EGFR-mutant disease); ALK rearrangement gates first-line alectinib and other next-gen ALK TKIs [NCCN NSCLC v4.2024](https://jnccn.org/view/journals/jnccn/22/4/article-p249.xml?print=).
- ROS1 fusions, BRAF V600E, MET exon 14 skipping, RET rearrangements, NTRK fusions, and HER2 mutations each gate their own approved targeted agents in the first-line or later setting; KRAS G12C (~14% of NSCLC, ~50% of KRAS mutations) gates second-line sotorasib (FDA-approved May 2021), though these patients are typically treated first-line with immunotherapy-based regimens [Targeted Therapies in NSCLC OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/targeted-therapies-continue-to-expand-the-treatment-paradigm-in-nsclc).
- PD-L1 IHC stratifies immunotherapy: tumor proportion score ≥50% supports single-agent anti–PD-1/PD-L1 (e.g., pembrolizumab), while any PD-L1 level or higher disease burden supports chemo-immunotherapy combinations in driver-negative disease [Combinations targeting EGFR/KRAS G12C OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/combinations-targeting-egfr-and-kras-g12c-may-advance-nsclc-treatment).
- Treatment landscape by stage/line: resectable disease uses surgery with neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy and/or adjuvant atezolizumab/pembrolizumab (or adjuvant osimertinib if EGFR-mutant); unresectable stage III uses chemoradiation followed by consolidation durvalumab (PACIFIC: median OS 47.5 vs 29.1 months); stage IV is matched to biomarker — targeted TKI for an actionable driver, otherwise immunotherapy ± platinum-doublet chemotherapy [Durvalumab/Osimertinib OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/durvalumab-osimertinib-envelop-adjuvant-and-neoadjuvant-nsclc-treatment).
