# cisplatin

## Overview

Cisplatin is a platinum-based, cell-cycle-nonspecific cytotoxic agent that forms DNA crosslinks to trigger tumor cell death. First approved in 1978, it became the foundational drug that transformed metastatic testicular cancer from largely incurable into a curable disease, and it remains a backbone of combination chemotherapy across many solid tumors. Its clinical use is defined by potent activity offset by substantial nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, neurotoxicity, and emetogenicity.

## Key facts

- Mechanism: cisplatin enters cells and forms covalent platinum–DNA adducts on guanine and adenine, producing intra- and inter-strand crosslinks that distort DNA, halt replication/transcription, and drive apoptosis; it acts as a cell-cycle-nonspecific bifunctional alkylating-like agent [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK547695/).
- Principal FDA-approved indications are advanced (metastatic) testicular cancer, advanced ovarian cancer, and advanced/transitional-cell bladder carcinoma, typically in combination regimens [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK547695/).
- Pivotal trial — Einhorn & Donohue (1977): cisplatin + vinblastine + bleomycin in 50 men with disseminated testicular cancer produced 74% complete and 26% partial remissions, with ~85% rendered disease-free after surgical resection of residual disease [Einhorn 1977 (Ann Intern Med)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/71004/).
- Historic efficacy magnitude: prior therapy cured only ~5% of disseminated testicular cancer, whereas cisplatin-based combinations push cure rates above 90%, and the U.S. testicular cancer death rate has fallen by roughly two-thirds since 1975 [NCI](https://www.cancer.gov/research/progress/discovery/cisplatin).
- Dose-limiting nephrotoxicity (acute renal failure, tubular damage) is the signature toxicity and is mitigated by aggressive pre/post-treatment IV hydration and forced diuresis [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK547695/).
- Ototoxicity (high-frequency hearing loss, tinnitus) and dose-dependent peripheral neuropathy can be cumulative and irreversible even after stopping therapy [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK547695/).
- Other serious adverse effects include severe nausea/vomiting (highly emetogenic; requires prophylactic antiemetics), myelosuppression with infection risk, electrolyte wasting (hypomagnesemia), and anaphylactic-like hypersensitivity reactions [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK547695/).
