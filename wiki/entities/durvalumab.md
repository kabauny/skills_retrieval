# Durvalumab

## Overview

Durvalumab (Imfinzi) is a human IgG1 kappa monoclonal antibody PD-L1 inhibitor that releases the brakes on T-cell-mediated antitumor immunity. It is approved across several tumor types, most notably as consolidation therapy in stage III unresectable NSCLC after chemoradiation, and in combination with chemotherapy for extensive-stage small cell lung cancer and biliary tract cancer. It is also used with tremelimumab in hepatocellular carcinoma and, more recently, in combination with BCG for high-risk non-muscle invasive bladder cancer.

## Key facts

- Mechanism: human IgG1κ monoclonal antibody that binds PD-L1 and blocks its interaction with PD-1 and CD80 (B7.1), restoring T-cell antitumor activity; Fc-engineered to reduce ADCC/complement-dependent cytotoxicity ([FDA approval — biliary tract cancer](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-durvalumab-locally-advanced-or-metastatic-biliary-tract-cancer)).
- PACIFIC (stage III unresectable NSCLC consolidation after chemoradiation): primary OS HR 0.68 (95% CI 0.53–0.87; p=0.00251); updated median OS 47.5 vs 29.1 months, with 4-year OS 49.6% vs 36.3% for placebo ([PACIFIC, NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1809697)).
- CASPIAN (first-line extensive-stage SCLC, with etoposide + carbo/cisplatin): OS HR 0.73 (95% CI 0.59–0.91; p=0.0047), median OS 13.0 vs 10.3 months vs chemotherapy alone ([CASPIAN 3-year update](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35279527/)).
- TOPAZ-1 (advanced biliary tract cancer, with gemcitabine + cisplatin): OS HR 0.80 (95% CI 0.66–0.97; p=0.021) at interim, HR 0.76 (95% CI 0.64–0.91) on updated analysis ([TOPAZ-1, NEJM Evidence](https://evidence.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/EVIDoa2200015)).
- FDA-approved indications include unresectable stage III NSCLC after chemoradiation, first-line extensive-stage SCLC, and locally advanced/metastatic biliary tract cancer (each via its pivotal phase 3 trial) ([FDA — biliary tract cancer approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-durvalumab-locally-advanced-or-metastatic-biliary-tract-cancer)).
- Toxicity is dominated by immune-mediated adverse reactions (pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies, nephritis, dermatologic and infusion reactions); pneumonitis is a particular concern after thoracic chemoradiation in the PACIFIC setting ([PACIFIC, NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1809697)).
