# carboplatin

## Overview

Carboplatin is a second-generation platinum-based alkylating chemotherapy agent (FDA-approved in 1989) that binds DNA to form intra- and interstrand crosslinks, blocking replication and triggering apoptosis. It is FDA-indicated for advanced ovarian carcinoma, both as first-line combination therapy and as palliative monotherapy after prior chemotherapy, and is widely used off-label across lung, breast, and other solid tumors. Compared with cisplatin it is less nephrotoxic, neurotoxic, and emetogenic, but its dose-limiting toxicity is thrombocytopenia.

## Key facts

- Mechanism: platinum compound that covalently binds DNA forming predominantly intrastrand crosslinks/adducts, preventing repair and replication and causing cell death [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2012/077139Orig1s016lbl.pdf).
- Principal FDA indication: initial combination treatment and secondary (palliative) single-agent treatment of advanced ovarian carcinoma [Carboplatin - NCI](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/drugs/carboplatin).
- Dosing uses the Calvert formula — Total Dose (mg) = Target AUC x (GFR + 25) — individualizing exposure by renal function rather than body surface area [Carboplatin FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2012/077139Orig1s016lbl.pdf).
- Pivotal GOG-158 (Ozols, 808 optimal stage III patients): carboplatin (AUC 6) + paclitaxel was non-inferior to cisplatin + paclitaxel — median PFS 22.0 vs 21.7 months, relative risk of progression 0.91 (95% CI 0.76–1.10), with less toxicity [GOG-158](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/gog-158-study-sets-standard-care-stage-iii-ovarian-cancer).
- Pivotal ICON3 (2074 patients): paclitaxel + carboplatin vs standard platinum chemotherapy showed median OS 38.7 vs 36.0 months, OS HR 0.82 (95% CI 0.69–0.97, p=0.02) [ICON3 trial](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(02)11984-2/fulltext).
- Dose-limiting toxicity is myelosuppression, especially thrombocytopenia; risk rises sharply with creatinine clearance <60 mL/min (~25% severe cytopenias) [Carboplatin FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2012/077139Orig1s016lbl.pdf).
- Other serious adverse events: hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis (more common after prior platinum exposure), nephrotoxicity at high doses, and significant nausea/vomiting requiring antiemetic prophylaxis [Carboplatin AUC dosing/safety](https://pharmacyfreak.com/carboplatin-auc-dose-calculator-calvert/).
