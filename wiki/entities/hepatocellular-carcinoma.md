# Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)

## Overview
Hepatocellular carcinoma is the dominant primary liver malignancy and a leading cause of cancer death worldwide, arising almost always on a background of chronic liver disease and cirrhosis. Unlike most solid tumors, management is driven jointly by tumor burden and underlying liver function, formalized in the Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) staging system. Early-stage disease is potentially curable with resection, ablation, or transplantation, while intermediate and advanced disease is treated with locoregional or systemic therapy, where immunotherapy combinations have become first-line standard of care.

## Key facts
- HCC accounts for the large majority of primary liver cancers and is among the leading causes of cancer-related death globally; principal risk factors are cirrhosis from chronic HBV or HCV infection, alcohol-associated liver disease, and metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), with a shift toward non-viral etiologies in Western countries [HCC epidemiology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41571-023-00825-3).
- BCLC stage integrates tumor extent, performance status, and liver function (Child-Pugh); curative intent (resection, ablation, transplantation) is reserved for very early/early stage (0–A) disease with preserved hepatic reserve, while intermediate (B) is treated with TACE and advanced (C) with systemic therapy [NCCN HCC guidelines](https://www.nccn.org/guidelines/guidelines-detail?category=1&id=1514).
- Liver transplantation (within Milan criteria) and surgical resection or ablation offer cure, but 60–80% of patients recur after resection/ablation, and TACE is used both as definitive intermediate-stage therapy and as a bridge to transplant [Locoregional therapy in HCC](https://www.onclive.com/view/nccn-guideline-expands-locoregional-treatment-candidates-in-hcc).
- First-line standard for advanced HCC is atezolizumab (anti–PD-L1) plus bevacizumab (anti-VEGF): the phase III IMbrave150 trial showed median OS 19.2 vs 13.4 months versus sorafenib (HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.52–0.85) [IMbrave150](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34902530/).
- Dual immunotherapy with the STRIDE regimen (single priming dose of tremelimumab [anti–CTLA-4] plus durvalumab [anti–PD-L1]) is an alternative first-line option: phase III HIMALAYA showed median OS 16.43 vs 13.77 months versus sorafenib (HR 0.78, 96.02% CI 0.65–0.93) [HIMALAYA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38319892/).
- After progression, multikinase inhibitors extend survival: regorafenib (RESORCE, OS 10.6 vs 7.8 mo, HR 0.63) and cabozantinib (CELESTIAL, OS 10.2 vs 8.0 mo, HR 0.76) versus placebo [Second-line HCC therapy](https://decisionpoint.medscape.com/oncology/viewarticle/936455).
- Serum alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) is a key biomarker: ramucirumab (anti-VEGFR2) showed benefit in REACH-2 specifically in patients with AFP ≥400 ng/mL (OS 8.5 vs 7.3 mo), making AFP a biomarker that gates that therapy [REACH-2 / second-line](https://www.onclive.com/view/treatment-options-for-advanced-hcc-in-the-second-line-and-beyond).
