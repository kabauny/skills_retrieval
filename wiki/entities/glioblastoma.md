# Glioblastoma

## Overview

Glioblastoma is the most common and most aggressive primary malignant brain tumor in adults, a diffuse astrocytic glioma classified as WHO grade 4. Under the 2021 WHO CNS classification it is defined as an IDH-wildtype, adult-type diffuse astrocytoma carrying a histopathologic or molecular hallmark (microvascular proliferation, necrosis, TERT promoter mutation, EGFR amplification, or +7/-10 copy-number changes). Prognosis remains poor, with median overall survival of roughly 14-16 months despite multimodal therapy. Treatment intent is rarely curative; maximal safe resection followed by chemoradiation aims to prolong survival and control symptoms.

## Key facts

- Most common primary malignant brain tumor in adults; age-adjusted incidence ~3.2 per 100,000/year in the US, median age at diagnosis in the early-to-mid 60s, with a male predominance (~1.6:1) [Epidemiology and Outcome of Glioblastoma](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470003/).
- The 2021 WHO classification defines glioblastoma as IDH-wildtype; IDH-mutant grade 4 astrocytomas are now a biologically distinct entity, making IDH status the gatekeeping molecular diagnostic test [Updates in IDH-Wildtype Glioblastoma](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9154038/).
- MGMT promoter methylation (present in ~40% of IDH-wildtype tumors) is the key predictive/prognostic biomarker, conferring greater sensitivity to alkylating chemotherapy and guiding temozolomide use [Quantitative Analysis of MGMT Methylation Status](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9264886/).
- Diagnosis and staging hinge on maximal safe surgical resection rather than TNM staging; extent of resection plus molecular profiling set the treatment plan, but cure is not achievable and intent is to prolong survival [Glioblastoma Overview Review](https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/26/24/12162).
- First-line standard of care (Stupp regimen): radiotherapy with concomitant and adjuvant temozolomide improved median OS to 14.6 vs 12.1 months and 2-year survival to 26.5% vs 10.4% (HR for death 0.63, p<0.001) [Stupp NEJM 2005](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa043330).
- Adding Tumor Treating Fields (Optune) to maintenance temozolomide (EF-14 trial) extended median OS by ~5 months and improved 5-year survival to 13% vs 5%; NCCN gives it a category 1 recommendation in newly diagnosed disease [EF-14 post hoc analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6009218/).
- At recurrence, options are limited; bevacizumab (alone or with cytotoxic chemotherapy) is the most frequently used systemic agent, alongside re-resection or re-irradiation in selected patients [EF-14 recurrence analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6009218/).
- Hematologic toxicity from concomitant temozolomide is modest (grade 3/4 in ~7% of patients), making chemoradiation broadly tolerable in fit patients [Stupp NEJM 2005](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa043330).
