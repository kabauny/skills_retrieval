# Bacillus Calmette-Guérin

## Overview

Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) is a live attenuated strain of *Mycobacterium bovis* administered intravesically as an immunotherapy for non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC). It is FDA-approved for treatment of carcinoma in situ (CIS) and for prophylaxis of recurrent stage Ta/T1 papillary tumors after transurethral resection (TURBT). Standard induction is 6 weekly instillations, often followed by maintenance, and BCG has been the standard of care for high-risk NMIBC for decades.

## Key facts

- BCG is a live attenuated *M. bovis* strain; its antitumor effect is T-lymphocyte dependent, producing a local granulomatous inflammatory response in the bladder urothelium and lamina propria ([Medscape overview](https://emedicine.medscape.com/article/1950803-overview)).
- FDA-approved for intravesical treatment and prophylaxis of CIS and prophylaxis of recurrent Ta/T1 papillary NMIBC after TURBT; it is the AUA-endorsed standard for high-risk NMIBC ([TICE BCG label, Merck PI](https://www.merck.com/product/usa/pi_circulars/t/tice_bcg/ticebcg_pi.pdf)).
- In the pivotal SWOG-8507 trial, maintenance BCG improved 5-year recurrence-free survival to 60% vs 41% with induction alone (p<0.0001), with median disease-free survival of 77 vs 36 months ([SWOG-8507, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10668417/)).
- SWOG-8507 also improved 5-year worsening-free survival (76% vs 70%, p<0.04); overall survival favored maintenance but was not significant (83% vs 78%, p=0.08) ([SWOG-8507, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10668417/)).
- Long-term follow-up confirms a durable, statistically significant reduction in recurrence and progression with BCG, yet up to ~40% of NMIBC patients ultimately fail BCG therapy ([Intravesical BCG long-term outcomes, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6510863/)).
- Most common adverse events are local: BCG-induced cystitis (~35%), urinary frequency, hematuria, plus flu-like systemic symptoms (malaise, fever, chills) typically lasting 1–2 days ([Managing AEs of intravesical BCG, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4630183/)).
- The most serious complication is disseminated BCG sepsis (~0.3% incidence) with associated mortality; the TICE label carries a boxed warning, and instillation should be avoided with active bleeding or within ~1 week of TURBT ([TICE BCG label, FDA](https://www.fda.gov/media/76396/download)).
- BCG combinations are under active study, including with checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., durvalumab) to improve outcomes in high-risk NMIBC ([Avelumab+BCG protocol, ClinicalTrials.gov](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/42/NCT03892642/Prot_000.pdf)).
