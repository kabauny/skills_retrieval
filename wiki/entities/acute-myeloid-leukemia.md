# Acute myeloid leukemia (AML)

## Overview
Acute myeloid leukemia is an aggressive clonal malignancy of myeloid progenitors marked by rapid accumulation of immature blasts in marrow and blood. It is predominantly a disease of older adults (median age ~68 years) with historically poor outcomes (5-year OS ~24% overall, far lower in the elderly). Management hinges on rapid genetic risk stratification and on patient fitness, which separates candidates for intensive induction from those treated with lower-intensity regimens.

## Key facts
- Incidence is roughly 4.3 per 100,000 per year, rising sharply with age (~20 per 100,000 in those ≥65 vs ~2 per 100,000 in younger adults); median age at diagnosis is ~68 years and ~75% of patients are ≥55 years [SEER/epidemiology review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0268960X18301395).
- Genetic risk is stratified by the ELN 2022 system into favorable, intermediate, and adverse groups, which guides intensity and the decision to pursue allogeneic transplant; ELN-2022 reclassified ~15% of patients (mostly intermediate→adverse via myelodysplasia-related mutations) versus ELN-2017 [ELN 2022 validation, Leukemia](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41375-023-01884-2).
- Key biomarkers gate targeted therapy: FLT3, IDH1, IDH2, NPM1, TP53, and cytogenetic/karyotype findings together set both prognosis and drug selection [ELN 2022 validation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11014905/).
- For fit, FLT3-mutated patients, adding midostaurin to intensive "7+3" chemotherapy improved survival in the RATIFY (CALGB 10603) trial: OS HR 0.78 (P=0.009), median OS 74.7 vs 25.6 months [RATIFY, NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1614359).
- For older/unfit patients ineligible for intensive induction, the VIALE-A trial established venetoclax plus azacitidine: median OS 14.7 vs 9.6 months (HR for death 0.66, P<0.001) and CR+CRi 66.4% vs 28.3% [VIALE-A, NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2012971).
- Mutation-targeted oral agents extend options in relapsed/refractory disease: ivosidenib (IDH1) and enasidenib (IDH2) are FDA-approved, with ivosidenib yielding ~32.8% CR/CRh in IDH1-mutated R/R AML [FDA ivosidenib approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-ivosidenib-relapsed-or-refractory-acute-myeloid-leukemia).
- Allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation remains the principal curative consolidation for intermediate- and adverse-risk fit patients in first remission, with ELN risk and age as the dominant survival determinants [ELN 2022 validation, Leukemia](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41375-023-01884-2).
