# What is the frontline treatment for chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL)?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

The frontline treatment for chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) has evolved significantly in recent years, largely shifting away from traditional chemoimmunotherapy in favor of highly effective targeted therapies [search-sourced]. According to recent guidelines, such as the 2025/2026 National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines, standard frontline treatment consists of Bruton's Tyrosine Kinase (BTK) inhibitors, BCL-2 inhibitors, and anti-CD20 monoclonal antibodies, used either as continuous therapy or as fixed-duration (time-limited) regimens [search-sourced].

**Preferred Frontline Regimens**
The current preferred regimens for treatment-naïve CLL typically include:

*   **Continuous BTK Inhibitors:** Second-generation covalent BTK inhibitors are preferred due to improved cardiovascular safety profiles over the first-generation agent, ibrutinib [search-sourced]. Options include:
    *   **Zanubrutinib** (monotherapy) [search-sourced].
    *   **Acalabrutinib** (with or without the monoclonal antibody obinutuzumab) [search-sourced].
*   **Fixed-Duration BCL-2 Inhibitor + Monoclonal Antibody:** 
    *   **Venetoclax plus obinutuzumab** is given for a limited duration (e.g., 12 months) rather than taken indefinitely until disease progression [search-sourced].
*   **Fixed-Duration BTK + BCL-2 Combinations:**
    *   **Acalabrutinib plus venetoclax** (with or without obinutuzumab) was recently added to the NCCN guidelines as a preferred frontline regimen. Backed by data from the AMPLIFY phase 3 trial, this combination provides a time-limited targeted option that offers high rates of undetectable measurable residual disease (uMRD) [search-sourced].
    *   **Zanubrutinib plus venetoclax** is also an emerging combination, classified by the NCCN as useful in certain circumstances [search-sourced].

**Treatment Selection Factors**
Clinicians individualize the choice of frontline therapy based on several factors:
*   **Genomic Risk Factors:** Specifically the presence of a *TP53* mutation or 17p deletion—which are historically associated with high-risk disease—and the *IGHV* mutational status [search-sourced]. While all the preferred targeted agents mentioned above are highly effective in these subgroups, these genetic markers help finalize therapy recommendations [search-sourced].
*   **Patient Comorbidities:** For instance, BTK inhibitors carry specific risks for hypertension, bleeding, and arrhythmias, which might guide a patient toward a venetoclax-based regimen if they have cardiovascular issues [search-sourced]. 
*   **Patient Preference:** A major decision point is whether the patient prefers a continuous, open-ended daily pill (like continuous BTK inhibitor monotherapy) or a fixed-duration, time-limited regimen that requires more upfront monitoring (like venetoclax regimens due to tumor lysis syndrome risks) but eventually allows the patient to live treatment-free [search-sourced].
