# What is the first-line treatment for advanced Hodgkin lymphoma?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

The first-line treatment for newly diagnosed advanced classical Hodgkin lymphoma (stage III and IV) has evolved significantly in recent years, shifting away from older chemotherapy-only regimens toward combinations that include targeted immunotherapy or antibody-drug conjugates. 

The primary first-line treatment options currently include:

*   **Nivolumab plus AVD (N-AVD):** The combination of the PD-1 inhibitor nivolumab with AVD chemotherapy (doxorubicin, vinblastine, and dacarbazine) is now considered the preferred standard of care [search-sourced]. The pivotal phase III SWOG S1826 clinical trial established that N-AVD provides superior progression-free survival and is generally much more tolerable than previous treatment standards for both adolescents (ages 12 and up) and adults [search-sourced]. 
*   **Brentuximab vedotin plus AVD (BV-AVD):** This combination uses brentuximab vedotin, a CD30-directed antibody-drug conjugate, alongside AVD [search-sourced]. Supported by long-term overall survival data from the ECHELON-1 trial, BV-AVD has been a highly effective, standard front-line treatment for about a decade [search-sourced]. 
*   **ABVD:** Historically, the ABVD regimen (doxorubicin, bleomycin, vinblastine, and dacarbazine) was the gold standard for many decades [search-sourced]. While it may still be used, there has been a major shift toward N-AVD and BV-AVD to both improve efficacy and avoid the severe pulmonary (lung) toxicity associated with the drug bleomycin [search-sourced]. 
*   **Intensified Regimens (e.g., BrECADD or escalated BEACOPP):** Particularly in Europe, patients may be treated with more intensive, PET-adapted chemotherapy approaches [search-sourced]. Recent data from the German Hodgkin Study Group (HD21 trial) has established the BrECADD regimen as a highly effective, more tolerable alternative to the historically used escalated BEACOPP regimen for advanced-stage disease [search-sourced].
