# What is the standard treatment for limited-stage small cell lung cancer?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

The standard treatment for limited-stage small cell lung cancer (LS-SCLC) is generally administered with curative intent and typically involves a combination of therapies based on the patient's overall health. 

**1. Concurrent Chemoradiotherapy**
For patients who are in good health, the primary standard of care is concurrent chemoradiotherapy, which involves administering chemotherapy and thoracic (chest) radiation therapy at the same time. 
*   **Chemotherapy:** The standard regimen is a "platinum-doublet," which combines etoposide with a platinum-based chemotherapy drug (either cisplatin or carboplatin). 
*   **Radiation Therapy:** High-energy radiation is targeted at the chest tumor. Giving radiation concurrently with chemotherapy provides better overall survival and a higher chance of a cure than administering the treatments one after the other, though it can result in more severe side effects.

**2. Consolidation Immunotherapy**
The treatment landscape for SCLC has recently advanced to include immunotherapy. Following the completion of concurrent chemoradiotherapy, the immune checkpoint inhibitor durvalumab is now approved and recommended as a consolidation therapy to help reduce the risk of the cancer relapsing. 

**3. Prophylactic Cranial Irradiation (PCI)**
Because small cell lung cancer has a high likelihood of spreading to the brain, patients who have a favorable response to their initial chemoradiation may be offered prophylactic cranial irradiation (PCI). This preventive radiation to the head is used to destroy microscopic cancer cells and lessen the risk of brain metastases.

**4. Surgery**
Surgery is rarely used in the management of small cell lung cancer because the disease usually spreads early. It is only considered in a very small minority of patients (roughly 2% to 5%) who are diagnosed at a very early stage with a single, small tumor and no evidence of spread to the lymph nodes. If a patient undergoes surgical resection, it is typically followed by adjuvant chemotherapy or chemoradiation.
