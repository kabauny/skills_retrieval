# What is the first-line treatment for metastatic clear cell renal cell carcinoma?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

According to current clinical guidelines (such as those from the NCCN and ASCO), the first-line treatment for metastatic clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) prioritizes combination regimens containing immune checkpoint inhibitors. The specific strategy chosen often depends on a patient's International Metastatic RCC Database Consortium (IMDC) risk classification (favorable vs. intermediate/poor risk). 

The preferred standard-of-care (Category 1) first-line regimens include combinations of either two immunotherapies (IO/IO) or an immunotherapy paired with a targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor (IO/TKI). These preferred combinations include:
*   **Pembrolizumab plus axitinib** (IO/TKI)
*   **Pembrolizumab plus lenvatinib** (IO/TKI)
*   **Nivolumab plus cabozantinib** (IO/TKI)
*   **Nivolumab plus ipilimumab** (IO/IO)

In highly select, favorable-risk patients who have asymptomatic, slow-growing, and low-volume disease, an initial period of active surveillance may be recommended before initiating systemic therapy. 

Additionally, precision medicine approaches have emerged for specific genetically driven subtypes. For example, belzutifan, a targeted HIF-2α inhibitor, is FDA-approved for advanced renal cell carcinoma and tumors associated with von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) disease [[belzutifan]]. Belzutifan targets the HIF-2α/pseudohypoxic pathway, offering a specialized targeted treatment avenue [[belzutifan]]; however, the cornerstone first-line treatment for standard sporadic metastatic ccRCC remains the aforementioned ICI-based combination therapies.
