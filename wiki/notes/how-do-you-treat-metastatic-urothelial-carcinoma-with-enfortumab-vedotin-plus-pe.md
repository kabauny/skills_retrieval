# How do you treat metastatic urothelial carcinoma with enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

The combination of enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab is an FDA-approved first-line treatment for patients with locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma [search-sourced]. 

The standard dosing regimen and schedule for this combination is as follows:
* **Cycle Length:** Treatment is administered on a 21-day (3-week) cycle [search-sourced].
* **Enfortumab Vedotin:** The recommended dose is 1.25 mg/kg (up to a maximum dose of 125 mg for patients weighing 100 kg or greater) [search-sourced]. It is administered as an intravenous (IV) infusion over 30 minutes on Days 1 and 8 of each 21-day cycle [search-sourced].
* **Pembrolizumab:** The recommended dose is 200 mg via IV infusion every 3 weeks (typically administered on Day 1 of the cycle, given sequentially after the enfortumab vedotin infusion) [search-sourced]. Alternatively, pembrolizumab can be dosed at 400 mg via IV infusion every 6 weeks [search-sourced].

**Treatment Duration:**
* The medications are generally continued until the patient experiences disease progression or unacceptable toxicity [search-sourced]. 
* Pembrolizumab therapy is typically administered for a maximum duration of 24 months (2 years) [search-sourced].

**Safety and Monitoring:**
* Healthcare providers must closely monitor for common and potentially severe adverse events associated with this regimen [search-sourced]. Notable toxicities include severe skin reactions/rash, peripheral neuropathy, pneumonitis or interstitial lung disease, fatigue, acute kidney injury, and hyperglycemia [search-sourced].
