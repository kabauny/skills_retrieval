# What is the second-line treatment after progression for HR-positive breast cancer?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-08.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

Standard first-line therapy for advanced HR-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer typically consists of endocrine therapy combined with a CDK4/6 inhibitor [search-sourced]. Upon disease progression, the selection of second-line treatment is highly individualized and guided by biomarker-driven decision-making rather than a rigid, one-size-fits-all treatment algorithm [[what-is-the-recommended-sequencing-of-vepdegestrant-relative-to-other-non-endocr]]. Testing via next-generation sequencing or liquid biopsies (such as the FDA-approved Guardant360 CDx) is strongly recommended to identify acquired resistance mutations and appropriately direct therapy [[what-is-the-recommended-sequencing-of-vepdegestrant-relative-to-other-non-endocr]].

Depending on the molecular profile of the tumor at the time of progression, second-line options generally include:

*   **ESR1 Mutations**: Mutations in the *ESR1* gene frequently arise as a mechanism of endocrine therapy resistance [search-sourced]. For patients with ESR1-mutant dominant disease, vepdegestrant (Veppanu)—a first-in-class PROTAC estrogen receptor degrader FDA-approved in May 2026—serves as a highly robust new standard-of-care monotherapy backbone [[what-is-the-recommended-sequencing-of-vepdegestrant-relative-to-other-non-endocr]]. Other targeted therapies, such as oral selective estrogen receptor degraders (SERDs) like elacestrant, are also recognized evidence-based options for tumors harboring these mutations [search-sourced].
*   **PI3K/AKT/mTOR Pathway Alterations**: If liquid biopsy or tissue testing reveals actionable co-occurring alterations in *PIK3CA, AKT1,* or *PTEN*, targeted pathway agents are critical therapeutic options [[what-is-the-recommended-sequencing-of-vepdegestrant-relative-to-other-non-endocr]]. Clinicians may utilize PI3K inhibitors (such as alpelisib or inavolisib) or mTOR inhibitors (like everolimus) [[what-is-the-recommended-sequencing-of-vepdegestrant-relative-to-other-non-endocr]]. Additionally, the AKT inhibitor capivasertib, which received FDA approval in November 2023 for use in specific types of advanced breast cancer, is frequently deployed in this setting [[tell-me-about-akt-pathway-drugs-available-for-nsclc]].
*   **Co-mutated Disease**: When a patient harbors both an *ESR1* mutation and an actionable PI3K/AKT pathway mutation, the optimal sequencing strategy (e.g., prioritizing vepdegestrant monotherapy versus a PI3K/AKT inhibitor combined with traditional endocrine therapy like fulvestrant) remains an open clinical question [[what-is-the-recommended-sequencing-of-vepdegestrant-relative-to-other-non-endocr]]. This decision must be customized based on the patient's dominant driver mutation, overall tumor burden, and expected tolerance profiles [[what-is-the-recommended-sequencing-of-vepdegestrant-relative-to-other-non-endocr]]. Because vepdegestrant possesses a highly manageable safety profile and avoids broad off-target toxicities, it is being evaluated in ongoing clinical trials as a combination agent alongside PI3K/mTOR inhibitors [[what-is-the-recommended-sequencing-of-vepdegestrant-relative-to-other-non-endocr]] [[what-are-the-specific-toxicity-profiles-and-recommended-management-strategies-fo]].
*   **Absence of Actionable Mutations**: For patients whose tumors lack specific targetable molecular drivers—or who progress on second-line targeted agents—subsequent treatments may include different single-agent endocrine therapies, antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) like sacituzumab govitecan, or traditional chemotherapy regimens [search-sourced].
