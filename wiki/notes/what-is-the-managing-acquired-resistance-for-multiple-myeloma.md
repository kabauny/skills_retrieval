# What is the managing acquired resistance for multiple myeloma?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-08.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

Managing acquired resistance in multiple myeloma (MM) is a critical focus in clinical oncology, as the vast majority of patients eventually experience relapsed/refractory disease following frontline treatments [search-sourced]. Overcoming this resistance requires targeting new pathways, utilizing combination regimens, and leveraging personalized and cellular immunotherapies.

### Mechanisms of Acquired Resistance
Acquired resistance in MM is driven by intra-tumor heterogeneity, clonal evolution, and protective interactions within the bone marrow microenvironment [search-sourced]. Resistance mechanisms often include the induction of JAK/STAT and PI3K/AKT signaling (frequently via IL-6), which upregulates anti-apoptotic proteins to promote cancer cell survival [search-sourced]. Furthermore, multiple myeloma stem cells and new genetic mutations contribute to progressive unresponsiveness to standard-of-care agents [search-sourced].

### BCMA-Targeted Immunotherapies
A cornerstone of managing relapsed and multi-drug resistant myeloma is utilizing agents that bind to the B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA). BCMA is a cell surface protein heavily expressed on multiple myeloma cells, making it a highly effective target to bypass traditional resistance mechanisms [[wiki/b-cell-maturation-antigen]]. Several modalities exploit this target:
*   **CAR T-cell Therapies:** Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapies, such as ciltacabtagene autoleucel (Carvykti), are personalized treatments that offer deep remissions [search-sourced]. Ciltacabtagene autoleucel is approved as early as the second-line setting for patients whose disease is refractory to lenalidomide and prior immunomodulatory/proteasome inhibitor therapies [[wiki/ciltacabtagene-autoleucel]]. 
*   **Bispecific Antibodies:** Bispecific T-cell engagers, such as teclistamab, redirect the patient's own immune cells to attack BCMA-expressing myeloma cells. The Phase 3 MajesTEC-3 trial demonstrated substantial overall and progression-free survival benefits for teclistamab when utilized in the second-line setting for patients who have failed prior lenalidomide and anti-CD38 therapies [[wiki/majestec-3-trial]].
*   **Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs):** For patients where personalized cellular therapy is inaccessible or inappropriate, off-the-shelf options like belantamab mafodotin (Blenrep) provide a non-CAR T, BCMA-targeted approach that has been shown to improve survival outcomes when used in combination regimens [[wiki/belantamab-mafodotin]].

### Emerging Drug Classes and Novel Agents
To overcome resistance to standard immunomodulatory drugs (IMiDs) and proteasome inhibitors, clinicians are turning to novel classes of therapies:
*   **CELMoDs (Cereblon E3 Ligase Modulatory Drugs):** Novel oral agents like mezigdomide act by binding to the cereblon protein significantly more tightly than standard IMiDs (e.g., lenalidomide, pomalidomide) [search-sourced]. By inducing nearly 100% degradation of targeted cancer-promoting proteins, mezigdomide can overcome resistance and stimulate immune attacks on remaining cancer cells [search-sourced].
*   **Personalized/Biomarker-Driven Therapies:** Patients with specific genetic profiles, such as the t(11;14) translocation, typically exhibit upregulated BCL-2 expression [search-sourced]. These patients often respond well to the targeted BCL-2 inhibitor venetoclax, managing resistance driven by apoptosis suppression [search-sourced].
*   **Exportin 1 (XPO1) Inhibitors:** Drugs like selinexor function by blocking the nuclear export of tumor suppressor proteins and have shown efficacy in restoring drug sensitivity in multidrug-resistant myeloma [search-sourced].
