# What is the role of PARP inhibitors in metastatic prostate cancer with HRR mutations?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

**Role of PARP Inhibitors in Metastatic Prostate Cancer with HRR Mutations**

Poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors have become a cornerstone in the targeted treatment of advanced prostate cancer, specifically for patients harboring homologous recombination repair (HRR) mutations. Approximately 20% to 25% of patients with metastatic prostate cancer have somatic or germline mutations in the DNA damage repair (DDR) pathway, with *BRCA1*, *BRCA2*, and *ATM* being among the most common. 

**Mechanism of Action**
HRR mutations compromise a cancer cell's primary ability to repair double-strand DNA breaks. PARP inhibitors work by blocking a secondary, "rescue" DNA repair mechanism by trapping PARP bound to single-strand DNA breaks. This causes replication forks to stall and collapse, leading to the generation of catastrophic double-strand breaks. Because these cancer cells already lack functional HRR, this overwhelming DNA damage causes synthetic lethality and subsequent cancer cell death.

**Clinical Efficacy and FDA Approvals**
PARP inhibitors have demonstrated significant efficacy in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC), fundamentally changing the treatment landscape. Key milestones and FDA approvals include:
* **Olaparib:** Approved in 2020 for mCRPC patients with a broad array of HRR gene mutations whose disease progressed following treatment with androgen receptor pathway inhibitors (such as abiraterone or enzalutamide). This was driven by the Phase III PROfound trial, which demonstrated significantly improved progression-free and overall survival.
* **Rucaparib:** Granted accelerated approval for mCRPC patients specifically with *BRCA1* or *BRCA2* mutations who have previously been treated with both androgen receptor-directed therapy and a taxane-based chemotherapy. This was based on the TRITON2 phase II study.
* **Emerging Combinations:** Newer trials and clinical applications have expanded to combine PARP inhibitors (such as niraparib or talazoparib) with androgen receptor-targeted therapies (like abiraterone or enzalutamide). Preclinical evidence suggests that androgen suppression can induce a "BRCA-like" state in tumor cells, producing synergistic sensitivity to PARP inhibition.

**Clinical Guidelines**
Due to the profound clinical benefit of PARP inhibitors in this biomarker-defined population, the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines recommend that all patients with metastatic prostate cancer undergo somatic tumor testing (and/or germline testing) for HRR mutations. Identifying these mutations early in the metastatic disease course is essential to uncover drivers that can be successfully targeted by PARP inhibitors.

**Safety profile**
The adverse effects of PARP inhibitors are generally similar across the class and include bone marrow toxicity (leading to anemia and thrombocytopenia), fatigue, nausea, and anorexia. These side effects are typically manageable but may require dose interruptions in about half of patients, and dose reductions in roughly a quarter.
