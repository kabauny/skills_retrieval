# How do you treat acute myeloid leukemia with FLT3 mutations?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

The treatment of acute myeloid leukemia (AML) with FLT3 mutations has evolved significantly with the introduction of targeted therapies [search-sourced]. FLT3 mutations primarily consist of internal tandem duplications (FLT3-ITD) or tyrosine kinase domain mutations (FLT3-TKD) [search-sourced]. Because mutation status can evolve or emerge over time, clinical guidelines strongly recommend molecular testing for FLT3 mutations at initial diagnosis and at each disease relapse or progression [search-sourced].

Depending on patient fitness, exact mutation subtype, and disease stage, treatment incorporates specific FDA-approved FLT3 inhibitors [search-sourced]. 

### 1. Frontline Therapy for Fit / Newly Diagnosed Patients
For younger patients or those fit for intensive therapy, standard intensive induction chemotherapy (e.g., "7+3" cytarabine and an anthracycline) and consolidation are combined with a targeted FLT3 inhibitor [search-sourced]:
*   **Midostaurin:** FDA-approved for use alongside standard induction and consolidation chemotherapy in newly diagnosed patients with any FLT3 mutation (ITD or TKD) [search-sourced].
*   **Quizartinib:** FDA-approved in 2023 specifically for newly diagnosed patients with the FLT3-ITD mutation. It is combined with standard induction and consolidation chemotherapy, and can subsequently be used as maintenance monotherapy [search-sourced].
*   **Stem Cell Transplant:** Following remission, eligible patients frequently proceed to an allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (alloSCT) to achieve the best long-term outcomes [search-sourced].

### 2. Frontline Therapy for Older or Unfit Patients
Older patients or those with comorbidities who are ineligible for intensive induction chemotherapy typically receive lower-intensity regimens [search-sourced].
*   The baseline standard of care is generally a hypomethylating agent (HMA) combined with venetoclax [search-sourced]. 
*   However, emerging data and retrospective analyses show that a "triplet" therapy regimen—combining an HMA, venetoclax, and a FLT3 inhibitor—yields excellent long-term overall survival outcomes and is heavily utilized in current practice [search-sourced].

### 3. Relapsed or Refractory AML
Patients with FLT3-ITD mutations historically have a higher risk of relapse [search-sourced]. For those whose disease recurs or does not respond to initial treatment, targeted monotherapy is heavily prioritized [search-sourced].
*   **Gilteritinib:** FDA-approved as a single-agent therapy (monotherapy) for adult patients with relapsed or refractory AML who test positive for a FLT3 mutation [search-sourced].
