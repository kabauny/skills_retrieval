# What is the first-line treatment for advanced biliary tract cancer / cholangiocarcinoma?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

The current standard first-line treatment for advanced, unresectable, or metastatic biliary tract cancer (which includes cholangiocarcinoma) is combination chemoimmunotherapy [search-sourced].

Specifically, clinical guidelines recommend combining the traditional chemotherapy backbone of **gemcitabine and cisplatin** with an immune checkpoint inhibitor [search-sourced]. The two established and recommended regimens are:

1. **Gemcitabine + Cisplatin + Durvalumab**: This regimen became a standard of care based on the pivotal Phase III TOPAZ-1 trial, which demonstrated a significant improvement in overall survival (OS) when the immunotherapy drug durvalumab was added to chemotherapy compared to chemotherapy alone [search-sourced]. 
2. **Gemcitabine + Cisplatin + Pembrolizumab**: This alternative standard is based on the Phase III KEYNOTE-966 trial, which likewise demonstrated an overall survival benefit for patients when pembrolizumab was added to the gemcitabine and cisplatin backbone [search-sourced].

Historically, prior to the introduction of immunotherapy to the frontline setting, the standard of care for over a decade was simply gemcitabine plus cisplatin (established by the ABC-02 trial) [search-sourced]. 

Additionally, current clinical practices strongly emphasize conducting early comprehensive molecular testing (e.g., next-generation sequencing) at the time of an advanced cholangiocarcinoma diagnosis [search-sourced]. While chemoimmunotherapy serves as the primary first-line systemic approach, identifying actionable genetic alterations (such as FGFR2 fusions, IDH1 mutations, HER2 overexpression, or MSI-H/dMMR) is critical for guiding molecularly-targeted treatments if the disease progresses to the second-line setting [search-sourced].
