# What is the first-line treatment for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (atezolizumab plus bevacizumab)?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

The combination of **atezolizumab plus bevacizumab** is a standard-of-care, first-line systemic treatment for patients with advanced or unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10327339/). 

**Approval and Clinical Efficacy**
* **The IMbrave150 Trial:** This combination regimen established its place as a preferred first-line therapy based on the positive results of the pivotal Phase III IMbrave150 clinical trial [search-sourced] [cancerdiagnosisprognosis.org](https://www.cancerdiagnosisprognosis.org/storage/cdp/1/2/cdp-1-83.pdf).
* **Superiority to Previous Standards:** The trial demonstrated that atezolizumab plus bevacizumab provided a significantly longer overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) when compared to sorafenib, which had been the standard first-line therapy for advanced HCC for over a decade [search-sourced] [ascopost.com](https://ascopost.com/issues/march-25-2020-supplement-conference-highlights-gi-cancers/imbrave150-prespecified-analysis-quality-of-life-with-atezolizumab-plus-bevacizumab/). Based on these outcomes, the combination regimen was officially approved for this indication in 2020 [search-sourced] [cancerdiagnosisprognosis.org](https://www.cancerdiagnosisprognosis.org/storage/cdp/1/2/cdp-1-83.pdf).
* **Quality of Life:** In addition to its survival advantages, patients receiving the atezolizumab/bevacizumab combination experienced less deterioration in physical functioning, role functioning, and overall quality of life compared to those taking sorafenib [search-sourced] [ascopost.com](https://ascopost.com/issues/march-25-2020-supplement-conference-highlights-gi-cancers/imbrave150-prespecified-analysis-quality-of-life-with-atezolizumab-plus-bevacizumab/).

**Mechanism of Action**
* **Atezolizumab:** This medication is an immune checkpoint inhibitor (specifically, an anti-PD-L1 antibody) that prevents cancer cells from suppressing the immune system, thereby helping the body's immune cells recognize and attack the tumor [search-sourced] [targetedonc.com](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/imbrave150-trial-combination-therapy-in-advanced-hcc).
* **Bevacizumab:** This is an anti-angiogenic targeted therapy (an anti-VEGF antibody) that inhibits the formation of new blood vessels that tumors rely on for growth [search-sourced] [targetedonc.com](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/imbrave150-trial-combination-therapy-in-advanced-hcc). Furthermore, inhibiting VEGF helps modulate the tumor's immune microenvironment, which can actually potentiate the immune-boosting effects of atezolizumab [search-sourced] [targetedonc.com](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/imbrave150-trial-combination-therapy-in-advanced-hcc).

**Patient Selection and Contraindications**
* **Liver Function:** The treatment is primarily recommended for patients with well-compensated liver function (typically categorized as Child-Pugh Class A), as this group was the primary focus of the IMbrave150 trial [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37767068/). 
* **Bleeding Risks:** Because bevacizumab restricts vascular endothelial growth factors, it carries a known risk for serious bleeding events [search-sourced] [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/OP.24.00189). Consequently, patients with significant cardiovascular comorbidities, other severe bleeding risks, or high-risk untreated esophageal varices are generally not appropriate candidates for this therapy [search-sourced] [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/OP.24.00189).
* **Alternatives:** For patients who have contraindications to atezolizumab plus bevacizumab (such as those with high bleeding risks), alternative first-line systemic therapies are utilized—most notably the oral tyrosine kinase inhibitors lenvatinib or sorafenib [search-sourced].
