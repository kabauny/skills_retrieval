# A 78-year-old male with relapsed/refractory AML, an IDH1 mutation, and poor performance status (ECOG 3) due to significa

For this 78-year-old patient with an ECOG 3 performance status and significant cardiac comorbidities, the **venetoclax + azacitidine** regimen is the safer and more appropriate choice over single-agent ivosidenib, provided the patient did not already receive venetoclax + azacitidine in the first-line setting. 

Here is the clinical rationale:

**The Risk of Ivosidenib in Cardiac Patients**
While ivosidenib is an FDA-approved and NCCN-recommended targeted therapy specifically designed for patients with relapsed/refractory IDH1-mutated AML [[what-is-the-second-line-treatment-after-progression-for-acute-myeloid-leukemia]], it carries a well-documented risk of QTc interval prolongation [tibsovo.com](https://www.tibsovo.com/aml). QTc prolongation can cause life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias [tibsovo.com](https://www.tibsovo.com/aml). Because this patient has significant pre-existing cardiac comorbidities, introducing a drug with known cardiotoxic and arrhythmia-inducing properties poses a severe and potentially unmanageable risk [cancercareontario.ca](https://www.cancercareontario.ca/en/system/files_force/ivosidenib.pdf?download=1).

**The Role of Venetoclax + Azacitidine**
A lower-intensity regimen combining a hypomethylating agent (azacitidine) with venetoclax is the standard recommendation for older, unfit patients who are unable to tolerate intensive salvage chemotherapy regimens [[what-is-the-second-line-treatment-after-progression-for-acute-myeloid-leukemia]]. 
* **Fitness appropriateness:** At 78 years old with an ECOG 3 performance score, this patient fits the profile for lower-intensity therapy [[what-is-the-second-line-treatment-after-progression-for-acute-myeloid-leukemia]].
* **Efficacy in IDH1:** Clinical data consistently shows that venetoclax-based regimens are highly effective in AML patients harboring IDH1 and IDH2 mutations, often achieving high overall response rates [ashpublications.org](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/140/Supplement%201/3358/490614/Venetoclax-Based-Therapies-for-Acute-Myeloid). 
* **Safety profile:** Crucially, this combination avoids the direct QTc prolongation warnings associated with ivosidenib, making it a much safer alternative for someone with a compromised cardiovascular system [cancercareontario.ca](https://www.cancercareontario.ca/en/system/files_force/ivosidenib.pdf?download=1) [tibsovopro.com](https://www.tibsovopro.com/aml/faqs).

**Other Considerations**
If the patient's performance status continues to decline, or if he previously received venetoclax + azacitidine during his first-line treatment, NCCN guidelines highly recommend enrolling in an appropriate clinical trial [[what-is-the-second-line-treatment-after-progression-for-acute-myeloid-leukemia]]. Alternatively, if further active cancer treatment is deemed too physically demanding, transitioning to best supportive care to prioritize symptom management and quality of life is the recommended clinical pathway [[what-is-the-second-line-treatment-after-progression-for-acute-myeloid-leukemia]].
