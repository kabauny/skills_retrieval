# What is the biomarker testing to guide therapy for diffuse large B-cell lymphoma?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-08.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

Biomarker testing plays a crucial role in the diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment selection for patients with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) [cancernetwork.com](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/3-things-you-should-know-about-biomarkers-in-dlbcl). The National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines recommend comprehensive molecular and phenotypic testing at diagnosis and upon relapse to guide personalized therapy [nccn.org](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/nhl-diffuse-patient.pdf). 

The primary biomarkers used to guide therapy in DLBCL include:

**1. Cell Surface Markers / Immunophenotyping**
*   **CD20:** Confirming CD20 expression is a required baseline test to establish B-cell origin and determine a patient's eligibility for anti-CD20 monoclonal antibodies (like rituximab), which are the foundation of frontline therapy (e.g., R-CHOP) [nccn.org](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/nhl-diffuse-patient.pdf) [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7734862/). It also determines eligibility for CD20xCD3 bispecific antibodies (e.g., epcoritamab, glofitamab, and mosunetuzumab) used in relapsed or refractory settings [jnccn.org](https://jnccn.org/abstract/journals/jnccn/23/10/article-e250048.xml) [onclive.com](https://www.onclive.com/view/fda-approves-glofitamab-for-relapsed-refractory-dlbcl) [cancernetwork.com](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/elucidating-bispecific-impact-in-diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma).
*   **CD19:** Testing for CD19 is essential for patients with relapsed or refractory DLBCL to guide the use of CD19-directed therapies [jnccn.org](https://jnccn.org/abstract/journals/jnccn/23/10/article-e250048.xml). These include CAR T-cell therapies (e.g., axicabtagene ciloleucel, tisagenlecleucel, lisocabtagene maraleucel), the monoclonal antibody tafasitamab, and the antibody-drug conjugate loncastuximab tesirine [jnccn.org](https://jnccn.org/abstract/journals/jnccn/23/10/article-e250048.xml) [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7646825/).
*   **CD79b:** Evaluating this biomarker establishes targetability for polatuzumab vedotin, an anti-CD79b antibody-drug conjugate approved as a frontline therapy (in the pola-R-CHP regimen) and for relapsed/refractory disease [primeinc.org](https://primeinc.org/resourcecenter/navigating-the-evolving-treatment-landscape-for-rr-dlbcl/activity/biomarkers-dlbcl-current-implications-diagnosis-prognosis-treatment/pre-test) [cancernetwork.com](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/elucidating-bispecific-impact-in-diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma).
*   **CD30:** Evaluated via immunohistochemistry (IHC), CD30 expression can occasionally be used to identify patients who may benefit from off-label targeted agents like brentuximab vedotin, or it can inform clinical trial eligibility [cap.org](https://documents.cap.org/protocols/cp-biomarker-diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma-2015-v1001.pdf) [primeinc.org](https://primeinc.org/resourcecenter/navigating-the-evolving-treatment-landscape-for-rr-dlbcl/activity/biomarkers-dlbcl-current-implications-diagnosis-prognosis-treatment/pre-test).

**2. Cell of Origin (COO)**
*   Assessed using IHC markers—such as CD10, BCL6, and IRF4/MUM1 (via the Hans algorithm) or through gene expression profiling (e.g., the Lymph2Cx assay)—COO testing classifies DLBCL into Germinal Center B-cell (GCB) or Activated B-cell (ABC)/Non-GCB subtypes.
*  [nccn.org](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/nhl-diffuse-patient.pdf) [cancernetwork.com](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/3-things-you-should-know-about-biomarkers-in-dlbcl) [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9381398/)  Identifying the ABC subtype provides significant prognostic value, as it typically correlates with poorer outcomes on standard R-CHOP regimens. CO [cancernetwork.com](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/3-things-you-should-know-about-biomarkers-in-dlbcl)O classification helps inform the use of newer tailored approaches, such as the incorporation of polatuzumab vedotin or deciding on clinical trial eligibility for targeted therapies like BTK inhibitors.

*   **MYC, BCL2, and BCL6 Rearrangements:** Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) testing for these specific gene rearrangements is critical during initial diagnosis. Tu [nccn.org](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/nhl-diffuse-patient.pdf)mors harboring a concurrent *MYC* rearrangement alongside *BCL2* and/or *BCL6* rearrangements are classified as "Double-Hit" or "Triple-Hit" high-grade lymphomas. Th [tri-kobe.org](https://www2.tri-kobe.org/nccn/guideline/hematologic/nhl/english/bcel.pdf) [primeinc.org](https://primeinc.org/resourcecenter/navigating-the-evolving-treatment-landscape-for-rr-dlbcl/activity/biomarkers-dlbcl-current-implications-diagnosis-prognosis-treatment/pre-test)ese variants are highly aggressive and generally require intensified chemotherapy regimens (such as DA-EPOCH-R) rather than standard R-CHOP.
*  [primeinc.org](https://primeinc.org/resourcecenter/navigating-the-evolving-treatment-landscape-for-rr-dlbcl/activity/biomarkers-dlbcl-current-implications-diagnosis-prognosis-treatment/pre-test)  **TP53 Mutations:** Molecular profiling for *TP53* indicates high-risk disease and guides prognostic expectations.
*  [haematologyhorizon.co.uk](https://www.haematologyhorizon.co.uk/content/dam/open-digital/haem-hub/en0/medical-education/downloads/importance-of-biomarker-testing-in-diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma-and-mantle-cell-lymphoma.pdf) [nccn.org](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/cll-patient.pdf)  **Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS):** NGS lymphoma panels search for other specific gene mutations (such as *EZH2*, *MYD88*, or *CD79B*) which are increasingly evaluated to match relapsed patients to precision medicines or novel targeted clinical trials.

* [nccn.org](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/nhl-diffuse-patient.pdf) [primeinc.org](https://primeinc.org/resourcecenter/navigating-the-evolving-treatment-landscape-for-rr-dlbcl/activity/biomarkers-dlbcl-current-implications-diagnosis-prognosis-treatment/pre-test)*4. Minimal Residual Disease (MRD) via Circulating Tumor DNA (ctDNA)**
*   Recent updates to the NCCN guidelines have formally incorporated highly sensitive ctDNA testing (such as PhasED-seq, utilizing a <1 ppm limit of detection) for MRD assessment at the end of first-line treatment.
*  [onclive.com](https://www.onclive.com/view/ctdna-testing-joins-nccn-guidelines-for-mrd-assessment-in-dlbcl)  This test serves as a critical biomarker guide for patients who yield a positive or equivocal PET scan after frontline therapy. A  [onclive.com](https://www.onclive.com/view/ctdna-testing-joins-nccn-guidelines-for-mrd-assessment-in-dlbcl)negative ctDNA MRD test result can serve as an alternative to an invasive biopsy, allowing oncologists to confidently assign patients to observation rather than unnecessary additional treatments, effectively mitigating the high false-positive rate of PET scans.
