# What is the second-line treatment after progression for follicular lymphoma?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-08.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

The second-line treatment for follicular lymphoma (FL) after progression depends on several factors, including prior therapies received, the duration of the initial response, and whether the patient is experiencing symptoms [search-sourced]. Before starting second-line treatment, a confirmatory biopsy is often recommended to ensure the disease has not histologically transformed into a more aggressive lymphoma, such as diffuse large B-cell lymphoma [search-sourced].

Treatment options and strategies typically include:

*   **Watchful Waiting:** For asymptomatic patients who experience a relapse or progression, a "watch and wait" approach is acceptable, delaying active treatment and avoiding medication side effects until symptoms arise or the disease significantly worsens [search-sourced].
*   **Rituximab and Lenalidomide (R²):** The combination of the immunomodulator lenalidomide and the monoclonal antibody rituximab (often referred to as R²) is an established, chemotherapy-free standard second-line treatment for relapsed or refractory FL [search-sourced].
*   **Epcoritamab plus R²:** Recently approved by the FDA as a second-line therapy, the bispecific antibody epcoritamab combined with rituximab and lenalidomide (R²) offers a highly effective, fixed-duration, outpatient treatment that significantly reduces the risk of disease progression compared to R² alone [search-sourced]. 
*   **Alternative Frontline Regimens:** Chemoimmunotherapy regimens typically used in the frontline setting (such as bendamustine plus rituximab or obinutuzumab) may be recommended as second-line therapy if the patient did not already receive them during their initial treatment [search-sourced].
*   **Tafasitamab combination:** The addition of the CD19-directed monoclonal antibody tafasitamab to lenalidomide and rituximab is also supported by recent phase 3 clinical trial data as an effective second-line regimen [search-sourced].
*   **Other targeted therapies:** Depending on specific patient circumstances, other options such as PI3K inhibitors (e.g., idelalisib) or enrollment in clinical trials may be considered in the second-line setting [search-sourced].
