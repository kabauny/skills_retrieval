# Tell me about AKT pathway drugs available for NSCLC

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

Currently, there are no AKT inhibitors that are FDA-approved specifically for the treatment of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11588547/). The first and only FDA-approved AKT inhibitor in oncology is capivasertib (Truqap), which was approved in November 2023 for specific types of locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer [search-sourced] [fda.gov](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-capivasertib-fulvestrant-breast-cancer).

However, the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway is frequently overexpressed or dysregulated in NSCLC [search-sourced] [plos.org](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0193344). This pathway's activation acts as a primary mechanism of acquired resistance to standard targeted therapies, particularly EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) like erlotinib and osimertinib [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7481581/). Because of this, several AKT pathway drugs are currently being heavily investigated in NSCLC clinical trials [search-sourced] [frontiersin.org](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1516583/full):

*   **Capivasertib (AZD5363):** Although currently only approved for breast cancer, this potent ATP-competitive AKT inhibitor is actively being studied for NSCLC [search-sourced] [acs.org](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jmedchem.4c00075). Phase I and II clinical trials are evaluating its efficacy in combination with osimertinib for patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC who have developed disease progression and resistance via PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN pathway alterations [search-sourced] [clinicaltrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07486648).
*   **MK-2206:** An allosteric pan-AKT inhibitor that has been investigated in phase II clinical trials for advanced NSCLC [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4592395/). It has been evaluated extensively in combination with EGFR inhibitors like erlotinib in an attempt to overcome TKI resistance [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4592395/).
*   **Ipatasertib (GDC-0068):** An orally administered pan-AKT inhibitor that targets all three isoforms of AKT, which is currently under investigation in Phase II clinical trials for NSCLC [search-sourced] [frontiersin.org](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1516583/full).
*   **Perifosine:** An alkylphospholipid AKT inhibitor that has been historically evaluated in phase II trials for NSCLC, though it has not achieved FDA approval [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7049515/).

While no AKT-directed drugs are commercially available for NSCLC patients yet, molecular profiling of tumors for AKT pathway alterations (such as AKT1 or PTEN mutations) remains highly relevant for matching patients to these ongoing targeted clinical trials [search-sourced] [frontiersin.org](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1516583/full).
