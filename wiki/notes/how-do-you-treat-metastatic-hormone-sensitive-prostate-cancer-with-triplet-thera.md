# How do you treat metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer with triplet therapy?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

To treat metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) with triplet therapy, oncologists use a combination of three systemic treatments to intensify therapy beyond traditional methods. 

**Components of Triplet Therapy**
Standard triplet therapy for mHSPC typically consists of androgen deprivation therapy (ADT), a chemotherapy agent (specifically docetaxel), and a second-generation androgen receptor pathway inhibitor (ARPI) such as darolutamide or abiraterone [search-sourced]. Both American and European clinical guidelines now recommend this triplet regimen as a first-line treatment for selected patients with mHSPC [search-sourced].

**Supporting Clinical Trials**
The clinical shift toward triplet therapy is strongly supported by two landmark phase III clinical trials [search-sourced]:
* **PEACE-1 Trial**: This trial evaluated the addition of abiraterone to ADT and docetaxel. It demonstrated that the triplet combination significantly improved overall survival (with a 25% lower risk of death) and prolonged radiographic progression-free survival by about 2.5 years compared to patients receiving only the doublet of ADT and docetaxel [search-sourced].
* **ARASENS Trial**: This trial evaluated the addition of darolutamide to ADT and docetaxel. It found that patients treated with this triplet intensification experienced a 32% lower risk of death compared to those treated with ADT and docetaxel alone [search-sourced].

**Patient Selection and Tolerability**
Patient selection is essential when deciding to deploy triplet therapy. The regimen is typically most beneficial for patients presenting with *de novo* (synchronous) or high-volume metastatic disease [search-sourced]. Because it involves chemotherapy, physicians must carefully evaluate patient comorbidities, age, and overall fitness [search-sourced]. Docetaxel can cause severe adverse events—such as neutropenia, peripheral neuropathy, and fatigue—which underscores the need for careful management and individualized treatment strategies, particularly in older adults [search-sourced]. 

**Emerging Non-Chemotherapy Triplets**
In addition to traditional chemotherapy-based triplets, novel targeted non-chemotherapy regimens are emerging [search-sourced]. For example, as of late 2025, the FDA approved the combination of abiraterone and niraparib (a PARP inhibitor) for patients with BRCA1/2-mutated mHSPC; when combined with ADT, this serves as a non-chemotherapy triplet option for patients with specific genetic mutations [search-sourced].
