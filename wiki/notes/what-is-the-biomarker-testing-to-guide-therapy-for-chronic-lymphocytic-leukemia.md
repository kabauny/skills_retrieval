# What is the biomarker testing to guide therapy for chronic lymphocytic leukemia?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-08.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

Biomarker testing is an essential step in guiding treatment decisions for patients with Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL) [nccn.org](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/cll-patient.pdf). Clinical guidelines, such as those from the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN), recommend specific tests to identify whether a patient has high-risk features that dictate the use of targeted therapies over traditional chemoimmunotherapy [ashpublications.org](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/146/Supplement%201/8077/554647/Real-world-chronic-lymphocytic-leukemia-CLL) [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10468359/).

The primary biomarker tests used to guide CLL therapy include:

*   **Fluorescence In Situ Hybridization (FISH):** This test identifies missing or extra parts of chromosomes in the leukemia cells [cllsociety.org](https://cllsociety.org/cll-sll-patient-education-toolkit/test-before-treat-handout/) [nccn.org](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/cll-patient.pdf). Clinicians look for specific chromosomal abnormalities such as **del(17p)**, **del(11q)**, **del(13q)**, and **trisomy 12 (+12)** [cancercare.org](https://media.cancercare.org/publications/original/419-2020_CLL_Whitepaper.pdf). Because chromosomal abnormalities can change over time, FISH testing is generally recommended at diagnosis and again before starting every new line of treatment [cllsociety.org](https://cllsociety.org/cll-sll-patient-education-toolkit/test-before-treat-handout/) [cancercare.org](https://media.cancercare.org/publications/original/419-2020_CLL_Whitepaper.pdf).
*   **TP53 Mutation Status:** The *TP53* gene produces a tumor suppressor protein. Mutations in this gene (frequently associated with del(17p)) indicate a higher-risk disease [ashpublications.org](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/146/Supplement%201/8077/554647/Real-world-chronic-lymphocytic-leukemia-CLL) [cllsociety.org](https://cllsociety.org/cll-sll-patient-education-toolkit/test-before-treat-handout/). Like FISH testing, *TP53* status can change and should be re-evaluated before initiating any new therapy [cllsociety.org](https://cllsociety.org/cll-sll-patient-education-toolkit/test-before-treat-handout/) [cancercare.org](https://media.cancercare.org/publications/original/419-2020_CLL_Whitepaper.pdf).
*   **IGHV Mutation Status:** This test evaluates the immunoglobulin heavy-chain variable region (IGHV) to determine if it is mutated or unmutated [ashpublications.org](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/146/Supplement%201/8077/554647/Real-world-chronic-lymphocytic-leukemia-CLL) [cllsociety.org](https://cllsociety.org/cll-sll-patient-education-toolkit/test-before-treat-handout/). "Unmutated" IGHV is considered a higher-risk feature [ashpublications.org](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/146/Supplement%201/8077/554647/Real-world-chronic-lymphocytic-leukemia-CLL). Because IGHV mutation status almost never changes, it typically only needs to be tested once, usually at or shortly after diagnosis [cllsociety.org](https://cllsociety.org/cll-sll-patient-education-toolkit/test-before-treat-handout/) [cancercare.org](https://media.cancercare.org/publications/original/419-2020_CLL_Whitepaper.pdf).

**How Biomarkers Guide Therapy Selection**
Patients who have high-risk genomic features—specifically the presence of a *TP53* mutation, a 17p deletion, or an unmutated IGHV—generally have poor outcomes with traditional chemoimmunotherapy.
