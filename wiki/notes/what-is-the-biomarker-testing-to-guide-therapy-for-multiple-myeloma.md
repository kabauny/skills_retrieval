# What is the biomarker testing to guide therapy for multiple myeloma?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-08.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

Biomarker testing in multiple myeloma is used to guide the selection of targeted therapies, assess risk, and evaluate the depth of treatment response. The primary approaches include therapeutic targets, cytogenetic testing, and sensitive disease burden monitoring. 

**Therapeutic Targets**
*   **B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA):** BCMA is a cell surface protein biomarker that is highly expressed on multiple myeloma cells [[b-cell-maturation-antigen]]. As a critical therapeutic target, it guides the use of several novel and highly effective therapies in the second-line and relapsed/refractory settings, including:
    *   **CAR T-cell therapies:** Personalized treatments like [[ciltacabtagene-autoleucel]] [[ciltacabtagene-autoleucel]].
    *   **Bispecific antibodies:** Therapies such as teclistamab, which demonstrated progression-free and overall survival benefits in the [[majestec-3-trial]] [[majestec-3-trial]].
    *   **Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs):** "Off-the-shelf" targeted agents like [[belantamab-mafodotin]] [[belantamab-mafodotin]].

**Cytogenetic and Genomic Risk Stratification**
*   **FISH (Fluorescence In Situ Hybridization) and NGS:** Testing is conducted on bone marrow biopsy samples—specifically using CD138-enriched plasma cells—to map out genetic and chromosomal alterations [search-sourced]. 
    *   Identifying abnormalities such as deletion 17p, t(4;14), t(14;16), and 1q21 amplification helps classify the myeloma as standard or high risk, which directly guides risk-adapted treatment strategies [search-sourced]. 
    *   Because the disease evolves, repeating cytogenetic testing via FISH or Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) is highly recommended at relapse to uncover newly acquired mutations that could influence subsequent treatment options [search-sourced].

**Response Monitoring and Disease Burden Biomarkers**
*   **Minimal Residual Disease (MRD) Testing:** Used to evaluate the depth of remission, MRD is a highly valuable prognostic biomarker in multiple myeloma [search-sourced]. It is typically assessed via bone marrow samples utilizing Next-Generation Flow (NGF) cytometry or NGS, detecting as few as one cancer cell in 1,000,000 normal cells [search-sourced]. While molecular MRD testing via [[circulating-tumor-dna]] remains strictly observational for guiding therapy escalation in solid tumors (such as breast cancer) [[mrd-guided-therapy-escalation]], in multiple myeloma, MRD negativity is actively utilized as a major prognostic marker and an evolving surrogate endpoint for overall survival [search-sourced].
*   **Mass Spectrometry ("Mass Spec"):** This is a highly sensitive blood test that detects and precisely measures trace amounts of monoclonal (M) protein [search-sourced]. It offers deeper and earlier insights into treatment response and disease activity compared to conventional blood tracking methods [search-sourced].
