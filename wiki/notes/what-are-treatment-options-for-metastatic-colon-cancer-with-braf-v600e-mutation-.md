# What are treatment options for metastatic colon cancer with BRAF V600E mutation who have progressed on FOLFOX + dabrafen

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

For a patient with BRAF V600E-mutated metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) who has progressed on FOLFOX (an oxaliplatin-based chemotherapy) combined with dabrafenib (a BRAF inhibitor) and trametinib (a MEK inhibitor), treatment options depend on their complete molecular profile (such as mismatch repair status) and sequential therapy guidelines. 

Treatment options include:

**1. Irinotecan-based Chemotherapy (e.g., FOLFIRI)**
Because the patient has already progressed on an oxaliplatin-based regimen (FOLFOX), switching the chemotherapy backbone to an irinotecan-based regimen like FOLFIRI is a standard next step [search-sourced]. This is typically combined with an anti-VEGF biologic such as bevacizumab, ramucirumab, or aflibercept [search-sourced].

**2. Immunotherapy (if MSI-H / dMMR)**
Treatment is highly dependent on whether the tumor is microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) or mismatch repair-deficient (dMMR) [[how-do-you-treat-braf-v600e-mutated-metastatic-colorectal-cancer]]. If the patient has an MSI-H/dMMR tumor and has not yet received immunotherapy, immune checkpoint inhibitors such as pembrolizumab monotherapy or nivolumab combined with ipilimumab are strongly recommended. These tumors form neoantigens and are highly responsive to upfront or subsequent immunotherapy [[how-do-you-treat-braf-v600e-mutated-metastatic-colorectal-cancer]].

**3. Encorafenib plus Cetuximab (BEACON Regimen)**
For patients with previously treated BRAF V600E mCRC, the landmark BEACON trial established the combination of the BRAF inhibitor encorafenib and the anti-EGFR antibody cetuximab as the optimal standard-of-care second-line treatment [[how-do-you-treat-braf-v600e-mutated-metastatic-colorectal-cancer]]. While this patient has already progressed on a BRAF and MEK inhibitor (dabrafenib/trametinib), BRAF inhibition in colorectal cancer often fails due to a powerful EGFR-mediated feedback loop [search-sourced]. Because their prior dabrafenib/trametinib regimen lacked an anti-EGFR agent, an EGFR-inhibitor-containing combination like encorafenib plus cetuximab may still be considered to overcome this bypass pathway, or it may be utilized in the context of targeted rechallenge [search-sourced]. 

**4. Late-Line Salvage Therapies**
If standard chemotherapy and targeted regimens fail, the patient would be eligible for later-line agents approved for treatment-refractory mCRC:
*   **Trifluridine/tipiracil (TAS-102):** An oral chemotherapy combination used to treat refractory colorectal cancer [[trifluridine-tipiracil]]. It is often administered in combination with bevacizumab to improve efficacy [search-sourced].
*   **Regorafenib:** An oral multi-kinase inhibitor that targets tumor angiogenesis and the microenvironment, approved for heavily pretreated mCRC [search-sourced].
*   **Fruquintinib:** A highly selective oral VEGF receptor inhibitor approved for late-line, refractory mCRC [search-sourced].

**5. Clinical Trials**
Given the aggressive nature of BRAF V600E mutations—which are a prognostic biomarker for poorer overall survival [[how-do-you-treat-braf-v600e-mutated-metastatic-colorectal-cancer]]—and the challenge of acquired resistance to MAPK pathway inhibitors, enrollment in a clinical trial is highly encouraged. Current trials are evaluating novel approaches for patients progressing on BRAF inhibitors, such as combinations involving ERK/SHP2 inhibitors or adding checkpoint inhibitors (like nivolumab) to BRAF/EGFR-targeted backbones [search-sourced].
