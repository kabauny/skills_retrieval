# What is the first-line treatment for metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

For patients with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC), the foundational step in first-line treatment is the continuation of **androgen deprivation therapy (ADT)** to maintain serum testosterone at castrate levels (< 50 ng/dL). 

Beyond continuing ADT, the choice of first-line systemic therapy depends on the patient's symptoms, sites of metastasis, prior treatments (such as whether they received certain therapies in the castration-sensitive setting), and specific biomarkers. The primary first-line additions to ADT include:

**1. Androgen Receptor Pathway Inhibitors (ARPIs)**
Novel hormonal therapies are frequently favored as first-line options due to their favorable safety and side-effect profiles compared to chemotherapy. Standard choices include:
*   **Abiraterone acetate**, administered with oral prednisone.
*   **Enzalutamide**. 

**2. Chemotherapy**
Taxane-based chemotherapy is a mainstay for first-line treatment, particularly for patients with rapidly progressing or highly symptomatic disease:
*   **Docetaxel** (given with prednisone) is a preferred first-line treatment. 
*   **Cabazitaxel** is also utilized and supported by network meta-analyses as a highly effective first-line or subsequent option.

**3. Immunotherapy**
*   **Sipuleucel-T**, an autologous cellular immunotherapy, is recommended for first-line treatment in patients who are asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic, have no liver metastases, and have an estimated life expectancy of greater than 6 months.

**4. Radiopharmaceuticals**
*   **Radium-223** may be offered as a first-line option for patients who have symptomatic bone metastases and no known visceral metastases. 

**5. Targeted Therapies (PARP Inhibitors)**
*   Newer guidelines have incorporated the use of **PARP inhibitors** (such as talazoparib combined with enzalutamide) in the first-line setting for mCRPC patients, particularly those harboring DNA repair pathway alterations (e.g., BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations). 

*(Note: The targeted radioligand Lutetium-177-PSMA-617 is typically reserved for patients who have already been treated with at least one prior ARPI and one prior line of taxane chemotherapy, rather than as a first-line treatment.)*

Optimal treatment selection is guided by shared decision-making, weighing the complementary mechanisms of action, patient comorbidities, and potential toxicities.
