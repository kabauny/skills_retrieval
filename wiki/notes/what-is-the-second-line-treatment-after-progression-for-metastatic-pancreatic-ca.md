# What is the second-line treatment after progression for metastatic pancreatic cancer?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-08.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

The second-line treatment for metastatic pancreatic cancer after progression relies heavily on what chemotherapy regimen was used in the first line, as well as the patient's specific genetic and molecular tumor profile. 

### Standard Chemotherapy Options
The standard clinical approach involves switching between fluoropyrimidine-based and gemcitabine-based regimens once the cancer progresses [search-sourced]:
*   **Progression after Gemcitabine-Based Therapy:** If a patient received a first-line gemcitabine regimen (such as gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel), the preferred second-line treatment is typically a fluoropyrimidine-based regimen [search-sourced]. The combination of 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) and leucovorin with nanoliposomal irinotecan (nal-IRI) is the standard FDA-approved option [search-sourced]. If nal-IRI is unavailable, 5-FU combined with standard irinotecan (FOLFIRI) or oxaliplatin (FOLFOX) can be used [search-sourced].
*   **Progression after Fluoropyrimidine-Based Therapy:** If a patient received a first-line fluoropyrimidine regimen (such as FOLFIRINOX or NALIRIFOX), the standard second-line therapy is typically gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel, or gemcitabine monotherapy for patients who cannot tolerate combination therapy [search-sourced].

### Biomarker-Directed Targeted Therapies and Immunotherapy
ASCO guidelines strongly recommend routine somatic and germline testing for all metastatic pancreatic cancer patients to identify candidates who could benefit from targeted treatments [[with-pmids-what-are-some-updates-with-pancreatic-cancer-that-was-recently-publis]]:
*   **MSI-H/dMMR:** Patients with high microsatellite instability (MSI-H) or mismatch repair deficiency (dMMR) are candidates for immunotherapy with checkpoint inhibitors like pembrolizumab [search-sourced] [[with-pmids-what-are-some-updates-with-pancreatic-cancer-that-was-recently-publis]].
*   ***BRCA* & *NTRK* Alterations:** Patients harboring *BRCA* mutations may be treated with PARP inhibitors like olaparib, while those with *NTRK* gene fusions can receive TRK inhibitors [[with-pmids-what-are-some-updates-with-pancreatic-cancer-that-was-recently-publis]].

### Recent Advancements in Second-Line Treatment (2024–2026)
Recent breakthroughs highlighted at ASCO meetings have introduced promising new options for pre-treated metastatic pancreatic cancer:
*   **Daraxonrasib (RAS(ON) Inhibitors):** At the ASCO 2026 Annual Meeting, the Phase 3 RASolute 302 trial showed that daraxonrasib—a multi-selective RAS(ON) inhibitor—nearly doubled survival time compared to standard chemotherapy in patients with pre-treated metastatic pancreatic cancer, marking a landscape-changing update for second-line therapy [search-sourced] [[with-pmids-what-are-some-updates-with-pancreatic-cancer-that-was-recently-publis]].
*   **MEK Inhibitor Combinations:** Following the 2026 ASCO Gastrointestinal Cancers Symposium, evaluations of genomically matched combinatorial therapies using MEK inhibitors showed efficacy in patients harboring specific *KRAS* alterations (G12R, G12D, and G12V) [[with-pmids-what-are-some-updates-with-pancreatic-cancer-that-was-recently-publis]].
*   **3rd-to-6th Line Immunotherapy:** For heavily pre-treated patients, the QUILT-88 trial (ASCO 2025) demonstrated a landmark survival benefit when treating patients with ANKTIVA (an IL-15 superagonist) alongside CAR-NK therapy, effectively reversing treatment-induced lymphopenia [[with-pmids-what-are-some-updates-with-pancreatic-cancer-that-was-recently-publis]].
