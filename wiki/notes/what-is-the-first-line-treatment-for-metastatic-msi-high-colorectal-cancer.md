# What is the first-line treatment for metastatic MSI-high colorectal cancer?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

The first-line standard of care for metastatic microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) or mismatch repair-deficient (dMMR) colorectal cancer is immunotherapy [search-sourced]. Because these specific tumors have a high probability of neoantigen formation, they respond very well to immune checkpoint inhibitors, which have largely replaced traditional chemotherapy as the optimal upfront treatment [search-sourced]. 

The primary first-line treatment options include:

*   **Pembrolizumab (Keytruda):** This anti-PD-1 monotherapy is widely considered a standard-of-care first-line treatment and was FDA-approved for this indication in 2020 [search-sourced]. Its approval was based on the Phase III KEYNOTE-177 trial, which demonstrated that pembrolizumab significantly improves progression-free survival compared to standard chemotherapy regimens [search-sourced].
*   **Nivolumab (Opdivo) plus Ipilimumab (Yervoy):** The combination of these two immunotherapy drugs is another highly effective, FDA-approved first-line option [search-sourced]. Data from clinical studies like CheckMate 142 and the Phase III CheckMate 8HW trial have underscored that this dual-immunotherapy approach is highly promising, slowing cancer growth and proving superior to chemotherapy for newly diagnosed MSI-H/dMMR metastatic disease [search-sourced].

**Emerging Strategies:**
While standalone immunotherapy is the current standard, recent data from the Phase III COMMIT trial has shown that a "triple-punch" combination of the immunotherapy drug atezolizumab alongside standard chemotherapy (FOLFOX) and targeted therapy (bevacizumab) yields superior progression-free survival compared to atezolizumab alone, marking it as a highly promising potential new first-line treatment option [search-sourced].
