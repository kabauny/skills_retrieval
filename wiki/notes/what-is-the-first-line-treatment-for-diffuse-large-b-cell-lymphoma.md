# What is the first-line treatment for diffuse large B-cell lymphoma?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

The first-line treatment for diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) typically consists of chemoimmunotherapy, which combines an anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody with chemotherapy [search-sourced]. 

The primary recommended regimens for fit patients include:
* **R-CHOP**: For many years, this has been the traditional standard of care. It consists of the drugs **R**ituximab, **C**yclophosphamide, **D**oxorubicin (hydroxydaunorubicin), **V**incristine (Oncovin), and **P**rednisone [search-sourced].
* **Pola-R-CHP**: This is a newer front-line option approved for patients with previously untreated DLBCL, typically those with advanced-stage disease or an International Prognostic Index (IPI) score of 2 or higher [search-sourced]. In this regimen, the targeted antibody-drug conjugate polatuzumab vedotin replaces vincristine [search-sourced]. 

Current major clinical guidelines (such as those from the NCCN and ESMO) recognize both R-CHOP and Pola-R-CHP as the primary, frontline backbone options for fit patients [search-sourced].

Treatment approaches may also be adapted based on the patient's age, fitness level, and disease stage:
* **Elderly or Frail Patients**: Individuals who are unfit to tolerate full-dose chemotherapy are often treated with reduced-intensity regimens, such as **R-mini-CHOP**, or other approaches designed to minimize chemotherapy toxicity [search-sourced].
* **Localized/Early-Stage Disease**: For localized DLBCL, standard treatment generally involves a course of chemoimmunotherapy, which may be utilized alone or followed by localized radiation therapy (such as involved-site radiation therapy, or ISRT) [search-sourced].

Additionally, while novel therapies like CAR-T cell therapy and bispecific antibodies are widely approved for relapsed or refractory DLBCL, ongoing clinical trials are currently evaluating their use as first-line treatments for high-risk patients [search-sourced].
