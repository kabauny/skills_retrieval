# What is the managing acquired resistance for small cell lung cancer?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-08.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

First-line treatment for small cell lung cancer (SCLC) typically yields high initial response rates, but almost all patients inevitably develop acquired resistance and experience disease relapse [search-sourced] [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/EDBK-26-524964). The management of acquired resistance in SCLC has advanced significantly in recent years with the introduction of novel immunotherapies and targeted agents [search-sourced] [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/EDBK-26-524964).

**Evolving Guidelines and Treatment Selection**
Historically, the choice of second-line therapy for relapsed SCLC relied heavily on the "chemotherapy-free interval" (CTFI) to classify a tumor as either platinum-sensitive (relapse occurring >6 months after treatment) or platinum-resistant (relapse occurring <6 months after treatment) [search-sourced] [mdlinx.com](https://www.mdlinx.com/article/the-new-second-line-playbook-after-platinum-based-therapy-in-relapsed-sclc/5Fvqb2PrJgIBX7Khf5kCl0). However, recent updates to the 2026 National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines have removed the strict CTFI cutoff from the subsequent-line therapy algorithm [search-sourced] [pharmacytimes.com](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/nccn-guideline-update-in-sclc-adds-maintenance-lurbinectedin-removes-chemotherapy-free-interval) [mdlinx.com](https://www.mdlinx.com/article/the-new-second-line-playbook-after-platinum-based-therapy-in-relapsed-sclc/5Fvqb2PrJgIBX7Khf5kCl0). Instead, therapy selection is now guided by clinical expertise, emerging clinical trial data, and the patient's ability to tolerate specific toxicity profiles [search-sourced] [mdlinx.com](https://www.mdlinx.com/article/relapsed-sclc-in-the-real-world-choosing-second-line-therapy-when-toxicity-and-dosing-drive-the/6K1HCc9V2dL4myGcfHcJQR) [mdlinx.com](https://www.mdlinx.com/article/the-new-second-line-playbook-after-platinum-based-therapy-in-relapsed-sclc/5Fvqb2PrJgIBX7Khf5kCl0).

**Current Options for Managing Relapsed/Resistant SCLC**

*   **Tarlatamab:** Tarlatamab is a novel DLL3-directed bispecific T-cell engager that has emerged as a new standard of care for patients whose disease progresses on or after platinum-based chemotherapy [search-sourced] [esmo.org](https://www.esmo.org/oncology-news/tarlatamab-prolongs-os-over-chemotherapy-among-patients-with-sclc-whose-disease-had-progressed-during-or-after-platinum-based-chemotherapy) [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/EDBK-26-524964). The Phase 3 DeLLphi-304 trial demonstrated that tarlatamab provides a significant overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) benefit when compared to standard chemotherapy in the relapsed setting [search-sourced] [esmo.org](https://www.esmo.org/oncology-news/tarlatamab-prolongs-os-over-chemotherapy-among-patients-with-sclc-whose-disease-had-progressed-during-or-after-platinum-based-chemotherapy) [outcomes4me.com](https://outcomes4me.com/cancer-topic/in-treatment-for-small-cell-lung-cancer/small-cell-lung-cancer-update-from-the-2026-asco-annual-meeting/). Its toxicity profile differs greatly from traditional chemotherapy; clinicians must primarily monitor for and manage cytokine release syndrome (CRS) and immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS), while it generally spares patients from severe cytopenias [search-sourced] [mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6694/18/11/1806) [mdlinx.com](https://www.mdlinx.com/article/relapsed-sclc-in-the-real-world-choosing-second-line-therapy-when-toxicity-and-dosing-drive-the/6K1HCc9V2dL4myGcfHcJQR).
*   **Lurbinectedin:** Lurbinectedin is another established second-line agent for relapsed SCLC [search-sourced] [pharmacytimes.com](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/lurbinectedin-and-tarlatamab-emerging-second-line-therapies-in-the-sclc-space) [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/EDBK-26-524964). Unlike tarlatamab, its primary adverse events are hematologic, commonly causing severe cytopenias such as anemia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia [search-sourced] [mdlinx.com](https://www.mdlinx.com/article/relapsed-sclc-in-the-real-world-choosing-second-line-therapy-when-toxicity-and-dosing-drive-the/6K1HCc9V2dL4myGcfHcJQR). Recent data from the IMforte trial also established its role earlier in the treatment paradigm as a first-line maintenance therapy in combination with atezolizumab to delay the onset of resistance [search-sourced] [pharmacytimes.com](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/nccn-guideline-update-in-sclc-adds-maintenance-lurbinectedin-removes-chemotherapy-free-interval).
*   **Traditional Chemotherapy (Topotecan or Amrubicin):** Topotecan served as the historical standard second-line treatment for relapsed SCLC for decades [search-sourced] [esmo.org](https://www.esmo.org/oncology-news/tarlatamab-prolongs-os-over-chemotherapy-among-patients-with-sclc-whose-disease-had-progressed-during-or-after-platinum-based-chemotherapy). While it remains an available option, it is increasingly less favored due to its notable hematologic toxicities and the superior efficacy data of newer agents [search-sourced] [esmo.org](https://www.esmo.org/oncology-news/tarlatamab-prolongs-os-over-chemotherapy-among-patients-with-sclc-whose-disease-had-progressed-during-or-after-platinum-based-chemotherapy). Amrubicin is also utilized in select regions [search-sourced] [esmo.org](https://www.esmo.org/oncology-news/tarlatamab-prolongs-os-over-chemotherapy-among-patients-with-sclc-whose-disease-had-progressed-during-or-after-platinum-based-chemotherapy). 
*   **Platinum Rechallenge:** For patients who achieve a prolonged treatment-free interval (traditionally considered greater than 3 to 6 months) before acquiring resistance, rechallenging the tumor with the original platinum-based chemotherapy regimen remains a viable and utilized clinical strategy [search-sourced] [mdlinx.com](https://www.mdlinx.com/article/relapsed-sclc-in-the-real-world-choosing-second-line-therapy-when-toxicity-and-dosing-drive-the/6K1HCc9V2dL4myGcfHcJQR).
