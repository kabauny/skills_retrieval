# How do you treat HER2-positive metastatic gastric or gastroesophageal cancer?

> ✅ **Verified by jim.chen on 2026-06-07.** Auto-ingested from an internet search; reviewed and confirmed.

The treatment landscape for HER2-positive metastatic gastric or gastroesophageal junction (GEJ) cancer relies heavily on HER2-targeted therapies combined with chemotherapy and, depending on biomarkers, immunotherapy. According to the latest 2026 NCCN and oncology guidelines, treatment is structured by the line of therapy.

**First-Line Treatment**
*   **Standard Backbone:** The standard initial regimen generally consists of a fluoropyrimidine and platinum-based chemotherapy (e.g., FOLFOX or CAPOX) combined with **trastuzumab**. Trastuzumab is a monoclonal antibody that targets the HER2 receptor and forms the backbone of HER2-targeted therapy [[trastuzumab]]. 
*   **Immunotherapy Integration:** Based on the KEYNOTE-811 trial, adding the immune checkpoint inhibitor **pembrolizumab** to the trastuzumab-chemotherapy backbone is the new standard of care, particularly for patients whose tumors express PD-L1 (typically a combined positive score [CPS] ≥ 1). For patients who are PD-L1 negative, trastuzumab and chemotherapy alone remain standard.
*   *Note on Dual Blockade:* While adding **pertuzumab** [[pertuzumab]] to trastuzumab and chemotherapy is standard for HER2-positive breast cancer, it is *not* used in metastatic gastric/GEJ cancer. The Phase 3 JACOB trial investigated this but failed to demonstrate a significant overall survival benefit, meaning pertuzumab has no approved role in this setting. 

**Second-Line Treatment**
*   **Trastuzumab Deruxtecan (T-DXd / Enhertu):** If the cancer progresses after first-line trastuzumab-based therapy, **trastuzumab deruxtecan** has emerged as the preferred second-line targeted option. It demonstrated significant progression-free and overall survival benefits over standard paclitaxel/ramucirumab in the DESTINY-Gastric04 trial. 
*   **Mechanism & Toxicity:** T-DXd is an antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) linking trastuzumab to a topoisomerase-1 inhibitor payload via a cleavable linker, giving it potent activity via a bystander effect [[trastuzumab-deruxtecan]]. Because T-DXd carries a risk of potentially severe interstitial lung disease (ILD/pneumonitis), strict baseline pulmonary assessment and vigilance for new respiratory symptoms remain essential [[trastuzumab-deruxtecan]].
*   **HER2 Loss:** HER2 expression can be lost in 20–40% of patients following first-line trastuzumab therapy. Therefore, it is highly recommended to perform a repeat biopsy to confirm the tumor is still HER2-positive before initiating T-DXd.
*   *Alternative regimens:* If the patient's tumor loses HER2 expression or if T-DXd is contraindicated, standard non-HER2 options like paclitaxel plus ramucirumab are utilized. 

**Agents Not Established for Gastric Cancer**
Other HER2-targeted therapies like **tucatinib** [[tucatinib]] are strictly established for advanced HER2-positive breast cancer. Trials such as HER2CLIMB-05 [[her2climb-05]] investigate tucatinib as a first-line maintenance therapy in metastatic breast cancer; tucatinib has no current standard role in metastatic gastric or GEJ cancer.
