# How do you treat BRAF V600E-mutated metastatic colorectal cancer?

The treatment of BRAF V600E-mutated metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) is highly personalized and depends significantly on comprehensive biomarker testing—specifically the tumor's mismatch repair (MMR) or microsatellite instability (MSI) status [[which-biomarkers-should-be-tested-in-metastatic-colorectal-cancer-to-guide-thera]]. 

The BRAF V600E mutation is a prognostic biomarker indicating poorer overall survival, as well as a predictive biomarker used to select patients for specific BRAF-targeted inhibitor regimens [[which-biomarkers-should-be-tested-in-metastatic-colorectal-cancer-to-guide-thera]]. Depending on the complete molecular profile, treatment generally follows these pathways:

**1. MSI-High or dMMR Tumors**
Testing for MSI-high (MSI-H) or mismatch repair-deficient (dMMR) status is critical because these tumors form neoantigens and are highly responsive to immunotherapy [[which-biomarkers-should-be-tested-in-metastatic-colorectal-cancer-to-guide-thera]]. 
*   **First-line therapy:** For patients with dMMR/MSI-H status, immunotherapy has replaced traditional chemotherapy as the optimal upfront approach [[which-biomarkers-should-be-tested-in-metastatic-colorectal-cancer-to-guide-thera]]. Guidelines recommend first-line immune checkpoint inhibitors, such as pembrolizumab monotherapy or a combination of nivolumab and ipilimumab [[which-biomarkers-should-be-tested-in-metastatic-colorectal-cancer-to-guide-thera]]. 
*   **Emerging targeted combinations:** Recent clinical trials, such as the SEAMARK trial, have shown promising results in combining pembrolizumab with encorafenib (a BRAF inhibitor) and cetuximab (an anti-EGFR antibody) specifically for dMMR tumors bearing the BRAF V600E mutation [search-sourced].

**2. MSS or pMMR (Microsatellite Stable) Tumors**
For patients with proficient MMR (pMMR) or microsatellite stable (MSS) tumors, standard therapy involves targeted therapy regimens and/or intensive chemotherapy [search-sourced].
*   **First-line therapy:** Based on the phase III BREAKWATER trial, a new standard of care was established for treatment-naïve patients [search-sourced]. The FDA has approved the use of encorafenib and cetuximab in combination with fluorouracil-based chemotherapy (such as mFOLFOX6 or FOLFIRI) [search-sourced]. Alternatively, traditional intensive chemotherapy combined with bevacizumab (such as FOLFOXIRI plus bevacizumab) remains a viable option for patients with a good performance status [search-sourced].
*   **Second-line therapy:** For patients who received traditional standard chemotherapy without BRAF-targeted agents in the first line, the combination of encorafenib and cetuximab is recommended as the optimal second-line treatment option, supported by data from the landmark BEACON trial [search-sourced].
