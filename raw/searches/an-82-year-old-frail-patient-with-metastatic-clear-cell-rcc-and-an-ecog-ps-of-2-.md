# An 82-year-old frail patient with metastatic clear cell RCC and an ECOG PS of 2 develops grade 3 fatigue and significant transaminitis after 2 cycles of first-line nivolumab plus ipilimumab. His disease is stable. Should both agents be held and restarted at lower doses once toxicities resolve, or should ipilimumab be discontinued with nivolumab monotherapy continued?

Based on clinical guidelines and the toxicity management principles for immune checkpoint inhibitors, the correct approach is to **hold both agents, treat the toxicity, permanently discontinue ipilimumab, and consider resuming nivolumab monotherapy once the toxicities resolve.** 

Here is the detailed rationale:

**1. Dose reductions are contraindicated for immune checkpoint inhibitors**
Unlike cytotoxic chemotherapy, immune-related adverse events (irAEs) are not managed with dose reductions [[immune-related-adverse-events]]. Immune toxicities are caused by off-target T-cell activation, meaning that restarting therapy at a lower dose is ineffective and inappropriate for managing the immune response [[immune-related-adverse-events]]. Instead, management relies on holding the drug and administering immunosuppression (such as corticosteroids) to calm the immune system [[immune-related-adverse-events]].

**2. Management of Grade 3 Transaminitis (Hepatitis)**
The patient's Grade 3 transaminitis requires immediately holding both nivolumab and ipilimumab and initiating high-dose systemic corticosteroids (e.g., prednisone 1–2 mg/kg/day or an intravenous equivalent) [[immune-related-adverse-events]]. Once the transaminitis resolves to baseline or Grade ≤1 and the patient has successfully tapered off high-dose steroids, the ipilimumab component must be permanently discontinued due to its contribution to severe toxicity [search-sourced]. However, guidelines and clinical trial protocols broadly support safely restarting and continuing nivolumab as monotherapy [search-sourced]. 

**3. Evaluation of Grade 3 Fatigue**
While the transaminitis cleanly dictates the discontinuation of ipilimumab, the Grade 3 fatigue warrants its own diagnostic workup. Severe fatigue in a patient on combination immunotherapy is a classic presentation of immune-mediated endocrinopathies, such as adrenal insufficiency, hypophysitis, or hypothyroidism [search-sourced]. Unlike immune-mediated hepatitis, endocrinopathies are often irreversible and managed with lifelong hormone replacement rather than immunosuppression; once the endocrine deficiency is stabilized, immunotherapy can generally be continued [[immune-related-adverse-events]].

In summary, you should not restart the regimen at lower doses. The patient should be treated with corticosteroids for the hepatitis, evaluated for underlying adrenal or thyroid dysfunction, and transitioned to nivolumab monotherapy once the irAEs have fully resolved [search-sourced].
