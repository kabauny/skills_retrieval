# What is the second-line treatment after progression for acute myeloid leukemia?

The second-line treatment for acute myeloid leukemia (AML) after disease progression (relapsed or refractory AML) depends heavily on the patient's specific genetic mutations, age, fitness level, and previous treatments received. Because the disease's mutation status can evolve or emerge over time, clinical guidelines strongly recommend comprehensive molecular testing at each disease relapse or progression to determine the best therapeutic approach [[how-do-you-treat-acute-myeloid-leukemia-with-flt3-mutations]] [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12235439/).

Based on National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines and current FDA approvals, second-line treatment options fall into the following categories:

### 1. Targeted Therapies (Mutation-Specific)
For patients whose relapsed or refractory disease harbors specific genetic mutations, targeted therapies are prioritized [nccn.org](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/aml-patient.pdf) [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12235439/).
*   **FLT3 Mutations (ITD or TKD):** Gilteritinib is heavily prioritized and is FDA-approved as a single-agent therapy (monotherapy) for adult patients with relapsed or refractory FLT3-mutated AML [[how-do-you-treat-acute-myeloid-leukemia-with-flt3-mutations]]. Other options may include quizartinib or a combination of hypomethylating agents (HMAs) with sorafenib [nccn.org](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/aml-patient.pdf).
*   **IDH1 Mutations:** Ivosidenib or olutasidenib are recommended targeted therapies [nccn.org](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/aml-patient.pdf) [prnewswire.com](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/national-comprehensive-cancer-network-adds-newly-approved-rezlidhia-olutasidenib-to-clinical-practice-guidelines-in-oncology-for-acute-myeloid-leukemia-301724233.html).
*   **IDH2 Mutations:** Enasidenib is FDA-approved for adults with relapsed/refractory AML with an IDH2 mutation [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7521119/).
*   **NPM1 or KMT2A Mutations:** Newly updated NCCN guidelines include the menin inhibitors ziftomenib and revumenib as preferred targeted therapies for relapsed or refractory AML harboring these specific mutations [oncnursingnews.com](https://www.oncnursingnews.com/view/nccn-recommends-revumenib-for-r-r-npm1-acute-myeloid-leukemia) [onclive.com](https://www.onclive.com/view/ziftomenib-added-to-nccn-clinical-practice-guidelines-for-npm1-mutant-r-r-aml).

### 2. Salvage Chemotherapy (For patients without actionable mutations)
If a patient does not have a targetable mutation, or if targeted therapy is inappropriate, the choice of chemotherapy depends on the patient's fitness to tolerate intensive treatment:
*   **Intensive Salvage Regimens (Fit Patients):** Patients who can tolerate aggressive therapy may receive high-intensity combination chemotherapy. Common salvage regimens include FLAG-IDA (fludarabine, cytarabine, G-CSF, and idarubicin), MEC (mitoxantrone, etoposide, and cytarabine), high-dose cytarabine (HDAC), or CLIA (cladribine, idarubicin, and cytarabine) [onclive.com](https://www.onclive.com/view/experts-unpack-the-most-notable-nccn-guideline-changes-heading-into-2026) [cancer.ca](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/acute-myeloid-leukemia-aml/treatment/relapsed-or-refractory). Venetoclax may also be added to regimens like FLAG-IDA or CLIA [onclive.com](https://www.onclive.com/view/experts-unpack-the-most-notable-nccn-guideline-changes-heading-into-2026).
*   **Lower-Intensity Regimens (Older or Unfit Patients):** For those unable to tolerate intensive chemotherapy, treatment typically consists of hypomethylating agents (HMAs) like azacitidine or decitabine combined with venetoclax (if this combination was not already used in the first-line setting) [mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/14/15/5586) [canjhealthtechnol.ca](https://canjhealthtechnol.ca/index.php/cjht/article/download/pc0238r/348?inline=1).

### 3. Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant (HSCT)
For patients who achieve a second complete remission using either targeted therapies or salvage chemotherapy, proceeding to an allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplant (alloSCT) is frequently recommended as the best curative option to achieve long-term survival [ashpublications.org](https://ashpublications.org/bloodadvances/article/9/15/3853/537378/A-revised-prognostic-model-for-patients-with-acute) [cancer.ca](https://cancer.ca/en/cancer-information/cancer-types/acute-myeloid-leukemia-aml/treatment/relapsed-or-refractory).

### 4. Clinical Trials and Best Supportive Care
Across all subgroups, participating in a clinical trial is highly recommended by NCCN guidelines for relapsed or refractory AML [nccn.org](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/aml-patient.pdf). For patients who are not candidates for further treatment or who do not respond to second-line therapies, best supportive care (which focuses on symptom relief and quality of life without active cancer treatment) is utilized [geriatrikhematoloji.org](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFN5W8f9abwW6H1CXXaQIruhWIjCvlb3SUhWm3tEJVBw3SfSqzf0IPpg4Y2t1ENg52kGTjXDfg_r11LhWYl-5CvpjMRloUmNVc39mIYshEl_vFgCEGQLwVibL71IXXHbQ3rX5DwlAEIDrNEgLvv09E5FSBBYT9PFsdiD6I56-M0EwxYe9v4HsAo2bsnxUFO).
