# How do you treat ALK-positive metastatic NSCLC in the first line?

First-line treatment for ALK-positive metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) typically involves targeted therapy with an anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) [search-sourced] [droracle.ai](https://www.droracle.ai/articles/175429/what-is-the-recommended-first-line-treatment-for-a-patient). 

Current clinical guidelines from organizations such as the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) and the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) recommend next-generation ALK TKIs as preferred first-line options due to their superior efficacy and better central nervous system (CNS) penetration compared to older therapies [search-sourced] [droracle.ai](https://www.droracle.ai/articles/175429/what-is-the-recommended-first-line-treatment-for-a-patient) [esmo.org](https://www.esmo.org/guidelines/living-guidelines/esmo-living-guideline-oncogene-addicted-non-small-cell-lung-cancer/management-of-advanced-and-metastatic-disease/alk-translocation/stage-iv-mnsclc-with-alk-translocation-before-systemic-progression/alk-positive-nsclc-first-line-treatment).

The highly preferred first-line agents include:
* **Alectinib** [search-sourced] [harborsidestudio.com](https://sponsored.harborsidestudio.com/alk-positive-metastatic-nsclc-optimizing-therapy-in-the-first-line-setting/) [esmo.org](https://www.esmo.org/guidelines/living-guidelines/esmo-living-guideline-oncogene-addicted-non-small-cell-lung-cancer/management-of-advanced-and-metastatic-disease/alk-translocation/stage-iv-mnsclc-with-alk-translocation-before-systemic-progression/alk-positive-nsclc-first-line-treatment)
* **Brigatinib** [search-sourced] [harborsidestudio.com](https://sponsored.harborsidestudio.com/alk-positive-metastatic-nsclc-optimizing-therapy-in-the-first-line-setting/) [esmo.org](https://www.esmo.org/guidelines/living-guidelines/esmo-living-guideline-oncogene-addicted-non-small-cell-lung-cancer/management-of-advanced-and-metastatic-disease/alk-translocation/stage-iv-mnsclc-with-alk-translocation-before-systemic-progression/alk-positive-nsclc-first-line-treatment)
* **Lorlatinib** [search-sourced] [harborsidestudio.com](https://sponsored.harborsidestudio.com/alk-positive-metastatic-nsclc-optimizing-therapy-in-the-first-line-setting/) [esmo.org](https://www.esmo.org/guidelines/living-guidelines/esmo-living-guideline-oncogene-addicted-non-small-cell-lung-cancer/management-of-advanced-and-metastatic-disease/alk-translocation/stage-iv-mnsclc-with-alk-translocation-before-systemic-progression/alk-positive-nsclc-first-line-treatment)

Additionally, **ensartinib** is recognized by some guidelines (such as ESMO) as a first-line option, though regional regulatory approvals (e.g., FDA vs. EMA) may vary [search-sourced] [harborsidestudio.com](https://sponsored.harborsidestudio.com/first-line-treatment-of-alk-positive-metastatic-nsclc/?utm_source=TAP&utm_medium=Website&utm_campaign=TAP_HP) [esmo.org](https://www.esmo.org/guidelines/living-guidelines/esmo-living-guideline-oncogene-addicted-non-small-cell-lung-cancer/management-of-advanced-and-metastatic-disease/alk-translocation/stage-iv-mnsclc-with-alk-translocation-before-systemic-progression/alk-positive-nsclc-first-line-treatment). First-generation (crizotinib) and other second-generation (ceritinib) TKIs are still listed as options in certain circumstances, but they are generally less preferred than alectinib, brigatinib, or lorlatinib [search-sourced] [cancernetwork.com](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/exploring-first-line-alk-inhibitors-tki-sequencing-in-alk-positive-nsclc) [esmo.org](https://www.esmo.org/guidelines/living-guidelines/esmo-living-guideline-oncogene-addicted-non-small-cell-lung-cancer/management-of-advanced-and-metastatic-disease/alk-translocation/stage-iv-mnsclc-with-alk-translocation-before-systemic-progression/alk-positive-nsclc-first-line-treatment). 

Lorlatinib, a third-generation ALK inhibitor, is particularly noted for its high intracranial activity and is explicitly preferred by ESMO for patients presenting with brain metastases [search-sourced] [esmo.org](https://www.esmo.org/guidelines/living-guidelines/esmo-living-guideline-oncogene-addicted-non-small-cell-lung-cancer/management-of-advanced-and-metastatic-disease/alk-translocation/stage-iv-mnsclc-with-alk-translocation-before-systemic-progression/alk-positive-nsclc-first-line-treatment). Recent long-term data from the phase 3 CROWN trial demonstrated unprecedented progression-free survival (PFS) benefits for lorlatinib in the first-line setting, with the median PFS not yet reached at the 5-year follow-up [search-sourced] [cancernetwork.com](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/exploring-first-line-alk-inhibitors-tki-sequencing-in-alk-positive-nsclc).

Guidelines also emphasize that comprehensive molecular testing for ALK fusions should ideally be completed prior to initiating first-line systemic therapy to ensure the proper targeted agent is selected [search-sourced] [harborsidestudio.com](https://sponsored.harborsidestudio.com/first-line-treatment-of-alk-positive-metastatic-nsclc/?utm_source=TAP&utm_medium=Website&utm_campaign=TAP_HP).
