# How do you treat newly diagnosed multiple myeloma in transplant-eligible patients?

The treatment of newly diagnosed multiple myeloma (NDMM) in transplant-eligible patients involves a multi-step, sequential approach consisting of induction therapy, an autologous stem cell transplant (ASCT), and long-term maintenance [search-sourced]. 

**1. Induction Therapy**
Recent updates to the NCCN guidelines (2024–2026) have established quadruplet therapy as the standard of care for induction [search-sourced]. The preferred primary regimen is **D-VRd**, which combines four drugs:
*   **Daratumumab** (an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody)
*   **Bortezomib** (a proteasome inhibitor)
*   **Lenalidomide** (an immunomodulatory drug)
*   **Dexamethasone** (a corticosteroid) [search-sourced].

The shift to quadruplet therapy is heavily supported by the results of the phase 3 PERSEUS and phase 2 GRIFFIN clinical trials, which demonstrated that adding daratumumab to the standard VRd backbone significantly deepens response rates, achieves durable minimal residual disease (MRD) negativity, and improves progression-free survival [search-sourced]. 

**2. Autologous Stem Cell Transplant (ASCT)**
Following induction therapy to rapidly reduce the tumor burden, eligible patients proceed to high-dose chemotherapy (typically melphalan) followed by ASCT [search-sourced]. High-dose melphalan supported by ASCT has remained a cornerstone of NDMM therapy for decades because it further solidifies the depth of the initial response [search-sourced].

**3. Consolidation and Maintenance Therapy**
After recovering from the stem cell transplant, patients generally undergo short-term consolidation therapy (often with the induction regimen) followed by continuous maintenance therapy [search-sourced]. Maintenance is designed to prevent disease progression and keep the myeloma in remission over the long term. Driven by recent clinical trial data, utilizing daratumumab combined with lenalidomide (or lenalidomide alone) is the new standard of care for maintenance in this population [search-sourced].

**Considerations for Relapsed Disease**
While frontline treatments leverage anti-CD38 antibodies, proteasome inhibitors, and immunomodulatory drugs, patients who eventually progress and become refractory to these medications (such as lenalidomide) transition to novel targeted therapies. At this stage, treatment heavily targets the B-cell maturation antigen (BCMA), a protein highly expressed on the surface of multiple myeloma cells [[b-cell-maturation-antigen]]. 

Key second-line and subsequent treatment options include:
*   **Bispecific Antibodies:** Teclistamab is a BCMA-targeted bispecific antibody that has shown substantial progression-free and overall survival benefits as early as the second-line setting, according to the MajesTEC-3 trial [[majestec-3-trial]].
*   **CAR T-cell Therapy:** Ciltacabtagene autoleucel (Carvykti) is a highly effective personalized cell therapy FDA-approved for early relapsed myeloma, specifically targeting patients refractory to lenalidomide and prior immunomodulatory/proteasome inhibitor therapies [[ciltacabtagene-autoleucel]]. 
*   **Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs):** Belantamab mafodotin (Blenrep) provides an "off-the-shelf" BCMA-targeted option [[belantamab-mafodotin]]. The 2024 DREAMM-7 trial firmly established its role as a highly effective second-line therapy, demonstrating that combining it with bortezomib and dexamethasone significantly improves overall and progression-free survival [[dreamm-7-trial]].
