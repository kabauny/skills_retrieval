# What is the managing acquired resistance for advanced gastric cancer?

Managing acquired resistance in advanced gastric cancer requires a highly personalized strategy tailored to the patient's overall performance status, prior treatments, and the tumor's biomarker profile (e.g., HER2, MSI-H/dMMR status) [[what-is-the-second-line-treatment-after-progression-for-advanced-gastric-cancer]]. Because first-line therapies eventually fail due to resistance, management heavily relies on shifting to second-line therapies and addressing new molecular targets [search-sourced].

### 1. General Chemotherapy and Anti-Angiogenic Strategies
For the majority of patients experiencing disease progression, the preferred standard-of-care regimen to manage resistance is the combination of ramucirumab (an anti-VEGFR2 monoclonal antibody) and weekly paclitaxel [[what-is-the-second-line-treatment-after-progression-for-advanced-gastric-cancer]].
*   **Alternative Regimens:** Major clinical guidelines also list FOLFIRI (fluorouracil plus irinotecan) as a preferred systemic option [[what-is-the-second-line-treatment-after-progression-for-advanced-gastric-cancer]]. 
*   **Monotherapies:** If patients cannot tolerate combination therapy, single-agent options such as ramucirumab, paclitaxel, docetaxel, or irinotecan are recommended [[what-is-the-second-line-treatment-after-progression-for-advanced-gastric-cancer]].

### 2. Managing Resistance in HER2-Positive Gastric Cancer
Acquired resistance to first-line trastuzumab therapies is common and often driven by mechanisms such as the loss of HER2 receptor expression, tumor heterogeneity, or the compensatory activation of alternative signaling pathways (like the PI3K/AKT, MAPK, and FGFR3 loops) [search-sourced].
*   **Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs):** To overcome these resistance pathways, clinical data from trials like DESTINY-Gastric04 established the targeted ADC trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) as the standard second-line treatment, demonstrating improved outcomes over ramucirumab plus paclitaxel for this population [[what-is-the-second-line-treatment-after-progression-for-advanced-gastric-cancer]].
*   **Investigational Therapies:** Researchers are also testing novel dual-HER blockade methods, including bispecific antibodies like zanidatamab or KN026, to achieve a more robust signaling blockade and overcome trastuzumab resistance [search-sourced].

### 3. Managing Resistance to Immunotherapy
For patients receiving immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), acquired resistance can develop through mechanisms like impaired antigen presentation (loss of HLA class I), T-cell exhaustion, a highly suppressive tumor microenvironment, or the activation of compensatory immune checkpoints (e.g., LAG-3, TIM-3, TIGIT, and VISTA) [search-sourced].
*   **MSI-H/dMMR Patients:** Patients with mismatch repair-deficient (dMMR) or microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) tumors who have not yet received immunotherapy during first-line treatment should be considered for checkpoint inhibitors at the time of progression [[what-is-the-second-line-treatment-after-progression-for-advanced-gastric-cancer]].
*   **Post-ICI Progression:** For those who progress after initial immunotherapy, management generally consists of pivoting to standard chemotherapy combinations (like ramucirumab plus paclitaxel). Furthermore, clinical trials are increasingly exploring ICI rechallenge in combination with targeted anti-angiogenic agents to help modulate the suppressive tumor microenvironment [search-sourced].

### 4. Molecular Profiling for Targeted Therapies
Because acquired resistance dynamically alters the tumor's genetic makeup, repeating molecular profiling at the time of disease progression is increasingly important to identify new targetable alterations [search-sourced]. This can inform management by enabling enrollment in clinical trials exploring precision therapies, such as alpelisib for acquired PIK3CA mutations or pemigatinib for newly identified FGFR2 fusions [search-sourced].
