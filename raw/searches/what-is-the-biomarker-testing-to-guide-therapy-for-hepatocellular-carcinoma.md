# What is the biomarker testing to guide therapy for hepatocellular carcinoma?

In hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the use of predictive biomarkers to guide systemic therapy is quite limited compared to other solid tumors. For most standard first-line therapies, routine biomarker testing is not required [nccn.org](https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/liver-hp-patient.pdf) [onclive.com](https://www.onclive.com/view/tislelizumab-pushes-hcc-field-forward-with-2024-nccn-guideline-category-1-recommendation). However, specific testing is recommended or available to guide certain second-line and tissue-agnostic treatments. 

Based on the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines and current oncological standards, the biomarker testing to guide therapy in HCC includes:

**1. Alpha-Fetoprotein (AFP) for Ramucirumab**
Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) is the most established predictive biomarker for selecting a specific targeted therapy in advanced HCC [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8039038/). 
*   **Ramucirumab:** Baseline serum AFP testing is used to determine eligibility for ramucirumab, a VEGFR-2 antagonist [onclive.com](https://www.onclive.com/view/ramucirumab-extends-survival-in-advanced-hcc). It is specifically FDA-approved and recommended by the NCCN as a second-line therapy for HCC patients who have progressed on or are intolerant to sorafenib, provided they have a baseline **AFP level of ≥ 400 ng/mL**.  [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8039038/) [onclive.com](https://www.onclive.com/view/ramucirumab-extends-survival-in-advanced-hcc) [targetedonc.com](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/ramucirumab-approved-by-fda-for-secondline-treatment-of-afpelevated-hcc)
*   Patients with AFP < 400 ng/mL do not derive a survival benefit from ramucirumab and should be offered alternative treatments.
 [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8039038/)
**2. Tissue-Agnostic Biomarkers for Immunotherapy**
While routine PD-L1 testing is not required or recommended to prescribe standard immunotherapies in HCC, comprehensive biomarker testing can identify candidates for tissue-agnostic immune checkpoint inhibitors:
 [frontiersin.org](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2021.803133/full) [nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32088930/)*   **Microsatellite Instability-High (MSI-H) or Mismatch Repair Deficiency (dMMR):** Testing for MSI-H/dMMR via immunohistochemistry (IHC) or polymerase chain reaction (PCR) can guide the use of PD-1 inhibitors like **pembrolizumab** or **dostarlimab**.  [merck.com](https://www.merck.com/news/mercks-keytruda-pembrolizumab-significantly-improved-overall-survival-os-versus-placebo-in-certain-patients-with-advanced-hepatocellular-carcinoma-hcc-previously-treated-with-sora/) [keytrudahcp.com](https://www.keytrudahcp.com/biomarker-testing/msi-mmr/)While MSI-H/dMMR is rare in HCC (found in less than 3% of patients), those who test positive are eligible for these agents if their disease progresses after prior lines of therapy.
 [nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32088930/) [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10463359/)*   **Tumor Mutational Burden (TMB):** Next-generation sequencing (NGS) can identify patients with high tumor mutational burden (TMB-H, defined as ≥ 10 mutations/megabase). Pembrolizumab is FDA-approved for any advanced solid tumor with TMB-H following progression on prior treatments. 

 [frontiersin.org](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2021.803133/full)**Therapies Not Requiring Biomarker Testing**
It is important to note that the most common first-line systemic therapies for advanced HCC do not currently require specific biomarker testing prior to initiation. Th [mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6694/17/19/3105)ese include:
*   **Immunotherapy combinations:** Atezolizumab plus bevacizumab, or durvalumab plus tremelimumab.
*  [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12428263/)  **Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKIs):** Lenvatinib, sorafenib, cabozantinib, and regorafenib.

* [onclive.com](https://www.onclive.com/view/tislelizumab-pushes-hcc-field-forward-with-2024-nccn-guideline-category-1-recommendation) [onclive.com](https://www.onclive.com/view/ramucirumab-extends-survival-in-advanced-hcc)(Note: Other circulating biomarkers such as AFP-L3 and Des-gamma-carboxy prothrombin (DCP) are commonly used for HCC screening, diagnosis, and monitoring treatment response rather than serving as predictive biomarkers for specific drug selection)*.
