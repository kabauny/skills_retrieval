# Tell me about AKT pathway drugs available for NSCLC

Currently, there are no AKT pathway inhibitors that are FDA-approved specifically for the treatment of Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) [search-sourced]. However, the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway is frequently dysregulated in NSCLC [search-sourced]. It is known to be a major mechanism of acquired resistance to standard treatments, including chemotherapy regimens and epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) like osimertinib and erlotinib [search-sourced]. As a result, several AKT inhibitors are being actively investigated in clinical trials for NSCLC patients [search-sourced].

Key AKT inhibitors in clinical development for NSCLC include:

*   **Capivasertib (Truqap)**: While capivasertib is currently FDA-approved for specific types of hormone receptor-positive metastatic breast cancer, it is still in the trial phase for NSCLC [search-sourced]. Evidence shows that PIK3CA/AKT/PTEN alterations can drive resistance to the targeted therapy osimertinib, and adding capivasertib may resensitize tumors to treatment [search-sourced]. Capivasertib is currently being evaluated in combination with osimertinib in clinical trials (e.g., NCT07486648) for patients with EGFR-mutated advanced NSCLC who have PIK3CA, AKT1, or PTEN alterations and have progressed on prior osimertinib therapy [search-sourced].
*   **Ipatasertib**: This pan-AKT inhibitor is undergoing Phase II clinical trials for NSCLC [search-sourced]. One prominent trial (NCT04467801) is investigating ipatasertib in combination with the chemotherapy agent docetaxel for patients with advanced or metastatic NSCLC whose disease has progressed after failing first-line immunotherapy [search-sourced]. The rationale is that AKT inhibitors may help modulate pathway activity to overcome resistance and enhance the therapeutic effects of standard chemotherapy and immunotherapies [search-sourced].
*   **MK-2206**: An older highly selective allosteric AKT inhibitor that has been investigated in earlier-phase clinical trials [search-sourced]. It was tested in a Phase II trial alongside erlotinib for patients with advanced NSCLC who had previously progressed on erlotinib, aiming to restore sensitivity to the EGFR inhibitor [search-sourced].

In summary, while AKT-targeted drugs are not yet available as a standard-of-care option for NSCLC, they represent an important pipeline of research primarily focused on combination therapies to overcome drug resistance [search-sourced].
