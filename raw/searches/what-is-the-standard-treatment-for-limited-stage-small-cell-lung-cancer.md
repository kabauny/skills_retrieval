# What is the standard treatment for limited-stage small cell lung cancer?

The standard treatment for limited-stage small cell lung cancer (LS-SCLC) is generally administered with curative intent and typically involves a combination of therapies based on the patient's overall health [go2.org](https://go2.org/blog/small-cell-lung-cancer-treatments-by-stage) [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10203822/). 

**1. Concurrent Chemoradiotherapy**
For patients who are in good health, the primary standard of care is concurrent chemoradiotherapy, which involves administering chemotherapy and thoracic (chest) radiation therapy at the same time [cancer.org](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/lung-cancer/treating-small-cell/by-stage.html) [go2.org](https://go2.org/blog/small-cell-lung-cancer-treatments-by-stage). 
*   **Chemotherapy:** The standard regimen is a "platinum-doublet," which combines etoposide with a platinum-based chemotherapy drug (either cisplatin or carboplatin) [cancer.org](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/lung-cancer/treating-small-cell/by-stage.html) [go2.org](https://go2.org/blog/small-cell-lung-cancer-treatments-by-stage). 
*   **Radiation Therapy:** High-energy radiation is targeted at the chest tumor [go2.org](https://go2.org/blog/small-cell-lung-cancer-treatments-by-stage). Giving radiation concurrently with chemotherapy provides better overall survival and a higher chance of a cure than administering the treatments one after the other, though it can result in more severe side effects [cancer.org](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/lung-cancer/treating-small-cell/by-stage.html) [go2.org](https://go2.org/blog/small-cell-lung-cancer-treatments-by-stage).

**2. Consolidation Immunotherapy**
The treatment landscape for SCLC has recently advanced to include immunotherapy [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12437200/) [nccn.org](https://education.nccn.org/node/97739). Following the completion of concurrent chemoradiotherapy, the immune checkpoint inhibitor durvalumab is now approved and recommended as a consolidation therapy to help reduce the risk of the cancer relapsing [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12437200/) [nccn.org](https://education.nccn.org/node/97739). 

**3. Prophylactic Cranial Irradiation (PCI)**
Because small cell lung cancer has a high likelihood of spreading to the brain, patients who have a favorable response to their initial chemoradiation may be offered prophylactic cranial irradiation (PCI) [youtube.com](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZmGjkx-bETc) [nccn.org](https://education.nccn.org/node/97739). This preventive radiation to the head is used to destroy microscopic cancer cells and lessen the risk of brain metastases [youtube.com](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CZxYjc9Svfc).

**4. Surgery**
Surgery is rarely used in the management of small cell lung cancer because the disease usually spreads early [youtube.com](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CZxYjc9Svfc) [cancer.org](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/lung-cancer/treating-small-cell/by-stage.html). It is only considered in a very small minority of patients (roughly 2% to 5%) who are diagnosed at a very early stage with a single, small tumor and no evidence of spread to the lymph nodes [cancer.org](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/types/lung-cancer/treating-small-cell/by-stage.html) [tri-kobe.org](https://www2.tri-kobe.org/nccn/guideline/lung/english/small.pdf). If a patient undergoes surgical resection, it is typically followed by adjuvant chemotherapy or chemoradiation [cancer.gov](https://www.cancer.gov/types/lung/hp/small-cell-lung-treatment-pdq).
