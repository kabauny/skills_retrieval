# What is the second-line treatment after progression for metastatic urothelial carcinoma?

The treatment landscape for locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma (mUC) has recently shifted, establishing the combination of enfortumab vedotin (EV) plus pembrolizumab as the standard first-line treatment [search-sourced] [ascopubs.org](https://dailynews.ascopubs.org/do/optimizing-treatment-strategies-advanced-urothelial-cancer-comes-after-frontline). Because this frontline regimen is relatively new, there is currently no single, definitively established standard-of-care for patients who experience disease progression after receiving it [search-sourced] [urotoday.com](https://www.urotoday.com/conference-highlights/asco-2025/asco-2025-bladder-cancer/161056-asco-2025-treatment-patterns-and-clinical-outcomes-with-platinum-based-chemotherapy-after-enfortumab-vedotin-and-pembrolizumab-in-patients-with-metastatic-urothelial-carcinoma.html). 

Based on the latest clinical guidelines and emerging data, second-line treatment options for mUC generally include the following:

*   **Platinum-Based Chemotherapy (PBCT)**: For patients who progress on first-line EV plus pembrolizumab, platinum-based chemotherapy (using either cisplatin or carboplatin) is widely recommended as the preferred subsequent therapy [search-sourced] [urotoday.com](https://www.urotoday.com/conference-highlights/asco-2025/asco-2025-bladder-cancer/161056-asco-2025-treatment-patterns-and-clinical-outcomes-with-platinum-based-chemotherapy-after-enfortumab-vedotin-and-pembrolizumab-in-patients-with-metastatic-urothelial-carcinoma.html). In this specific post-immunotherapy setting, it is typically administered without avelumab maintenance [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12559142/).
*   **Targeted Therapies**:
    *   **Erdafitinib**: This targeted kinase inhibitor is a recommended alternative for patients whose tumors harbor susceptible *FGFR2* or *FGFR3* genetic alterations [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12559142/).
    *   **HER2-Directed Therapy**: For patients with HER2-overexpressing tumors, therapies such as fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan-nxki (T-DXd) are considered an alternative option [search-sourced] [ascopubs.org](https://dailynews.ascopubs.org/do/optimizing-treatment-strategies-advanced-urothelial-cancer-comes-after-frontline).
*   **Other Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs)**: Sacituzumab govitecan is another available regimen utilized to navigate second-line or subsequent treatment after EV and pembrolizumab [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38797658/).

**Alternative Scenarios**:
For patients who initially received platinum-based chemotherapy as their first-line treatment—often due to global access barriers, reimbursement issues, or clinical contraindications with EV plus pembrolizumab—second-line options generally rely on immune checkpoint inhibitors (such as pembrolizumab or avelumab maintenance), enfortumab vedotin as a monotherapy, or targeted agents like erdafitinib for those with *FGFR* mutations [search-sourced]. Ad [ascopubs.org](https://dailynews.ascopubs.org/do/optimizing-treatment-strategies-advanced-urothelial-cancer-comes-after-frontline)ditionally, cytotoxic chemotherapies such as taxanes (paclitaxel or docetaxel) and vinflunine remain essential second-line or third-line strategies in settings where immunotherapy or targeted agents are not accessible or feasible [search-sourced].
