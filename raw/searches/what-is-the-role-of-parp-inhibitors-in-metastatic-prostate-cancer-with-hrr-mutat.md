# What is the role of PARP inhibitors in metastatic prostate cancer with HRR mutations?

**Role of PARP Inhibitors in Metastatic Prostate Cancer with HRR Mutations**

Poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors have become a cornerstone in the targeted treatment of advanced prostate cancer, specifically for patients harboring homologous recombination repair (HRR) mutations [auanews.net](https://auanews.net/issues/articles/2024/july-extra-2024/the-evolving-role-of-parp-inhibitors-in-advanced-prostate-cancer). Approximately 20% to 25% of patients with metastatic prostate cancer have somatic or germline mutations in the DNA damage repair (DDR) pathway, with *BRCA1*, *BRCA2*, and *ATM* being among the most common [auanews.net](https://auanews.net/issues/articles/2024/july-extra-2024/the-evolving-role-of-parp-inhibitors-in-advanced-prostate-cancer) [hematologyandoncology.net](https://www.hematologyandoncology.net/archives/december-2020/parp-inhibitors-in-prostate-cancer-practical-guidance-for-busy-clinicians/) [binaytara.org](https://binaytara.org/journal/article/92258-parp-inhibitors-in-prostate-cancer-understanding-the-current-landscape). 

**Mechanism of Action**
HRR mutations compromise a cancer cell's primary ability to repair double-strand DNA breaks [auanews.net](https://auanews.net/issues/articles/2024/july-extra-2024/the-evolving-role-of-parp-inhibitors-in-advanced-prostate-cancer) [astrazeneca.com](https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2020/lynparza-approved-in-the-us-for-hrr-gene-mutated-metastatic-castration-resistant-prostate-cancer.html). PARP inhibitors work by blocking a secondary, "rescue" DNA repair mechanism by trapping PARP bound to single-strand DNA breaks [astrazeneca.com](https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2020/lynparza-approved-in-the-us-for-hrr-gene-mutated-metastatic-castration-resistant-prostate-cancer.html). This causes replication forks to stall and collapse, leading to the generation of catastrophic double-strand breaks [astrazeneca.com](https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2020/lynparza-approved-in-the-us-for-hrr-gene-mutated-metastatic-castration-resistant-prostate-cancer.html). Because these cancer cells already lack functional HRR, this overwhelming DNA damage causes synthetic lethality and subsequent cancer cell death [astrazeneca.com](https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2020/lynparza-approved-in-the-us-for-hrr-gene-mutated-metastatic-castration-resistant-prostate-cancer.html).

**Clinical Efficacy and FDA Approvals**
PARP inhibitors have demonstrated significant efficacy in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC), fundamentally changing the treatment landscape [urologytimes.com](https://www.urologytimes.com/view/parp-inhibitors-and-prostate-cancer-what-urologists-need-to-know) [targetedonc.com](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/parp-inhibitors-gain-indications-in-advanced-prostate-cancer). Key milestones and FDA approvals include:
* **Olaparib:** Approved in 2020 for mCRPC patients with a broad array of HRR gene mutations whose disease progressed following treatment with androgen receptor pathway inhibitors (such as abiraterone or enzalutamide) [auanews.net](https://auanews.net/issues/articles/2024/july-extra-2024/the-evolving-role-of-parp-inhibitors-in-advanced-prostate-cancer) [astrazeneca.com](https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2020/lynparza-approved-in-the-us-for-hrr-gene-mutated-metastatic-castration-resistant-prostate-cancer.html). This was driven by the Phase III PROfound trial, which demonstrated significantly improved progression-free and overall survival [auanews.net](https://auanews.net/issues/articles/2024/july-extra-2024/the-evolving-role-of-parp-inhibitors-in-advanced-prostate-cancer) [astrazeneca.com](https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2020/lynparza-approved-in-the-us-for-hrr-gene-mutated-metastatic-castration-resistant-prostate-cancer.html).
* **Rucaparib:** Granted accelerated approval for mCRPC patients specifically with *BRCA1* or *BRCA2* mutations who have previously been treated with both androgen receptor-directed therapy and a taxane-based chemotherapy [auanews.net](https://auanews.net/issues/articles/2024/july-extra-2024/the-evolving-role-of-parp-inhibitors-in-advanced-prostate-cancer) [urologytimes.com](https://www.urologytimes.com/view/parp-inhibitors-and-prostate-cancer-what-urologists-need-to-know). This was based on the TRITON2 phase II study [urologytimes.com](https://www.urologytimes.com/view/parp-inhibitors-and-prostate-cancer-what-urologists-need-to-know).
* **Emerging Combinations:** Newer trials and clinical applications have expanded to combine PARP inhibitors (such as niraparib or talazoparib) with androgen receptor-targeted therapies (like abiraterone or enzalutamide) [auanews.net](https://auanews.net/issues/articles/2024/july-extra-2024/the-evolving-role-of-parp-inhibitors-in-advanced-prostate-cancer) [urotoday.com](https://www.urotoday.com/library-resources/advanced-prostate-cancer/150515-parp-inhibitor-therapy-for-prostate-cancer-patients-emerging-combinations.html) [binaytara.org](https://binaytara.org/journal/article/92258-parp-inhibitors-in-prostate-cancer-understanding-the-current-landscape). Preclinical evidence suggests that androgen suppression can induce a "BRCA-like" state in tumor cells, producing synergistic sensitivity to PARP inhibition [auanews.net](https://auanews.net/issues/articles/2024/july-extra-2024/the-evolving-role-of-parp-inhibitors-in-advanced-prostate-cancer).

**Clinical Guidelines**
Due to the profound clinical benefit of PARP inhibitors in this biomarker-defined population, the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines recommend that all patients with metastatic prostate cancer undergo somatic tumor testing (and/or germline testing) for HRR mutations [hematologyandoncology.net](https://www.hematologyandoncology.net/archives/december-2020/parp-inhibitors-in-prostate-cancer-practical-guidance-for-busy-clinicians/) [targetedonc.com](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/parp-inhibitors-gain-indications-in-advanced-prostate-cancer). Identifying these mutations early in the metastatic disease course is essential to uncover drivers that can be successfully targeted by PARP inhibitors [hematologyandoncology.net](https://www.hematologyandoncology.net/archives/december-2020/parp-inhibitors-in-prostate-cancer-practical-guidance-for-busy-clinicians/).

**Safety profile**
The adverse effects of PARP inhibitors are generally similar across the class and include bone marrow toxicity (leading to anemia and thrombocytopenia), fatigue, nausea, and anorexia [auanews.net](https://auanews.net/issues/articles/2024/july-extra-2024/the-evolving-role-of-parp-inhibitors-in-advanced-prostate-cancer) [urologytimes.com](https://www.urologytimes.com/view/parp-inhibitors-and-prostate-cancer-what-urologists-need-to-know). These side effects are typically manageable but may require dose interruptions in about half of patients, and dose reductions in roughly a quarter [auanews.net](https://auanews.net/issues/articles/2024/july-extra-2024/the-evolving-role-of-parp-inhibitors-in-advanced-prostate-cancer).
