# A 72-year-old patient with del(17p) CLL and good performance status has progressed after two years on a BTK inhibitor. Should she initiate a BCL-2 inhibitor, switch to a different BTK inhibitor, or consider clinical trial enrollment?

For a 72-year-old patient with high-risk del(17p) CLL who has **progressed** (rather than experienced intolerance) on a BTK inhibitor, the clinical management differs from a patient merely suffering from drug toxicities. The recommended approaches are as follows:

**1. Initiate a BCL-2 inhibitor (Recommended)**
This is the standard-of-care next step. BCL-2 inhibitors, such as venetoclax, are preferred, highly effective targeted therapies that maintain robust disease control in high-risk subgroups, including patients with a del(17p) deletion [[a-68-year-old-patient-with-high-risk-cll-del17p-currently-on-acalabrutinib-for-t]]. National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines strongly recommend transitioning to a BCL-2 inhibitor-based regimen for patients who exhibit disease progression after frontline treatment with a BTK inhibitor [onclive.com](https://www.onclive.com/view/lamanna-details-evolution-of-cll-treatment-following-resistance) [hematologyandoncology.net](https://www.hematologyandoncology.net/archives/june-2023/managing-cll-that-has-progressed-after-btk-inhibition/). 

**2. Switch to a different BTK inhibitor (Not Recommended)**
While switching to an alternative covalent BTK inhibitor (such as zanubrutinib) is an appropriate strategy to manage severe drug *intolerance* [[a-68-year-old-patient-with-high-risk-cll-del17p-currently-on-acalabrutinib-for-t]], it is not recommended for managing disease *progression*. Progression on a standard covalent BTK inhibitor is typically driven by acquired resistance mutations (such as in the BTK active site or PLCG2), which confer class-wide cross-resistance to other covalent BTK inhibitors [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8074228/) [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8074228/). 
*(Note: A third-generation, non-covalent BTK inhibitor called pirtobrutinib bypasses these specific mutations due to its different binding mechanism and is an NCCN-recommended exception for patients who have relapsed on prior covalent BTK inhibitors [nccn.org](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFu95r9y4uojR9jnN89xAdrOy_6Euyue78EP8hMzL9_OTL_efsylTzp9MsGLcRQXU-aZjROF5A32OESq1yLe6tY1wBIx-ynI6PvuDC7g38uHDpJW6IP6BE=) [nccn.org](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQF_KPH7VC1YX0hZIUzBAz9uEKEWZp6M9kKYYHeQCJLw09SFnFK60bapTjHlP09M2LrKYOpB5kAex51d1k0Nbz7EjIB_3x6h2nkOXGcmY6HKISO5kgXYD6edFZp_57u7GPkysyzqxAUYE_yukQJGOd1zMJab1lQNt8dX).)*

**3. Consider clinical trial enrollment (Recommended)**
Enrolling in a clinical trial is always an excellent consideration, particularly for patients with high-risk genetic features like del(17p) [nccn.org](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQF_KPH7VC1YX0hZIUzBAz9uEKEWZp6M9kKYYHeQCJLw09SFnFK60bapTjHlP09M2LrKYOpB5kAex51d1k0Nbz7EjIB_3x6h2nkOXGcmY6HKISO5kgXYD6edFZp_57u7GPkysyzqxAUYE_yukQJGOd1zMJab1lQNt8dX) [oncpracticemanagement.com](https://www.oncpracticemanagement.com/issues/2023/may-2023-vol-13-no-5/new-nccn-guidelines-for-cll-sll-include-second-generation-btk-inhibitors). Trials can provide access to emerging therapeutic strategies, such as novel combination therapies (e.g., dual BTK and BCL-2 inhibition) or cellular therapies (like CAR-T), which are designed to overcome resistance mechanisms [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10891251/) [nccn.org](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFu95r9y4uojR9jnN89xAdrOy_6Euyue78EP8hMzL9_OTL_efsylTzp9MsGLcRQXU-aZjROF5A32OESq1yLe6tY1wBIx-ynI6PvuDC7g38uHDpJW6IP6BE=).

**Conclusion:** 
The best clinical decision is to **initiate a BCL-2 inhibitor** or pursue **clinical trial enrollment**. Switching to another standard covalent BTK inhibitor will likely be ineffective due to cross-resistance and should be avoided.
