# What is the biomarker testing to guide therapy for small cell lung cancer?

Unlike non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), standard predictive biomarker testing (such as comprehensive genomic profiling or next-generation sequencing) is currently not routinely recommended or required to guide therapy selection for small cell lung cancer (SCLC) [search-sourced]. 

The primary use of biomarkers in SCLC is to confirm the histopathologic diagnosis using immunohistochemistry (IHC) tests for neuroendocrine markers, such as synaptophysin, chromogranin, CD56, and INSM1, rather than to match patients to targeted treatments [search-sourced].

For specific therapeutic decisions, biomarker testing remains largely unnecessary in SCLC due to the following clinical standards:
*   **Immunotherapy:** While immune checkpoint inhibitors (such as atezolizumab or durvalumab) combined with platinum-based chemotherapy are the standard first-line treatment for extensive-stage SCLC, they are given to patients regardless of PD-L1 status [search-sourced]. Therefore, routine PD-L1 testing is not used to select candidates for immunotherapy in SCLC [search-sourced].
*   **Targeted Therapy:** Tarlatamab (Imdelltra), a bispecific T-cell engager, was recently approved for extensive-stage SCLC that has progressed on or after platinum chemotherapy [search-sourced]. Although tarlatamab specifically targets the DLL3 protein on cancer cells, companion diagnostic testing for DLL3 expression is not required prior to starting the drug [search-sourced]. This is because DLL3 is naturally overexpressed in the vast majority (up to 85–96%) of SCLC tumors [search-sourced].
