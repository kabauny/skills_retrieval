# What is the standard first-line management of follicular lymphoma?

The standard first-line management of follicular lymphoma (FL) is highly individualized and depends on the disease stage, the patient's tumor burden, and the presence of symptoms [search-sourced]. According to major oncology guidelines (such as ESMO and NCCN), the standard approaches are generally stratified as follows:

### Early-Stage FL (Stage I and contiguous Stage II)
*   **Involved-Site Radiation Therapy (ISRT):** For limited-stage disease, localized radiation therapy (sometimes given alongside rituximab) is typically the standard, potentially curative treatment [search-sourced].
*   **Systemic Therapy:** This may be considered if radiation is not feasible, if the tumor burden is high, or if there are other adverse clinical prognostic features [search-sourced].

### Advanced-Stage FL (Stage III and IV) with Low Tumor Burden / Asymptomatic
*   **Watch and Wait (Active Observation):** For patients without symptoms (such as B symptoms or threatened end-organ function) and a low tumor burden, careful clinical observation without immediate treatment remains the standard of care [search-sourced].
*   **Single-Agent Immunotherapy:** Rituximab monotherapy (an anti-CD20 antibody) is sometimes utilized as an alternative to active observation [search-sourced].

### Advanced-Stage FL (Stage III and IV) with High Tumor Burden / Symptomatic
When patients have advanced disease accompanied by symptoms, bulky disease, or a high tumor burden (such as meeting GELF criteria), systemic treatment is required [search-sourced].
*   **Immunochemotherapy:** The standard induction approach pairs an anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody (rituximab or obinutuzumab) with a chemotherapy regimen [search-sourced]. Preferred chemotherapy options include:
    *   **Bendamustine** [search-sourced]
    *   **CHOP** (cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone) [search-sourced]
    *   **CVP** (cyclophosphamide, vincristine, and prednisone) [search-sourced]
*   **Targeted / Chemotherapy-Free Options:** The combination of rituximab with the immunomodulator lenalidomide is another recognized first-line alternative [search-sourced].
*   **Maintenance Therapy:** If the patient achieves a complete or partial remission following induction immunochemotherapy, maintenance therapy with an anti-CD20 antibody (rituximab or obinutuzumab) administered every two months for up to two years is highly recommended to delay disease progression [search-sourced]. 

### Older or Frail Patients
*   For elderly or frail patients unable to tolerate standard intensive immunochemotherapy, options include single-agent rituximab, rituximab combined with milder chemotherapy agents like chlorambucil or cyclophosphamide, or palliative low-dose radiation therapy [search-sourced].
