# What is the managing acquired resistance for metastatic urothelial carcinoma?

The combination of enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab is the FDA-approved first-line standard of care for patients with locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma (mUC) [[how-do-you-treat-metastatic-urothelial-carcinoma-with-enfortumab-vedotin-plus-pe]]. This regimen is generally continued until the patient experiences disease progression or unacceptable toxicity [[how-do-you-treat-metastatic-urothelial-carcinoma-with-enfortumab-vedotin-plus-pe]]. 

Despite the significant survival benefits of this frontline regimen, some patients exhibit primary refractory disease, and many will eventually develop secondary (acquired) resistance [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11986644/). Known mechanisms of resistance to enfortumab vedotin (a Nectin-4-directed antibody-drug conjugate) include the downregulation of Nectin-4 expression, the upregulation of efflux pumps that expel the drug's payload, and direct tubulin resistance to the toxin [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11986644/). Acquired resistance to the immune checkpoint inhibitor pembrolizumab typically involves various pathways of immune system evasion [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11986644/).

Currently, there is no single, established standard of care for managing mUC following acquired resistance to frontline enfortumab vedotin and pembrolizumab [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38797658/). Management and treatment sequencing generally involve the following options:

* **Platinum-Based Chemotherapy:** Because enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab is now the preferred frontline regimen, platinum-based chemotherapy combinations (such as cisplatin or carboplatin with gemcitabine), which were the historical standard of care, are frequently utilized as the next line of treatment for eligible patients [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38797658/) [vjoncology.com](https://www.vjoncology.com/video/rryg0lsde0e-evolving-standards-in-first-line-metastatic-urothelial-cancer/).
* **Targeted Therapies:** For patients whose tumors harbor susceptible *FGFR3* mutations or alterations, the pan-FGFR inhibitor erdafitinib is an approved therapeutic option [search-sourced] [frontiersin.org](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2025.1654771/full) [nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38797658/).
* **Alternative Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs):** To overcome resistance to a Nectin-4 ADC, alternative ADCs targeting different cellular antigens can be utilized. Sacituzumab govitecan, which targets TROP-2, is an established option [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38797658/). Additionally, HER2-targeted agents (such as trastuzumab deruxtecan) are emerging alternatives for patients with the appropriate biomarkers [search-sourced] [vjoncology.com](https://www.vjoncology.com/video/xjt9svpuol4-treatment-sequencing-after-ev-plus-pembrolizumab-in-metastatic-bladder-cancer/).
* **Clinical Trials:** Because the post-progression treatment landscape is highly nuanced and rapidly evolving, enrolling patients in clinical trials is strongly encouraged to help inform optimal sequencing and discover new therapies [search-sourced] [nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38797658/).
