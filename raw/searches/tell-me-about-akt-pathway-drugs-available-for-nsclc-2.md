# Tell me about AKT pathway drugs available for NSCLC

Currently, there are no FDA-approved drugs that specifically target the AKT pathway for the treatment of Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7049515/) [aacrjournals.org](https://aacrjournals.org/cancerdiscovery/article/9/1/OF7/10570/Capivasertib-Active-against-AKT1-Mutated). However, the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway is frequently upregulated or dysregulated in NSCLC, and its activation is closely linked to tumor progression, metastasis, and resistance to other treatments like chemotherapy and EGFR-tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7049515/) [mednexus.org](https://mednexus.org/doi/full/10.1097/CM9.0000000000002195). Because of this, targeting the AKT pathway in NSCLC is a highly active area of ongoing oncology research [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8271933/).

Here is the current landscape of AKT pathway drugs and their relevance to lung cancer:

*   **Capivasertib (Truqap):** Capivasertib is a first-in-class AKT inhibitor [acs.org](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jmedchem.4c00075) [biophact.fr](https://www.biophact.fr/en/in-2023-the-fda-granted-approval-to-13-new-oncology-drugs-constituting-24-of-the-total-approvals-for-the-year/). It recently received landmark FDA approval for the treatment of HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer with specific genetic alterations (PIK3CA, AKT1, or PTEN), but it is **not** approved for NSCLC [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11588547/) [biophact.fr](https://www.biophact.fr/en/in-2023-the-fda-granted-approval-to-13-new-oncology-drugs-constituting-24-of-the-total-approvals-for-the-year/). Nevertheless, capivasertib is currently being investigated in clinical trials for various solid tumors, including lung cancer, and has shown promise in preclinical models for reversing therapy resistance and augmenting the effects of chemotherapy in lung cancer cells [aacrjournals.org](https://aacrjournals.org/cancerdiscovery/article/9/1/OF7/10570/Capivasertib-Active-against-AKT1-Mutated) [nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39501557/) [cancer.gov](https://www.cancer.gov/research/participate/clinical-trials/intervention/capivasertib?pn=1).
*   **Ipatasertib:** This is another investigational, orally administered small-molecule inhibitor that targets all three isoforms of AKT [oncnursingnews.com](https://www.oncnursingnews.com/view/ipatasertib-plus-pembrolizumab-boosts-response-in-first-line-r-m-hnscc). It is currently being evaluated in clinical trials for various cancers, often in combination with immunotherapies (such as pembrolizumab) or chemotherapies, to see if blocking the AKT pathway can overcome tumor immunosuppression and treatment resistance [oncnursingnews.com](https://www.oncnursingnews.com/view/ipatasertib-plus-pembrolizumab-boosts-response-in-first-line-r-m-hnscc).
*   **Combination Therapies in Clinical Trials:** Because AKT pathway activation is a known mechanism for acquired drug resistance in lung cancer (such as resistance to standard cisplatin chemotherapy), many active clinical trials and preclinical studies are focused on combining experimental AKT inhibitors with standard NSCLC therapies to restore cell sensitivity and improve patient outcomes [mednexus.org](https://mednexus.org/doi/full/10.1097/CM9.0000000000002195) [mdpi.com](https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/26/17/8378).
