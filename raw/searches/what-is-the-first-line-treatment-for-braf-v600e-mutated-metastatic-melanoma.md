# What is the first-line treatment for BRAF V600E-mutated metastatic melanoma?

According to current oncology guidelines (such as those from ASCO and NCCN), there are two primary classes of systemic treatments for patients with BRAF V600E-mutated unresectable or metastatic melanoma [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.23.01136) [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/EDBK_174934):

**1. Preferred First-Line Approach: Immunotherapy (Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors)**
Recent landmark clinical trials, specifically the Phase III DREAMseq trial and the SECOMBIT trial, have shifted the treatment paradigm to establish immunotherapy as the preferred initial treatment for most patients with BRAF V600E-mutated metastatic melanoma [vjoncology.com](https://www.vjoncology.com/video/ypmzbcqrei4-final-dreamseq-results-favor-first-line-immunotherapy-for-melanoma/) [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10113073/). The DREAMseq trial demonstrated a significant overall survival benefit (72% vs. 52% at two years) when patients started with combination immunotherapy and switched to targeted therapy upon progression, compared to the reverse sequence [cancer.gov](https://www.cancer.gov/news-events/cancer-currents-blog/2021/advanced-melanoma-braf-immunotherapy-first) [melanoma.org](https://melanoma.org/news-press/2025-asco-annual-meeting-recap/). 

Approved first-line immunotherapy options include:
*   Nivolumab plus ipilimumab (anti-PD-1 + anti-CTLA-4 combination) [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10113073/) [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.23.01136)
*   Nivolumab plus relatlimab (anti-PD-1 + anti-LAG-3 combination) [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.23.01136)
*   Anti-PD-1 monotherapy with either nivolumab or pembrolizumab [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.23.01136)

**2. Alternative First-Line Approach: Targeted Therapy (BRAF and MEK Inhibitors)**
While combination immunotherapy is generally preferred as the first step in sequencing, targeted therapy remains a category-1 guideline-approved first-line option [lifewisewa.com](https://www.lifewisewa.com/medicalpolicies-group/5.01.589.pdf) [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.23.01136). Because BRAF and MEK inhibitors typically elicit a much faster tumor response than immune checkpoint inhibitors, targeted therapy is frequently favored as the initial treatment for patients with rapidly progressing, highly symptomatic disease, or those who have strict medical contraindications to immunotherapy [nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10177463/). 

Approved targeted combinations include:
*   Dabrafenib plus trametinib [lifewisewa.com](https://www.lifewisewa.com/medicalpolicies-group/5.01.589.pdf) [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.23.01136)
*   Encorafenib plus binimetinib [lifewisewa.com](https://www.lifewisewa.com/medicalpolicies-group/5.01.589.pdf) [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.23.01136)
*   Vemurafenib plus cobimetinib [lifewisewa.com](https://www.lifewisewa.com/medicalpolicies-group/5.01.589.pdf) [ascopubs.org](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.23.01136)
