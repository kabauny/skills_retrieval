# How is non-clear-cell renal cell carcinoma treated?

Non-clear-cell renal cell carcinoma (nccRCC) represents a highly heterogeneous group of kidney cancers, which includes subtypes such as papillary, chromophobe, collecting duct, renal medullary, and unclassified carcinomas [search-sourced]. Because most clinical trials predominantly focus on the more common clear-cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), treatment guidelines for nccRCC have historically been extrapolated from ccRCC recommendations [search-sourced]. 

Management of nccRCC depends heavily on disease stage and the specific histological subtype:

**Surgical Management**
*   **Localized disease:** The foundation of treatment is surgical resection, which can involve a radical nephrectomy or partial kidney resection [search-sourced]. 
*   **Advanced/Metastatic disease:** Upfront cytoreductive nephrectomy (removal of the primary tumor) continues to play a role and is associated with survival benefits for selected patients who are subsequently treated with modern systemic therapies [search-sourced].

**Systemic Therapies**
For advanced or metastatic nccRCC, the treatment landscape consists primarily of targeted therapies and immunotherapies [search-sourced]:
*   **Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (TKIs):** Targeted agents like cabozantinib and sunitinib are standard treatments [search-sourced]. Notably, the PAPMET clinical trial demonstrated that cabozantinib provided superior progression-free survival and overall response rates compared to sunitinib in patients with metastatic papillary RCC [search-sourced].
*   **Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors (ICIs):** Immunotherapies such as pembrolizumab have shown efficacy; pembrolizumab is used as a first-line monotherapy for advanced nccRCC (demonstrated in the KEYNOTE-427 trial) and as an adjuvant therapy after surgery to reduce recurrence risks [search-sourced].
*   **HIF-2α Inhibitors:** Belzutifan provides a precision medicine approach by targeting the HIF-2α pathway and is FDA-approved for the treatment of VHL-associated tumors and advanced renal cell carcinoma [[belzutifan]].
*   **MET Inhibitors:** Because certain papillary RCCs are driven by MET mutations, specific MET kinase inhibitors like savolitinib are actively used and studied for these subtypes [search-sourced].
*   **Combination Regimens:** The newest frontier in nccRCC treatment involves combining TKIs with immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., combinations like nivolumab with zanzalintinib or tivozanib) to improve clinical response rates, which is currently the subject of several major clinical trials [search-sourced].
