# What is the regimen for adjuvant and maintantence TMZ?

Based on the standard Stupp protocol for treating newly diagnosed glioblastoma, the terms "adjuvant" and "maintenance" are generally used interchangeably to describe the phase of temozolomide (TMZ) treatment that follows initial concurrent chemoradiotherapy [search-sourced]. 

The standard dosing regimen for the adjuvant/maintenance phase is as follows:

*   **Timing:** The maintenance phase typically begins 4 weeks after the completion of the concurrent phase (during which patients take 75 mg/m² daily alongside radiation) [search-sourced].
*   **Cycle 1:** Temozolomide is dosed at **150 mg/m² once daily for 5 consecutive days** followed by 23 days of rest, making up a 28-day cycle [search-sourced]. 
*   **Cycles 2 through 6:** If the first cycle is well tolerated—specifically if the Absolute Neutrophil Count (ANC) is ≥ 1.5 × 10⁹/L, platelets are ≥ 100 × 10⁹/L, and non-hematologic toxicities are Grade ≤ 2 (excluding alopecia, nausea, and vomiting)—the dose is escalated to **200 mg/m² once daily for 5 consecutive days** of a 28-day cycle [search-sourced].
*   **Duration:** The standard recommended duration is 6 cycles, although some clinical practices may extend dosing up to 12 cycles depending on the patient's response and physician discretion [search-sourced].

**Important Administration & Monitoring Guidelines:**
*   **Administration:** It is highly recommended to take TMZ on an empty stomach (e.g., fasting overnight or at least 1 hour before a meal) to optimize absorption and reduce the risk of nausea [search-sourced].
*   **Premedication:** Patients should typically be premedicated with an antiemetic (such as ondansetron) prior to each dose [search-sourced].
*   **Monitoring:** Complete blood counts (CBC) and liver function tests (LFTs) must be checked prior to initiating each cycle. Dose reductions, delays, or permanent discontinuation may be necessary if severe neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, or liver toxicities occur [search-sourced]. 
*   **Prophylaxis:** Prophylactic medication for *Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia (PJP) is mandatory during the concurrent phase and may need to be continued into the maintenance phase until lymphocytopenia resolves [search-sourced].
